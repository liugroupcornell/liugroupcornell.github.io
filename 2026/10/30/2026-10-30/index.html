Thermodynamic demonstration of tetracriticality and multicomponent superconductivity in UTe2

Original Paper | Superconducting properties and materials | 2026-10-29 20:00 EDT

Sahas Kamat, Jared Dans, Shanta Saha, Artem D. Kokovin, Johnpierre Paglione, Jörg Schmalian, B. J. Ramshaw

The candidate topological superconductor UTe2 exhibits a complex phase diagram with multiple superconducting states, and the nature of their coexistence is not yet resolved. In particular, the apparent observation of two intersecting second-order phase boundaries at a triple point in the pressure-temperature phase diagram is thermodynamically forbidden. This suggests either a hidden phase transition or a fundamental misunderstanding of the superconductivity in this material. Here we observe an additional phase boundary that is characterized by an upward jump in the sound velocity. This is direct thermodynamic evidence for a phase transition in which superconducting order is lost on cooling. Our results establish a tetracritical point beyond which the ambient and pressure-induced superconducting order parameters form a multicomponent state. We use the measured phase diagram to demonstrate that strong competition between the two superconducting order parameters drives the loss of order when cooling, and leads to phase locking that suppresses superconducting fluctuations. These findings provide the definitive magnetic field-pressure-temperature phase diagram of UTe2 and establish a thermodynamic foundation for multicomponent superconductivity.

Multiparticle quantum walks in a dipole-conserving Bose-Hubbard model

Original Paper | Ultracold gases | 2026-10-29 20:00 EDT

Sooshin Kim, Byungmin Kang, Perrin Segura, Yanfei Li, Ethan Lake, Brice Bakkali-Hassani, Markus Greiner

A strong potential gradient can localize individual particles in a crystal or optical lattice by making ordinary hopping energetically costly. Interactions can nevertheless enable correlated hopping processes that preserve the system’s centre of mass or, equivalently, its dipole moment. This constraint gives rise to fractonic behaviour, in which isolated excitations are immobile but their bound states can move. Here we realize individual mobile bound states of fractons in a dipole-conserving Bose-Hubbard model and observe their coherent quantum dynamics. Starting from a one-dimensional chain of ultracold bosonic atoms in an optical lattice, we use site-resolved optical potentials to deterministically generate localized dipole and antidipole excitations, which represent mobile bound states of a hole and a doublon. We track their individual quantum walks and characterize their strongly interacting scattering dynamics. Our study provides a bottom-up investigation of a Hamiltonian with kinetic constraints and offers an approach to exploring low-energy phases of fractonic matter.

Automated inverse design of metainterfaces with tailored friction laws

Original Paper | Surfaces, interfaces and thin films | 2026-10-29 20:00 EDT

Li Fu, Djibril Gabriel Kashala, Davy Dalmas, Julien Scheibert

Providing dry solid contacts with on-demand macroscale frictional behaviour remains a formidable challenge in tribology, haptics and robotics. It has been shown that metainterfaces created from surfaces with engineered asperity-based topographies can achieve such friction control. However, only a few types of friction behaviour were demonstrated because suitable topographies were identified on the basis of human intuition. Here we introduce a numerical-optimization-based inverse design framework to automatically discover new metainterfaces that satisfy specified friction laws–relationships between friction and the normal force. To illustrate the framework’s versatility, we first expand the range of achievable friction coefficients for a given pair of materials. We next unlock friction laws showing power-law variation of frictional force with normal force, with exponents ranging between 2/3 and 1.35. We then achieve bilinear variation when the range of normal force is divided into segments, including cases for a smaller slope in the second segment than in the first. We validate relevant cases experimentally. Our automated framework enables systematic exploration of large parameter spaces, not limited to topography but potentially incorporating the individual asperities’ bulk material or surface physicochemistry. It offers design solutions for different friction laws and provides insights into the relationship between local interfacial properties and macroscopic friction.

Motors use biased diffusion to shepherd diffusible proteins on microtubules

Original Paper | Motility | 2026-10-29 20:00 EDT

Leila Farhadi, Shane A. Fiorenza, Sithara Wijeratne, Konstantinos Nakos, Yang Yue, Morgan L. Pimm, Jakia Jannat Keya, Kristen J. Verhey, Radhika Subramanian, Meredith D. Betterton

Far-from-equilibrium mixtures of active and passive molecules self-organize, but the principles remain unclear. Microtubules are cytoskeletal polymers composed of 13 protofilaments bound by mixtures of actively moving motors and passively diffusing microtubule-associated proteins (MAPs). They can serve as a model system for self-organization on a multi-lane lattice. Here we show that motors can shepherd diffusive MAPs along the lattice by rectifying Brownian motion into net directional drift without motor-MAP binding. Our model reveals that biased movement increases when fewer protofilaments are accessible or when lateral diffusion between protofilaments is limited, and identifies local dimensionality and diffusion as key parameters governing spatial patterning. In particular, shepherding depends on the total number of motors bound to the microtubules, and not on the individual motor processivity. We experimentally recapitulate micrometre-scale shepherding with a kinesin-1 motor (K401) and a diffusive MAP (PRC1), which show no detectable binding. These findings suggest shepherding is an emergent mechanism by which ensembles of motors generate micrometre-scale spatial patterning and transport of MAPs without direct binding interactions.

In this work we explore the complex magnetic behaviour of SrMnO3/La0.7Sr0.3MnO3/SrMnO3 trilayers combining magneto-optic Kerr effect (MOKE) and magneto-resistance measurements. The interplay of structural and orbital reconstruction and the competition of ferromagnetism/antiferromagnetism at the interfaces is responsible of a variety of complex magnetic properties, highlighting the use of heterostructures as an effective strategy to tune and control magnetic behaviour. A $B_{1g}$ phonon unlocks $d$-wave superconductivity in the repulsive Hubbard model

New Submission | Superconductivity (cond-mat.supr-con) | 2026-10-30 20:00 EDT

Wen O. Wang, Tianshu Li, Xiaoyue Ma, Rong Zhang, Lei Zhang, Shruti Agarwal, Benjamin Cohen-Stead, Emily Z. Zhang, Brian Moritz, Steven Johnston, Zhi-Xun Shen, Thomas P. Devereaux

Using numerically exact finite-temperature quantum Monte Carlo, we demonstrate that a $ B_{1g}$ phonon coupled to the doped $ t$ -$ t’$ repulsive Hubbard model produces genuine $ d$ -wave superconductivity at a substantial transition temperature, in excess of either interaction alone. The observed trends suggest that stronger Hubbard repulsion may cooperate with the phonon to further raise the pairing scale. The enhancement persists at stronger repulsion within the accessible window, limited by the fermion sign problem, and follows a common trend across phonon frequencies $ \Omega$ when organized by a retardation scale $ 2\pi T/\Omega$ . We also find a well-defined superconducting dome in doping, echoing a characteristic feature of correlated superconductors. Our results identify the $ B_{1g}$ phonon as a plausible microscopic ingredient for robust $ d$ -wave superconductivity in the Hubbard model and suggest a concrete, physically motivated route toward materials with higher superconducting transition temperatures.

Approaching Landauer’s limit in a single superconducting vortex memory bit

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-10-30 20:00 EDT

Nofar Fridman, Itai Keren, Avia Noah, Yishay Zur, Martin E. Huber, Nadav Katz, Roland Willa, Hadar Steinberg, Yonathan Anahory

This work investigates the energy required to manipulate a memory bit using a superconducting vortex confined in a nanoscale double-well pinning potential. In the experiments, various double-well systems subjected to sinusoidal forces is explored, and the effects of both driving force and frequency is analyzed. Results reveal characteristic stochastic resonance behavior for weak drives and adiabatic manipulation at strong, low-frequency drives. Although our experimental method requires a steady sinusoidal drive incompatible with Landauer’s erasure protocol, our numerical analysis demonstrates that the work required for such bit manipulation (erasure) is within an order of magnitude of Landauer’s limit ($ k_B T \ln 2$ ) at 4.2 K. These findings open possibilities for creating highly efficient memory elements that could be integrated with cryogenic quantum circuits or function as topological quantum computing qubits; thereby contributing to memory devices with lower energy consumption and mitigating the escalating global energy demand in the computing industry.

Meta-Altermagnetism and Enhanced Spin-Splitting

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-10-30 20:00 EDT

Bogeng Wen, Hae-Young Kee

Altermagnets are collinear magnets with zero net magnetization and spin-split electronic bands. Here, we explore whether altermagnetism can exhibit a discontinuous jump, a phenomenon we refer to as meta-altermagnetism. We show that meta-altermagnetism can emerge in 2D systems when magnetic-order-induced band reconstruction drives spin-split van Hove singularities (vHSs) across the Fermi level. Using mean-field theory for an extended $ t$ -$ J$ model on a two-sublattice square lattice including anisotropic hopping integral, we find that a first-order meta-altermagnetic transition produces an intermediate altermagnetic regime with reduced staggered magnetization but strongly enhanced magnetic octupole and time-reversal-odd spin-splitter conductivity. We further show that the first-order transition line terminates at a quantum critical endpoint, giving rise to a rich phase diagram as the chemical potential and anisotropic hopping strength are varied. Our results identify the spin split vHSs as a route to enhancing spin-splitter effect for spintronic applications.

MoireKP: An Automated Workflow for Symmetry-Constrained Moiré Continuum Models

New Submission | Materials Science (cond-mat.mtrl-sci) | 2026-10-30 20:00 EDT

Yan Zhang, Hanqi Pi, Wangqian Miao, Zhong Fang, Hongming Weng, Xi Dai, B. Andrei Bernevig, Quansheng Wu

First-principles Hamiltonians contain the electronic information needed to study moiré materials, but their size makes subsequent calculations costly. We present MoireKP, an automated workflow for constructing compact continuum models from localized-orbital ab initio Hamiltonians. The workflow constructs a valley-resolved truncated atomic-plane-wave (TAPW) Hamiltonian, selects a low-energy basis, and projects the crystal-symmetry operations into this basis. These representations determine the symmetry-allowed Hamiltonian terms, whose coefficients are obtained by projecting the low-energy effective Hamiltonian onto the corresponding operator basis. The model dimension, localized reference states, and harmonic cutoff can be selected automatically or specified by the user. This Hamiltonian-based construction retains wave-function information within the selected low-energy subspace. All models reproduce TAPW band energies within 3.0 meV in the target windows along high-symmetry $ k$ paths.

Field theory of competing phases in the interacting Kitaev chain

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-10-30 20:00 EDT

Pradip Kattel, Thierry Giamarchi

We study the interacting Kitaev chain with nearest-neighbor interactions and finite chemical potential using a combination of bosonization and density-matrix renormalization-group calculations. Focusing away from special exactly solvable lattice limits, we develop a continuum field-theoretic description of the generic finite-density interacting regime, where doping nonperturbatively modifies the competition between superconducting pairing and commensurate charge ordering. We show that the phase diagram consists of an intermediate incommensurate Luttinger liquid between the topological superconducting and commensurate charge-density-wave Mott phases, as well as a trivial band-insulating regime at large chemical potential. The commensurate–incommensurate and incommensurate–topological-superconducting phase boundaries are governed by Pokrovsky–Talapov and Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless criticality, respectively. We analyze the interacting topological superconducting regime and its boundary excitations. At the semiclassical level, and explicitly in the free-fermion boundary theory, the topological phase supports exponentially localized Majorana edge modes at interfaces between topological and trivial regions. We also construct a dual continuum fermionic representation in which this topological bulk sector is described instead by conventional symmetry breaking. This gives a continuum version of the SPT–SSB correspondence between different fermionic representations. Overall, our results provide an analytical description of one-dimensional interacting topological superconductors, from the competition between topology and commensurability and the resulting quantum criticality to the dual symmetry-breaking description of the topological phase.

We introduce two-dimensional toric code models with additional long-range loop interactions. Spin Space Symmetry Without Spin Space Groups

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-10-30 20:00 EDT

Jeffrey G. Rau

Spin space groups provide a complete symmetry classification of magnetic structures in the non-relativistic limit and have become an increasingly useful language for organizing magnetic phases and their physical properties. Each spin space group with a given parent space group is specified, up to spin-only symmetries, by a real representation of that space group acting in spin space. We argue that working directly with this representation not only avoids the construction and identification of the group, but also organizes and simplifies the resulting symmetry constraints on allowed responses. Determining the presence or absence of magnetic compensation, piezomagnetism, spin and charge transport, the Edelstein effect, and momentum-space spin textures becomes an ordinary problem in the representation theory of space groups. The resulting conditions reduce to overlaps between the three exterior powers of the defining representation and multipole representations of the point group. Several physically distinct responses then reduce to the same condition on the defining representation. For example, a $ p$ -wave spin texture and the time-even Edelstein effect are allowed under the same condition, as are piezomagnetism and a $ d$ -wave spin texture in compensated order. The wave-vector content of the exterior powers gives a further hierarchy of selection rules: depending on the relevant power, a uniform response requires one, two, or three ordering wave vectors to sum to a reciprocal lattice vector. These rules can exclude many responses before point-group symmetry is considered. We illustrate the approach with examples drawn from a wide variety of materials hosting collinear, coplanar and non-coplanar magnetic order. Finally, we discuss extensions of these results to magnetic space groups, connections to Landau theories of collinear order, and the characterization of odd-parity magnets.

Evidence for a charged vortex lattice in an exciton superfluid

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-10-30 20:00 EDT

Ryan L. Lee, Jungwoo Lee, Viliam Vaňo, Bo Zou, Cheng-Li Chiu, Kenji Watanabe, Takashi Taniguchi, Allan MacDonald, Ali Yazdani

Lattices of quantized vortices are a defining property of rotating superfluids and of type-II superconductors in magnetic fields. Realizing an analogous vortex lattice in an exciton superfluid-formed from bound electron-hole pairs-has remained challenging because excitons are electrically neutral. Building on previous theoretical studies of the low-energy excitations in an exciton superfluid, recent theory has predicted that in a strong magnetic field, a weakly charged exciton superfluid can stabilize a vortex-antivortex lattice arising from the mutual repulsion of fractionally charged vortex cores. Here we report evidence for this predicted vortex lattice in a bilayer exciton condensate by imaging its charged vortices with the scanning tunnelling microscope. The condensate forms between Landau levels of two graphene layers separated by a thin hexagonal boron nitride barrier in a perpendicular magnetic field. We find that the vortex lattice periodicity scales with the total excess charge and is insensitive to its distribution between the two layers, in quantitative agreement with theoretical predictions. Our observations provide evidence for phase coherence in an exciton superfluid system.

Parity-Resolved Many-Body Kinetics of a Topological Qubit under Nonequilibrium Phonon Pulses

New Submission | Superconductivity (cond-mat.supr-con) | 2026-10-30 20:00 EDT

Han Huang

Phonon bursts from ionizing radiation cause correlated errors in superconducting quantum processors. We study prescribed nonequilibrium phonon pulses in a Majorana qubit formed by two $ 2,\mu\mathrm{m}$ proximitized nanowires. Combining the lattice Lutchyn–Oreg model, microscopic electron–phonon coupling, and parity-resolved many-body dynamics, we track excitation, relaxation, and logical coherence. Without quasiparticle escape, recombination restores the initial Majorana occupations in about 130 ns, while phonons exchanged during excitation and recovery can retain logical-state information, leaving permanent coherence loss. For mirror-symmetric wires, the fractional coherence loss per wire is 0.75–1.0 times the excitation probability averaged over the two logical states, even at zero Majorana splitting; breaking mirror symmetry can suppress dephasing at small splitting. Factorized Boltzmann kinetics neglects quasiparticle correlations and overestimates residual parity flips. At equal deposited energy density, phonon spectra yield different excitation probabilities and coherence losses. Comparisons with Kitaev chains matched to the wire’s gap and Majorana decay length reveal that low electron density suppresses excitation by energetic phonons, whereas hard-wall boundaries in a high-density control increase excitation efficiency by a factor of $ 5\times10^4$ relative to the wire for pulses centered at 1 meV. Magnetic-field and chemical-potential tuning yield different phonon sensitivities at the same bulk gap through changes in spin and electron–hole structure. Including hybrid states’ semiconductor weights reduces near-threshold excitation rates fourfold when the tunnel coupling equals the parent superconducting gap. Our framework connects phonon spectra to leakage, poisoning, and coherence loss to guide Majorana-device design.

Geometry-controlled active transport in smectic textures

New Submission | Soft Condensed Matter (cond-mat.soft) | 2026-10-30 20:00 EDT

Yhony M. Arce, William G. C. Oropesa, Alef R. Siqueira, Hartmut Löwen, André P. Vieira, Danilo B. Liarte

Self-propelled particles moving through structured anisotropic media such as smectic liquid crystals exhibit transport properties that differ qualitatively from those in isotropic environments. Here we introduce a minimal model of an active Brownian particle whose propulsion axis is coupled to the layer-normal field of a smectic liquid crystal. Using numerical simulations complemented by approximate analytical calculations, we investigate the dynamics of an active particle in three representative two-dimensional smectic microstructures: a uniform planar texture, an isolated toroidal focal conic domain, and a periodic array of toroidal focal conic domains. For the planar and isolated focal-conic geometries, we characterize the crossover from short-time superdiffusion to long-time normal diffusion. These results demonstrate that the competition between the intrinsic persistence of active motion and the mean free time between encounters with smectic defects provides a simple physical mechanism for the emergence of nontrivial diffusive behavior in structured anisotropic media.

Anomalous Hall Conductivity as an Intrinsic Property of a Tight-Binding Graph

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-10-30 20:00 EDT

E. S. Andriyakhina, G. Lemut, P. W. Brouwer

In a ballistic system, the anomalous dc Hall response generically depends on microscopic details, including the geometric embedding of the orbitals and the precise implementation of the measurement. Here, we consider the question under what circumstances the anomalous Hall conductivity can be regarded as an intrinsic property of a system. We show that the full dc Hall response $ \sigma_{xy}^{\rm H}$ becomes embedding-independent in the presence of relaxation by impurity scattering, although the individual (intrinsic) band and (extrinsic) side-jump and anomalous-distribution contributions to the Hall conductivity separately remain embedding-dependent. More broadly, our results establish embedding dependence as a diagnostic of whether a response coefficient represents a stationary intrinsic property of a material or retains sensitivity to measurement protocol.

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-10-30 20:00 EDT

Archisman Panigrahi, Vladislav Poliakov, Leonid Levitov

Van Hove (vH) singularities can enhance superconductivity by generating a large pairing susceptibility. Yet, it remains challenging to experimentally establish whether pairing really occurs near these points. We show that the saddle-point dispersion near a vH point gives rise to a `light-cone’ vH-selective behavior of Friedel oscillations, which is drastically different from the familiar concentric circles. Each vH point maps onto its own real-space light-cone sector, allowing STM around a single impurity to measure whether—and how strongly—that saddle-point quasiparticle sector is gapped. A superconducting gap near vH points suppresses the oscillations in the directions corresponding to peaks in the gap function angular dependence, a behavior that provides a signature of vH-related pairing at STM-accessible length scales. This STM protocol, targeting vH superconductivity, is capable of determining whether superconducting pairing emerges near the vH points or elsewhere on the Fermi surface.

Renormalization for Robust Memory without Synchronization

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-10-30 20:00 EDT

Shashwat Chakraborty, Jong Yeon Lee

Local error correction can preserve classical information against noise, but protection under synchronized updates need not survive when sites evolve at independent random times. Establishing stability is particularly challenging when protection relies on the collective action of competing local rules. Here we develop a spacetime renormalization framework that reveals how independently acting local updates can collectively stabilize memory. We identify sufficient conditions under which the geometry of stable interfaces and suppression of defect propagation produce reliable erosion at large scales. Applying this framework, we prove stability of the asynchronous \textsf{R} squeezing code and introduce robust processes in two and three dimensions. In these systems, both encoded phases persist under sufficiently weak noise, even when the noise favors one phase. Our results provide a criterion for designing autonomous memories sustained by competing local stochastic dynamics.

Variational wavefunctions for ideal anyon gas: superfluid and skyrmion Hall crystal

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-10-30 20:00 EDT

Ya-Hui Zhang

The ground state of an ideal gas of anyons with statistical angle $ \theta\in(0,\pi)$ is a long-standing open problem, with renewed interest from the recent observations of fractional Chern insulators. In this work we construct variational wavefunctions that build in the exact anyon statistics and combine theory-guided composite-boson, composite-fermion and parton wavefunctions with neural-network correlations on the sphere. For spinless and SU(2) semions we confirm the paired anyon superfluid, well below the best crystal states. At $ \theta=\pi/3$ liquids and crystals are closer in energy for the spinless case. For $ \theta=\pi/3$ anyons with SU(3) flavor, we find two candidates. One is a skyrmion Hall crystal, in which the anyons form an integer quantum Hall state in the effective magnetic field from the spin texture. The other is a chiral color superconductor of six-anyon clusters, the color-flavor-locked pairing descendant of a holon metal with SU(3) color gauge symmetry and SU(3) flavor symmetry; its wavefunction is a hyperdeterminant rather than a product of parton determinants. Within our variational ansatz the color superconductor has better energy than both crystals and the color-flavor locked phases with U(1)$ ^2$ and Z$ _3$ gauge groups. For twisted MoTe$ _2$ this suggests that the reentrant integer quantum anomalous Hall phase is the skyrmion Hall crystal of the doped anyons, and that the observed superconductivity may be the color superconductor, which can undergo a transition to an $ f+if$ BCS superconductor with a jump of the chiral central charge by 3.
