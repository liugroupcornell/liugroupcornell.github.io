Revealing how distributed cortical circuits coordinate behavior in freely moving animals remains a central challenge in systems neuroscience, constrained by limited fields of view, low throughput, and the tethering required by current miniaturized microscopes1-4. Here, we present Airscope, a wireless mesoscope delivering 4 μm lateral resolution across a 6 mm field of view at 12.5 Hz, weighing 1.0 gram. An aspheric-lens design achieves a 2.84× shorter total track length than previous miniaturized microscopes1,2,5,6 while matching the neuron yield of benchtop one-photon mesoscopes; a tailored wireless link supports cable-free calcium imaging from >8,500 neurons per animal (25‑fold increase). Using Airscope, we demonstrate versatile applications of untethered cortical imaging across diverse behavioral paradigms. We identify cortical activity patterns across multiple dorsal regions that correlate with behavioral signatures of social dominance, and show dorsal-cortical population activity prospectively decodes competition outcomes across paired animals. We observe reward-location-associated cortical activity patterns during maze exploration, and characterize Granger-causal influence from spinal cord to cortex during fear conditioning, enabled by simultaneous brain-spinal recordings. By uniting large-scale, cellular-resolution recording with unconstrained behavior, Airscope makes naturalistic, multi-animal cortical recording experimentally practical, and provides a broadly accessible technology for systems neuroscience.

Metal-free molecular magnets differ from conventional magnetic devices that predominantly rely on metallic magnets as these metal-free materials may offer biocompatibility and bioresorbability. A liquid-crystal analogue of the skyrmion Hall effect

Original Paper | Fluid dynamics | 2026-10-18 20:00 EDT

Rodrigo C. V. Coelho, Hanqing Zhao, Guilherme N. C. Amaral, Ivan I. Smalyukh, Margarida M. Telo da Gama, Mykola Tasinkevych

Topology provides a unifying framework across disciplines, ranging from particle physics and cosmology to condensed-matter systems. In condensed matter, one of its most striking manifestations is the skyrmion Hall effect, where skyrmions–emergent spin configurations characterized by a conserved topological charge–acquire a transverse velocity under driving. Recent advances have revealed that chiral liquid crystals host a wide variety of topological solitons, including torons and skyrmion tubes. Here we show that such solitons exhibit a Hall-like response under shear flow, undergoing a transverse drift away from the shear direction. Our theoretical analysis predicts a weak net transverse mass flow whose magnitude scales as the cube of the shear rate. However, tracer-particle experiments and numerical simulations show that the solitons do not behave as passive tracers of this flow: their transverse motion is much stronger, and in simulations, it occurs opposite to the net transverse mass current. A liquid-crystal analogue of the Thiele equation provides a simple physical framework that highlights the central role of flow vorticity in the effect. These results establish a liquid-crystal analogue of the skyrmion Hall effect and open pathways for microfluidic technologies that exploit soft topological solitons as functional building blocks.

Evidence for spin-fluctuation-mediated superconductivity in electron-doped cuprates

Original Paper | Superconducting properties and materials | 2026-10-18 20:00 EDT

C. M. Duffy, S. J. Tu, Q. H. Chen, J. S. Zhang, A. Cuoghi, R. D. H. Hinlopen, T. Sarkar, R. L. Greene, K. Jin, N. E. Hussey

In conventional, phonon-mediated superconductors, the transition temperature and the linear-in-temperature normal-state scattering rate are linked through the electron-phonon coupling strength. In cuprate high-temperature superconductors, no equivalent relationship between critical temperature and electron-boson coupling has been identified. Here we highlight such a link with electrons scattering off commensurate antiferromagnetic spin fluctuations of variable strength. We use d.c. resistivity and high-field magnetoresistance to extract the scattering rate in electron-doped La2-xCexCuO4 as a function of doping. We reveal a highly anisotropic inelastic component to the scattering rate whose magnitude diminishes markedly as doping increases. The correlation we highlight between the strength of scattering and the critical temperature then identifies low-energy spin fluctuations as the primary pairing glue in electron-doped cuprates. The contrasting magnetotransport behaviour in hole-doped cuprates suggests that the higher critical temperature in those materials cannot be attributed solely to an increase in the coupling strength to the antiferromagnetic fluctuations. The success in modelling La2-xCexCuO4 reinforces the notion that resolving the origin of high-temperature superconductivity in hole-doped cuprates may require more than a simple extension of the Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer theory.

Tipping points in complex ecological systems

Review Paper | Theoretical ecology | 2026-10-18 20:00 EDT

Alan Hastings, Sergei Petrovskii, Valerio Lucarini, Andrew Morozov

In the modern physics of complex systems, a topic receiving increasing attention is tipping points. These are usually thought of as states of a system in which changing parameters slightly can lead to a large, and generally abrupt and irreversible, change in properties. There exists a number of different mathematical structures that can show this behaviour, and the one that was originally used (nowadays often referred to as the bifurcation-induced tipping) is just one of many. Different preconditions and/or different levels of details included into the model, reflecting also different environmental forcing, can lead to various tipping mechanisms. Furthermore, in a spatially extended system and/or a system with multiple scales, different parts can react to a change in environmental conditions differently or at a different time, interacting with each other to create a tipping cascade. In this Review, using ecosystems as a paradigm of complex nonlinear open systems, we survey the progress made in tipping point science over the past 15 years and identify gaps in the knowledge.

Despite a vigorous effort, a comprehensive and satisfactory understanding of the dynamics underlying the glass transition in finite-dimensional systems has not yet emerged. One key hallmark of supercooled liquids approaching the glass transition is the breakdown of the classical Stokes-Einstein (SE) relation, manifested as the decoupling between the self-diffusion coefficient and structural relaxation time, commonly described by a fractional SE relation. Whether and how the fractional SE exponents vary remains an ongoing debate, even for the simplest supercooled liquids. Our study demonstrates that investigating dynamical heterogeneity yields more valuable insights into understanding glassy dynamics than expected.

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-10-19 20:00 EDT

Fayez Abu-Ajamieh

We study a quantitative form of noise-induced restoration of entropy concavity for one-dimensional nonequilibrium states relaxing toward a Gaussian thermal equilibrium. A partially thermalized state is modeled by $ X_q=\sqrt q,X+\sqrt{1-q},G$ , where $ G$ is Gaussian equilibrium noise and $ q$ is the retained signal fraction. Numerical searches over broader log-concave families are reported only as supporting evidence.

Thermodynamics of coupled electromagnetic and mass fields

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-10-19 20:00 EDT

Fei Wang

We formulate a unified thermodynamic framework for chemical, mechanical, and electromagnetic transport by extending the Gibbs–Duhem relation to include kinetic free-energy contributions associated with material and field motion. The resulting kinetic Gibbs–Duhem relation establishes a thermodynamic connection between free-energy variations, generalized chemical potentials, pressure, and transport momentum, while preserving the invariance of the system free energy under changes of reference frame. Within a Lagrangian formulation of free-energy evolution, the relation provides a common structure for diffusion, momentum transport, and electromagnetic dynamics. For nonuniform chemical systems, the framework recovers the coupling between Cahn–Hilliard dynamics and the Korteweg stress arising from composition gradients. Its extension to electromagnetic systems incorporates electric, magnetic, charge, and mechanical contributions into the same thermodynamic formulation, yielding a unified description of electromagnetic and momentum evolution. The framework further separates dissipative from nondissipative transport and establishes a common thermodynamic form for mechanical and electromagnetic propagation. In the isentropic limit, the characteristic propagation velocity is determined by the derivative of the thermodynamic pressure with respect to density, yielding the speed of sound for mechanical systems and the speed of light for electromagnetic systems. These results show that kinetic contributions can be incorporated consistently into the Gibbs–Duhem framework, providing a common thermodynamic basis for coupled chemical, mechanical, and electromagnetic transport without introducing separate thermodynamic principles for each mechanism.

Semiclassical Analysis of Wave Dynamics via Wigner Transforms: Applications to the Quantum Harmonic, Anharmonic, and Dirac Oscillator

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-10-19 20:00 EDT

August Burg

In this Master’s thesis, we give a pedagogical introduction to the semiclassical analysis of wave dynamics in single-band and multi-band systems. We show how the semiclassical evolutions of Wigner functions can approximate the dynamics of wave packet centers and wave packet deformations. Three systems of increasing complexity are considered: the quantum harmonic oscillator and the quantum anharmonic oscillator as single-band systems, and the Dirac oscillator as a two-band system. We explain the origins of the first-order semiclassical corrections (Moyal, Littlejohn-Flynn, and Berry) in the equations of motion and examine their effects on predictive accuracy.
Another focus is set on the conditions for validity of the semiclassical analysis. For both the quantum anharmonic and the Dirac oscillators, a dimensionless parameter controlling the accuracy of the approximation is identified. In multi-band systems, the formalism applies only to states within a single adiabatic subspace, which, for the Dirac oscillator, corresponds to an energy branch. This motivates the construction of single-branch almost coherent states: approximately Gaussian wave packets formed by superpositions of states from a single energy branch. Their centers undergo oscillations with an aperiodic envelope. These states enable comparison between semiclassical Wigner function evolution and the commonly used Sundaram-Niu formalism. We show that semiclassical Wigner function evolution captures deformation-related dynamics, including the initial decay of oscillations, which the Sundaram-Niu formalism cannot describe by construction.
Additionally, exact quantum evolution reveals partial relocalization of these single-branch almost coherent states, a phenomenon that cannot be captured by the semiclassical evolution.

We investigate how local particle reservoirs can probe transport and density profiles in tilted spinless fermion chains with nearest-neighbor interactions. Using finite-chain Lindblad calculations with particle injection and extraction at opposite ends, we define a threshold-dependent transmission range from the stationary current as the chain length is varied. Attraction from repulsion in an interacting Chern band: chiral superconductivity, generalized Hall crystals, and competing fractional Chern insulators

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-10-19 20:00 EDT

Fabian Pichler, Clemens Kuhlenkamp, Ashvin Vishwanath, Michael Knap

Experiments realizing strong interactions in partially filled Chern bands have observed fractional Chern insulators (FCIs), superconductors, and topological crystals in close proximity. Yet the microscopic relation between them remains poorly understood. Here, we use tensor network simulations to study repulsively interacting spinless fermions in a tunable topological band of the checkerboard lattice. We characterize the competing phases and identify key features of the experimental phenomenology. FCIs from the Jain sequence transition into chiral p-wave superconductivity both below and above half filling as the band dispersion is enhanced. By contrast, at half filling, a generalized Hall crystal with quantized Hall conductance and a doubled unit cell forms for sufficiently strong coupling. Surprisingly, a weak-coupling approach starting from a metallic parent state captures the nature of both ordered phases at intermediate coupling. We find that fluctuations in the metal generate longer-range attraction, which we analyze within the random-phase approximation. This approach captures the pairing symmetry as well as the radial form factors of the Cooper pairs, and incorporating this attraction into Hartree-Fock selects the observed Hall crystal. Together, these results identify fluctuation-induced attraction from repulsion as a common microscopic ingredient of ordering, while our tensor network computations establish the direct competition of both phases with FCIs.

Quantum-interference correction to conductivity in disordered electronic bands with non-uniform quantum geometry

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-10-19 20:00 EDT

Yip Chun Wong, Asimpunya Mitra, Yong Baek Kim

Weak localization in two-dimensional disordered electron systems originates from constructive interference between time-reversed scattering paths, giving rise to a logarithmically divergent quantum-interference correction to the dc conductivity. This behavior is generally suppressed when time-reversal symmetry (TRS) is broken. In this study, using a band-projected framework motivated by spin- and valley-polarized rhombohedral $ N$ -layer graphene (RNG), we show that nonuniform quantum geometry can qualitatively modify quantum-interference corrections to dc transport. In particular, continuously tuning the quantum geometry can change the sign of the conductivity correction, corresponding to a crossover between localization and antilocalization tendencies. Remarkably, at special values of the parameter controlling the quantum geometry, weak-localization (WL) or weak-antilocalization (WAL) can re-emerge despite broken microscopic TRS. At these special points, the orbital structure of the Bloch states gives rise to an emergent antiunitary symmetry that protects a gapless Cooperon mode and restores the logarithmically divergent quantum-interference correction characteristic of WL or WAL. Our results establish nonuniform quantum geometry as a mechanism for controlling quantum interference and electron localization in multiorbital electronic systems.
