Topological control of polaritonic flatbands in anisotropic van der Waals metasurfaces

Original Paper | Semiconductors | 2026-10-28 20:00 EDT

Connor Heimig, Thomas Weber, Cristina Cruciano, Armando Genco, Thomas Possmayer, Luca Sortino, Gianluca Valentini, Cristian Manzoni, Maxim V. Gorkunov, Giulio Cerullo, Alexander A. Antonov, Andreas Tittl

Anisotropic van der Waals materials exhibit direction-dependent optical and electronic properties, enabling tailored directional light-matter interactions. Rhenium disulfide (ReS2) stands out among them for its strong in-plane anisotropy and thickness-independent direct-bandgap excitons. These excitons can hybridize with light to form exciton-polaritons, rendering ReS2 a compelling material for nanophotonic integration. Metasurfaces are arrays of subwavelength resonators and can support ultrasharp quasi-bound states in the continuum resonances. Topological transformations of their band structure can give rise to flatbands, which host dispersionless states with quenched kinetic energy and vanishing group velocity. Here we show that structuring ReS2 into a resonant metasurface reshapes its photonic band topology, splitting the integer topological charge of the quasi-bound states in the continuum into two half-integer polarization singularities and driving the formation of directional far-field flatbands. Tuned into resonance with the linearly polarized excitonic transitions of ReS2, these flatband states hybridize into distinct directionally polarized exciton-polaritons. This points towards a scalable route to polarization-resolved quantum emitters, flatband-enhanced nonlinear optics and low-threshold polariton devices.

Two-dimensional metal oxides offer a versatile platform for exploring quantum phenomena and enable functionalities beyond those of their bulk counterparts. Despite advances in synthesizing ultrathin and free-standing oxides, controlled fabrication at the single-atomic-layer limit has remained a formidable challenge. Here we report the controlled fabrication of diverse free-standing two-dimensional metal oxides (MO), including CrO, ErO and BiO, down to the single-atomic-layer limit via electron-beam-induced dehalogenation of layered metal oxyhalides (MOX). Using monolayer CrO as a representative system, which features a single-atomic-layer square lattice, we obtain free-standing single-crystalline domains exceeding 104 nm2 and demonstrate controllable patterning at the micrometre scale. This approach further enables the fabrication of MOX-MO lateral heterostructures as well as aligned and twisted metal oxide bilayers, which exhibit pronounced excitonic responses with an unusually large exciton binding energy. Our work opens opportunities for two-dimensional metal oxide monolayers, heterostructures and moiré superlattices, and for exploring emergent excitonic phenomena in low-dimensional oxides.

Imaging hot-electron Thomson cooling beyond the Boltzmann-Onsager framework

Original Paper | Imaging techniques | 2026-10-28 20:00 EDT

Xinrong Zuo, Guokang Zhang, Weikang Lu, Anish Das, Guijian Pang, Ziyi Xu, Ludi Qin, Chengfeng Ding, Hongtao Xu, Siyu Duan, Ziteng Zhang, Pingping Chen, Wu Li, Zhenghua An

Classical Thomson cooling, constrained by lattice-dominated heat dissipation and the weak entropy variation of charge carriers under thermal equilibrium, remains inefficient within the Boltzmann-Onsager framework. Driving electrons far from equilibrium broadens their energy spectrum and smears the Fermi edge, and this amplifies the temperature dependence of the electronic entropy. However, the hot-electron thermoelectric response beyond the classical thermal equilibrium limit has remained unexplored due to the lack of direct access to hot-electron dynamics. Here we image spatial distributions of electron and lattice temperatures together with the corresponding thermoelectric signals in HgTe and HgCdTe nanoconstrictions using coupled scanning-probe techniques. In HgTe, weak electron-phonon coupling allows hot electrons to remain far from thermal equilibrium with the lattice, and this generates large electron temperature gradients and a pronounced Thomson compensation effect. In HgCdTe the response shifts towards lattice-mediated heat redistribution due to the suppressed non-equilibrium state. These observations reveal a pronounced departure from the conventional near-equilibrium Boltzmann-Onsager expectations in high-field thermoelectricity, identify the Thomson effect as a key channel of non-equilibrium energy dissipation and establish a platform for exploring non-equilibrium thermoelectric phenomena in nanoscale conductors.

Pervasive patterns in bird songs resemble those in human speech and song and are independent of vocal learning

Research Article | Comparative vocalization | 2026-10-29 03:00 EDT

Logan S. James, Kendra Oudyk, Maria I. Pescaru, Erin M. Wall, Yining Chen, William D. Pearse, Jon T. Sakata

Bird songs are often described as analogs to human speech and songs, but expansive comparisons are necessary to determine analogy and mechanistic substrates. By analyzing global corpora consisting of 367 bird species (>74,500 vocal elements from ~5900 songs) within two clades of vocal learners (oscines and hummingbirds) and related nonlearners (suboscines and nightjars) and 52 human languages (>15,000 elements in >800 speech or song phrases), we revealed common patterns in bird songs that resemble prevalent patterns in human speech and song with variation in resemblance to speech or song. These data underscore the deep analogies between bird and human vocalizations and convergent motor mechanisms shaping communication.

Altermagnetism has been recognized as a magnetic phase that bridges the properties of ferro- and antiferromagnetism. The momentum-dependent spin splitting observed in these materials reflects their distinctive symmetry characteristics, which also establish the conditions for chiral magnons to emerge. Here we provide direct experimental evidence for a chiral magnon in the altermagnetic candidate manganese telluride (MnTe) revealed by circular-dichroism resonant inelastic x-ray scattering. This mode, which we term chiral altermagnon, exhibits a distinct momentum dependence of its spin polarization consistent with the altermagnetic g-wave symmetry of MnTe. Emergence of Chern metal in a moiré Kondo lattice

Research Article | 2026-10-29 03:00 EDT

Wenjin Zhao, Zui Tao, Yichi Zhang, Bowen Shen, Zhongdong Han, Patrick Knüppel, Yihang Zeng, Zhengchao Xia, Kenji Watanabe, Takashi Taniguchi, Jie Shan, Kin Fai Mak

A Chern metal is a two-dimensional metallic state of matter carrying chiral edge states. We report the observation of a Chern metal at the onset of the magnetic Kondo breakdown in a frustrated moiré Kondo lattice–angle-aligned MoTe2/WSe2 bilayers. The state is compressible and is manifested by a nearly quantized Hall resistance and a finite longitudinal resistance arising from a bad metallic bulk. We demonstrate the presence of a chiral edge state by nonlocal transport measurements and current-induced quantum anomalous Hall breakdown. Magnetic circular dichroism measurements further reveal a magnetization plateau for the Chern metal before a metamagnetic transition at the Kondo breakdown. Our results open an opportunity for moiré engineering of exotic quantum phases through the interplay between band topology and Kondo interactions.

Altermagnets are compensated magnets with symmetry-enforced spin split bands. Direct spectroscopic evidence for their existence has been reported in several materials. Rapid rifting of the Gulf of California oblique rift (GOCOR) ~6 million to 8 million years ago is a key example; it may have resulted from the lithosphere responding to external kinematics or acceleration of long-lived extension. New Submission | Quantum Gases (cond-mat.quant-gas) | 2026-10-28 20:00 EDT

We show that the steady state of a periodically driven Bose gas coupled to a thermal bath features emergent Fermi surfaces both in its high temperature and Bose-Einstein-condensate (BEC) states.
