Beating the break-even point with autonomous quantum error correction

Original Paper | Qubits | 2026-10-22 20:00 EDT

Yi Li, Qingyuan Mei, Qing-Xuan Jie, Weizhou Cai, Yue Li, Zhiyuan Liu, Zi-Jie Chen, Zihan Xie, Xu Cheng, Xingyu Zhao, Zhenghao Luo, Mengxiang Zhang, Xu-Bo Zou, Chang-Ling Zou, Yiheng Lin, Jiangfeng Du

Quantum error correction protects fragile quantum information by encoding it in high-dimensional Hilbert spaces. Conventional protocols typically require repeated syndrome measurements, real-time feedback or post-selection, and multiple physical qubits, which add technical complexity. Here we realize autonomous quantum error correction with a logical qubit encoded in multiple internal spin states of a single trapped ion and surpass the break-even point for qubit lifetime. We use engineered spin-motion couplings to transfer error-induced entropy into motional modes, which is then dissipated by sympathetic cooling with an ancilla ion. This eliminates the need for measurement and feedback. By repeatedly applying this protocol, we observe lifetime extension under both ambient laboratory conditions and injected low-frequency magnetic noise. Our results show that the multilevel structure of trapped ions can support hardware-efficient quantum error correction and robust quantum memories with reduced overhead.

Stochastic Diversity Stabilization in Nonlinear Competitive Dynamics: A Statistical Mechanics Approach to Fluctuation Suppression and Finite-Size Scaling

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-10-23 20:00 EDT

Ruocun Ou

Stochastic forcing can prevent diversity collapse in competitive dynamical systems, yet the quantitative conditions and the dynamical mechanism responsible remain poorly characterized. We address both questions in a class of nonlinear stochastic replicator dynamics on the N-dimensional probability simplex, parameterized by a nonlinearity exponent gamma and additive Gaussian noise of intensity sigma. Under the baseline interaction, the minimum noise required to sustain diversity decays as a power law, sigma_c ~ N^(-alpha); replacing the baseline interaction with a superlinear variant shifts alpha from about 1.1 to about 2.0, in agreement with a linear-stability prediction alpha = (gamma+2)/2, identifying alpha as a model-specific quantity rather than a universal index. Scanning the (gamma, sigma) plane reveals a smooth dynamical crossover: the fluctuation susceptibility chi_H = N Var(H) peaks at gamma ~ 0.7 (chi_H ~ 0.6) and is suppressed at high gamma, with no divergence near gamma ~ 1.4; matched finite-size analysis finds no systematic divergence of the peak with N, ruling out a genuine second-order phase transition. The entropy relaxation time tau_relax(gamma) remains approximately constant (about 2.9 time units) across the full range of gamma, even as chi_H decreases sixfold; this decoupling between relaxation timescale and fluctuation amplitude implies that suppression arises from convergence of diverse pathways to similar endpoints rather than from accelerated relaxation along a single channel. These findings identify nonlinear fluctuation suppression via pathway multiplicity – distinct from both noise-induced transitions and equilibrium criticality – as the operative stabilization pattern in this class of nonequilibrium stochastic systems.

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-10-23 20:00 EDT

Distinguishing rapid local energy exchange from sustained transport matters when thermal conductivity is inferred from finite simulations. Foresight-dependent entropy production in stochastic two-agent dynamics

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-10-23 20:00 EDT

Arpan Dey

Nonreciprocal interactions can break detailed balance by sustaining stationary probability currents. Here we ask whether such currents can also emerge when the agents’ immediate coordination preferences are reciprocal, but their state-adjustment rules are determined by forward-looking optimization (foresight). We study a minimal stochastic linear-quadratic game in which two agents control noisy scalar states and minimize individual discounted costs in a full-information feedback Nash equilibrium. The optimized controls yield an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process whose stationary probability current and entropy-production rate, measuring statistical time-irreversibility, can be calculated exactly. For reciprocal coordination with unequal self-anchoring strengths, the effective drift asymmetry first appears at order $ \tau^5$ , where $ \tau$ is the planning timescale, and the normalized entropy production consequently scales as $ \tau^8$ in the limit $ \tau\to0$ . We derive this cancellation from the coupled Nash Riccati equations. For conformist-contrarian coupling, normalized entropy production approaches a nonzero short-foresight limit and initially decreases from that limit by an amount proportional to $ \tau^2$ . We also determine the long-foresight limit for a conformist-contrarian pair with equal self-anchoring strengths. Numerical solutions of the full Nash equations verify the asymptotic laws and reveal the effect of foresight. Thus, forward-looking optimization can generate or reduce normalized stationary irreversibility, depending on the underlying interaction.

Electronic Screening as a Design Lever in La-Doped BaTiO$_3$: Coupled Polarization, Dielectric Response, and Optical Anisotropy

New Submission | Materials Science (cond-mat.mtrl-sci) | 2026-10-23 20:00 EDT

Sikandar Azam, Muhammad Fareed, Qaiser Rafiq, Jan Minar, Abed Alataway

Donor substitution can tune the coupled ferroelectric, dielectric, electronic, and optical properties of perovskite oxides by modifying lattice distortions and electronic screening. We investigate these effects in La-doped BaTiO3 using density functional theory, density-functional perturbation theory, and the Berry-phase formalism for polarization. For a model with 12.5% La substitution at the Ba site, La incorporation induces anisotropic lattice contraction, reduces tetragonality and Ti off-centering, and weakens the energetic stabilization of the tetragonal ferroelectric phase. The accompanying reduction in polarization is associated with enhanced electronic screening and reduced anomalous Born effective charges. Phonon calculations indicate that the relaxed doped structure is dynamically stable, while hardening of a low-frequency polar mode is consistent with weakened ferroelectricity. The high-frequency dielectric response increases moderately, whereas the static dielectric response decreases as the lattice contribution is suppressed. Electronic-structure calculations show a Fermi-level shift toward the conduction band without mid-gap defect states. The optical spectra exhibit an absorption-edge shift consistent with a Burstein-Moss-type mechanism, enhanced near-edge transparency, and reduced birefringence. Together, these results suggest a microscopic connection between donor-induced screening, polar-mode hardening, reduced dynamical charges, polarization suppression, and decreased optical anisotropy. The conclusions apply to the investigated 12.5% composition; calculations at additional concentrations are needed to establish quantitative doping trends.

Vacancy-driven topological transition of a Chern insulator

New Submission | Disordered Systems and Neural Networks (cond-mat.dis-nn) | 2026-10-23 20:00 EDT

Peize Ding, Raquel Queiroz

We develop a microscopic description of the strong-disorder transition in a Chern insulator using the in-gap ring states bound to individual vacancies. Using Green’s function formalism, we construct an energy-dependent Hamiltonian describing their hybridization. In a commensurate periodic vacancy lattice, a Dirac inversion of the zero-energy vacancy kernel accounts for the change in the occupied-state Chern number of the full system. We treat weak positional disorder using the self-consistent Born approximation and numerical disorder averaging, and study independently distributed vacancies using transfer-matrix and multifractal scaling. The local Chern marker and current textures track the transition in real space. Together, these results highlight the role that nonperturbative ring states have as microscopic degrees of freedom through which strong disorder destroys the Chern phase.

Nature of the bosonic edge phase in a double quantum spin Hall insulator

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-10-23 20:00 EDT

Andreas Feuerpfeil, Ronny Thomale, Trithep Devakul, Nicolas Regnault, Julian May-Mann

Field-theoretic analysis has predicted that the edge of a double quantum spin Hall insulator (DQSHI) can undergo an interaction-driven transition from a phase with fermionic edge modes into one with only bosonic modes. All single-fermion edge excitations are gapped in this bosonic edge phase, but fermion pairs remain gapless, leading to distinct experimental signatures. We numerically simulate the interacting DQSHI edge and verify the existence of the bosonic edge phase. This calculation requires an explicit bulk embedding of the DQSHI edge, due to its mixed anomaly between charge and $ S^z$ conservation. We hence employ segment DMRG on an infinite two-dimensional cylinder, where the DQSHI bulk is modeled in terms of two copies of the spin-up lowest Landau level, combined with their time-reversed counterparts. We find that interactions can open a single-fermion gap at the DQSHI edge while fermion pairs remain gapless, matching the predictions for the bosonic edge phase. We find the scaling of the single-fermion gap with interaction strength to be consistent with field-theoretic calculations, and discuss the relevance of the bosonic edge phase to twisted homobilayers of WSe$ _2$ and MoTe$ _2$ . We elaborate on the methodological extension of the segment geometry used here to other topological phases with edge anomalies.

Discrete time liquids

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-10-23 20:00 EDT

Zuo Wang, Paul M. Schindler, Marin Bukov, Marios H. Michael

Discrete time crystals exhibit robust subharmonic oscillations, spontaneously breaking the discrete time-translation symmetry of a periodic drive. Since their emergence, the question of whether other types of temporal many-body order can exist has attracted considerable attention. Here we identify discrete time liquids – a new form of spatiotemporal order characterized by short-range temporal coherence as a result of time-solitons. We study a two-dimensional spin system coupled to a finite-temperature cavity, whose fluctuations provide a noisy, approximately periodic drive with only finite temporal coherence. Remarkably, the spins nevertheless form a metastable state with robust period-doubled dynamics over an extended parameter regime. Unlike a time crystal, this state has only finite temporal coherence: individual trajectories stochastically switch between the two symmetry-related temporal configurations of the period-doubled response. We identify these switches as time-solitons: topological defects in temporal order that preserve local period doubling while destroying long-range temporal coherence, thereby producing the time liquid state. We propose a minimal model which reproduces the distribution of temporal correlations of the many-body system, suggesting a general description of the time liquid as a time crystal with a finite density of time-solitons. Because these solitons can occur at arbitrary times, ensemble averaging restores continuous time-translation symmetry, raising the intriguing possibility that discrete time liquids can exist in thermal equilibrium.

Charge-monitored Luttinger liquid

New Submission | Disordered Systems and Neural Networks (cond-mat.dis-nn) | 2026-10-23 20:00 EDT

Victor Gurarie, Leo Radzihovsky

We discuss phases of interacting fermionic Luttinger liquids without particle number conservation, such as those in proximity to a superconductor, subjected to weak continuous measurements of their particle density, in the regime where the bosonization approach is applicable. We show that the phases of these systems are controlled by the competition between measurements, which favor the area-law phase and particle tunneling in and out of the Luttinger liquid, which favors the volume-law phase. The volume-law phase in this system is always charge fuzzy. We show that within this framework, the phase boundary between the two phases is given by a universal dependence of the Luttinger parameter on the measurement rate. This behavior can be contrasted with the non-universal phase boundaries in more generic highly excited one dimensional systems whose behavior is no longer captured by the Luttinger liquid framework.

Charge Density Waves and In-Gap States in Coupled Chains and Moiré Superlattices

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-10-23 20:00 EDT

Muhammad Akram, Onur Erten

Charge density waves (CDWs) arise from the interplay between electrons and lattice distortions and are a hallmark of low-dimensional systems. Here we study coupled Su-Schrieffer-Heeger chains, both lattice-matched ladders and lattice-mismatched moiré superlattices, and find a rich set of CDW phases together with symmetry-protected bound states. In lattice-matched chains, the competition between electron-phonon coupling and interchain tunneling drives a sequence of CDW phases, including staggered dimers, trimers, and incommensurate states. These phases are generally gapped and host bound states protected by crystalline symmetries rather than chiral symmetry, and are therefore not pinned to zero energy. Lattice mismatch further reconstructs these phases into long-period supermoiré CDWs, with an ordered array of defects, pinned to the moiré length scale, that host localized electronic states. Interchain tunneling and lattice mismatch thus emerge as natural control parameters for CDW order and bound states in one-dimensional chains.

Non-destructive early detection of water stress is critical for precision agriculture, yet current detection methods are often labor-intensive or destructive. We propose a rapid, non-invasive method based on Spectral Submanifold (SSM) reduction to analyze the nonlinear vibration dynamics of soybean plants. Unlike traditional linear modal analysis, our data-driven approach reveals a distinct transition in the dominant dynamics of the plant stem as drought progresses. Specifically, we uncover a sign reversal in the nonlinear damping coefficient within SSM-reduced models of full plants. $π$-Flux Tuning of Quantum Paramagnetism in the Triangular-Lattice Hubbard Model

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-10-23 20:00 EDT

Zhaochen Liu, Andreas Feuerpfeil, Ronny Thomale

We compare two time-reversal-symmetric triangular-lattice Hubbard models at half filling: the zero-flux model and a staggered $ \pi$ -flux model with zero and $ \pi$ flux through oppositely oriented triangles. Although both reduce to the same Heisenberg model at leading order in the strong-coupling limit, their distinct charge fluctuation profiles yield different quantum paramagnetic regimes. Combining a strong-coupling expansion with infinite density matrix renormalization group (iDMRG) calculations, we find that the positive ring-exchange of the zero-flux model promotes spontaneous chirality, consistent with its notion of a chiral spin liquid at intermediate coupling. In the staggered-$ \pi$ -flux model, whose noninteracting limit is a Dirac semimetal, the ring exchange changes sign and disfavors chiral order and we find no resolvable scalar chirality. This renders flux-tuned ring-exchange an efficient discriminator between chiral and non-chiral quantum paramagnets at finite coupling.

Non-adiabatic Optimisation via Localisation-Delocalisation Transitions

New Submission | Disordered Systems and Neural Networks (cond-mat.dis-nn) | 2026-10-23 20:00 EDT

Ryan Smith, Sabrina Yue Wang, Filippo Maria Gambetta, Raul A. Santos

The promise of efficiently solving classical optimisation problems on quantum computers is tantalizing. As many such problems can be mapped to approximating the ground state of spin-glasses, paradigmatic models for studying many-body localisation, it is natural to ask if some of the intuition coming from quantum statistical mechanics can help guide the construction of novel quantum optimisation algorithms. In this work, we discuss how continuous reverse quantum annealing crossing a many-body localisation transition can be accelerated by considering the density of Landau-Zener crossings as the system is quenched. We provide an interpretation of this behaviour in terms of an effective Fokker-Planck equation for the success probability of the algorithm. We compare the performance of our digitised algorithm, which we call FRIO, against that of the QAOA for finding the ground state of spin-glass problems, in particular the SK model. The numerical results suggest that with suitably selected circuit parameters which can be efficiently found using thermalizing dynamics, FRIO almost always outperforms the conventional QAOA ansatz with pre-optimized angles.

Understanding these mechanisms remains challenging due to the complex interplay between interparticle forces, hydrodynamic interactions, and shear flow. Reconfigurable high-power Kerr optical parametric oscillators with sub-hertz linewidths

Article | Nonlinear optics | 2026-10-22 20:00 EDT

Bin Zhang, Xin Zhao, Di Xia, Linyi Wang, Liyang Luo, Gengze Wang, Guosheng Lin, Ran Huang, Xueyan Zheng, Zhaohui Li, Hui Jing, Dingyuan Tang

Integrated coherent light sources combining broad wavelength tunability, narrow-linewidth, and high output power are essential for precision spectroscopy, coherent communications, and quantum information processing, yet remain challenging to realize simultaneously. Kerr microresonator optical parametric oscillators (OPOs) provide coherent wavelength access beyond conventional gain media, but their oscillating signal-idler pairs are typically fixed by the dispersion and loss of the fabricated resonator, while pump-noise transfer and nonlinear mode competition limit coherence and power scaling. Here, we demonstrate post-fabrication reconfiguration of Kerr OPOs through coherent feedback enabled by mode-selective sideband self-injection locking, without the need for resonator redesign. By tuning the relative effective gain of competing parametric channels, coherent feedback makes a selected signal-idler pair dominant over the free-running state. At a fixed pump wavelength, our method enables stepwise sideband selection across 1.27 THz and continuous tuning over 202.17 GHz, while joint tuning of the pump and feedback-selected sideband extends the accessible span beyond 23 nm. The same feedback reduces the intrinsic linewidth of the selected sideband to sub-hertz, corresponding to a ~ 30,000-fold reduction from the free-running OPO and suppresses cluster-comb formation to deliver more than 42 mW on-chip sideband power. Our work establishes coherent feedback as a new control degree of freedom for reconfiguring nonlinear oscillation states in integrated Kerr photonics.
