Current-Driven Molecular Machine with Rotation-Enhanced Conductance

Original Paper | Molecular machines and motors | 2026-10-21 20:00 EDT

Anil Kumar Singh, León Martin, Matea Sršen, Freja Schou Guttesen, Bing-Zhong Hu, Yuta Ito, Stephan Korsgaard Pedersen, Fadhil Suleiman Kamounah, Fabian Pauly, Michael Pittelkow, Jing-Tao Lü, Per Hedegård, Oren Tal

For more than two millennia, Archimedean screws have been used for water pumping and, more recently, as turbines in hydroelectric power stations[1]. Their helical-blade structure, essential to their function, is also found in chiral molecules and nanostructures, where injected electric current has been proposed to generate torque-mediated rotation[2,3]. Here, we present a torque-driven molecular machine with a rotor actuated by electric current and conductance enhanced by its rotation. Suspending a helicene molecule between two electrode tips, we show that an applied voltage induces cyclic molecular motion with 100% directional preference that can be inverted by the voltage’s polarity. Above a voltage threshold, the current through the rotating molecule is significantly enhanced, and the enhancement onset is tuned by stretching or compressing the helical molecule. Such effects were absent in previous helical molecular junctions, likely due to structural and operational differences[4-6]. Calculations show that molecular rotation by current-induced forces explains both the observed cyclic motion and current enhancement. This mechanism predicts a transition from stepwise to continuous rotation at a threshold voltage, yielding a current increase as rotation allows adopting highly conducting configurations not accessible at rest. Our work introduces a prototype for a new class of torque-driven molecular machines with perfect rotor unidirectionality and significant current modulation.

Magnonic circuits that use spin waves for nanoscale signal processing and computing are widely regarded as a promising alternative to traditional semiconductor electronics. Wang

The surface code is a leading candidate for harnessing long-range entanglement to protect logical quantum information against errors, and teleportation of the code’s logical states is desirable for robust quantum information processing. However, large numbers of physical qubits are needed to produce well-protected logical states, making quantum teleportation a formidable experimental challenge. Here we demonstrate the teleportation of surface code states with code distances up to 7, implemented on a superconducting quantum processor with 125 qubits. We obtain a phase diagram for successful teleportation of the logical state by tuning the local entangling gates uniformly across a finite threshold. This serves as a guiding principle to minimize the entanglement needed to achieve many-body teleportation. Our results establish the potential for surface code teleportation to enable distributed fault-tolerant quantum computation.

The underlying mechanism of the thermal Hall effect generated by phonons in a variety of insulators is yet to be identified. In this work, we report on a sizable thermal Hall conductivity in nickel phosphorus trisulfide (NiPS3), a van der Waals stack of honeycomb layers with a zigzag antiferromagnetic order. Two-dimensional moiré materials provide a versatile platform to explore phase transitions in strongly correlated systems. We observed notable electron-hole asymmetry in GWC melting: Hole-doped GWCs yielded interaction-driven disordered states, whereas electron-doped GWCs melted into delocalized liquid-like states. Realization of decoherence-induced average symmetry-protected topological phases on quantum processors

Ruizhe Shen, Xuejia Yu, Ching Hua Lee

Symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phases are conventionally formulated for pure states protected by exact symmetries. In open quantum systems, however, decoherence generates mixed-state ensembles in which average symmetry can instead emerge only after averaging over microscopic trajectories. In this work, we realize decoherence-induced average SPT (ASPT) order on programmable quantum processors. Starting from a one-dimensional cluster SPT, we engineer sublattice-selective Pauli-$Z$ dephasing through ancilla-assisted quantum circuits. We diagnose the loss of strong symmetry through the decay of the symmetry charge, while the engineered channel preserves the corresponding symmetry at the ensemble level. Moreover, a two-replica R’enyi-2 string correlator measured through destructive SWAP readout remains nontrivial, revealing the ASPT structure encoded at the density-matrix level. We further show that the engineered dephasing redistributes spectral weight among reduced-stabilizer sectors while preserving the characteristic twofold pairing of the half-chain entanglement spectrum. These results establish a gate-based route to engineering and probing ASPT order and demonstrate structured decoherence as a programmable resource for realizing mixed-state topological quantum matter.
