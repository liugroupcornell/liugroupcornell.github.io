Atomic-scale spin sensing of a 2D d-wave altermagnet via helical tunneling

Original Paper | Topological insulators | 2026-10-12 20:00 EDT

Zhuying Wang, Shuikang Yu, Xingkai Cheng, Xiaoyu Xiao, Wanru Ma, Yitian Ma, Feixiong Quan, Hongxi Song, Kunming Zhang, Yunmei Zhang, Wenhao Liu, Priti Yadav, Haohao Sheng, Aifeng Wang, Xiangbiao Shi, Zhijun Wang, Qian Niu, Yang Gao, Bin Xiang, Junwei Liu, Zhenyu Wang, Xianhui Chen

Altermagnets simultaneously possess momentum-dependent nonrelativistic spin splitting and zero net magnetization (1-6). Their defining crystalline symmetry connects opposite-spin sublattices in real space and imposes alternating spin polarization on the symmetry-related electronic states in momentum space (1-3). Identifying such an order and its associated spin response therefore calls for atomic-scale spin-sensitive measurements in both real and momentum space, an experimental milestone yet to be achieved. Here we show that the helical edge modes of the higher-order topological insulator α-Bi4Br4 can serve as a spin-selective scanning tunnelling microscopy probe of the two-dimensional d-wave altermagnetic order in the surface layer of KV2Se2O. Atomically registered mapping reveals the spatial relationship between the two V sublattices and a density-wave lattice modulation, as well as real-space patterns consistent with the checkerboard antiferromagnetic order. Field-dependent spectroscopy further finds opposite energy shifts of the unidirectional quasiparticle excitations extending along two orthogonal crystal axes and a predominantly out-of-plane magnetic response. In momentum space, spin-sensitive quasiparticle interference imaging reveals a bias-reversing d-wave anisotropy between the symmetry-paired X and Y valleys. These results provide converging evidence for two-dimensional d-wave altermagnetism on the surface of KV2Se2O and demonstrate helical-edge-mode tunnelling as a route for atomic-scale spin sensing in quantum materials.

Selective, Catalytic Oxidation of Polyethylene Surfaces

Original Paper | Homogeneous catalysis | 2026-10-12 20:00 EDT

Cristian P. Woroch, Liana Klivansky, Declan P. McCarthy, Heidi Miao, Zachary Fink, Xiaobing Zuo, Christopher Guthrie, Cheng Wang, Brett A. Helms, John F. Hartwig

Polyethylene (PE) accounts for more than a quarter of all plastic produced, owing to its exceptional material properties and scalable production.1 However, pristine PE lacks critical properties, such as adhesion, that are needed for many additional commercial applications.2 Selective post-polymerization functionalization of PE can impart properties that are absent in the pristine polymer, but global functionalization in a uniform medium perturbs bulk properties.3-5 Here, we demonstrate catalytic functionalization of solid PE at a solid-liquid interface. The resulting materials possess enhanced surface properties while maintaining bulk properties, such as melting temperature or tensile strength. The method selectively converts methylene units to ketones with just 0.001 mol% iron catalyst below 80 °C with inexpensive oxidants. PE functionalized at the solid-liquid interface adheres to polar surfaces up to 90-fold more strongly than does pristine PE and up to 4-fold more strongly than does PE functionalized in a uniform medium at a comparable level. We demonstrate that the surface ketones can be modified to enhance adhesive strength further and that the augmented surface properties persist for longer than they do after other unselective functionalizations. Thus, selective C-H bond functionalization at the solid-liquid interface produces materials inaccessible by bulk functionalization methods or by traditional surface oxidations.

Thermal conductance of atomic-scale glass reaches 40% of quantum limit

Original Paper | Nanoscale devices | 2026-10-12 20:00 EDT

Sai C. Yelishala, Yunxuan Zhu, P. M. Martinez, O. Mateos-Lopez, Wan Xiong, Dishan Chooi, Sophie Kimura, Juan Carlos Cuevas, J. G. Vilhena, Longji Cui

Heat dissipation limits the performance of nanoscale electronic and quantum devices, yet the fundamental limits of phonon-mediated heat transport at the atomic scale remains unknown. By contrast, atomic-scale electron transport has been extensively studied over the past decades using electronic break-junction platforms. Here we show a custom-fabricated, picowatt-resolution scanning thermal probe that uses thermal feedback to create phononic break junctions at the ultimate atomic scale. We report the formation of atomic junctions of amorphous silicon dioxide (a-SiO2)–an archetypal glass–capturing, in real time, the transition from a bulk diffusive heat insulator to a ballistic efficient heat conductor as the tip is withdrawn from a nano-contact to an atomic contact. Remarkably, atomically thin glass junctions support phononic thermal conductance up to 40% of the quantum limit at room temperature, approximately an order of magnitude higher than the phonon thermal conductance of crystalline gold. These results, in conjunction with our atomic simulations, demonstrate that intrinsic structural disorder, which strongly affects macroscopic thermal properties, does not fundamentally constrain heat flow at the atomic limit. Instead, we find that it is the Debye frequency that dictates the phononic thermal conductance. We expect our approach to offer an experimental platform to study heat flow via wave-like coherent phonons and enable the engineering of high-performance thermal phononic devices, such as a thermal diode and transistor.

Spinodal-controlled freeze-out and nucleation at the first-order superfluid-supersolid transition of a dipolar gas

New Submission | Quantum Gases (cond-mat.quant-gas) | 2026-10-12 20:00 EDT

Wai Kui Wong

Dipolar quantum gases in elongated traps become supersolid through a transition that is continuous at intermediate density but first order at low and high density; at the continuous point, ramps of the scattering length follow Kibble–Zurek scaling. We study ramps across the first-order superfluid–supersolid transition of $ ^{164}$ Dy in an infinite tube using the three-dimensional extended Gross–Pitaevskii equation with truncated-Wigner noise. With quantum noise only, the uniform superfluid survives the metastable window and transforms after its spinodal, where the roton softens. For onsets occurring during the linear ramp, the delay measured from this spinodal has a fitted exponent $ \zeta = 0.36 \pm 0.02$ , close to the continuous-transition result and the mean-field value $ 1/3$ . The quoted uncertainty combines statistical and systematic sensitivities. Reverse ramps show a comparable delay beyond the fold of the crystal branch, producing dynamical hysteresis. Initial thermal noise with nominal temperatures of 2.5–10 nK produces crystalline regions inside the metastable window during holds, with faster nucleation closer to the spinodal and at higher initial temperature. Without a heat bath these effective rates depend on preparation: ramps crossing the window at the same rate nucleate early in 2 of 32 or 11 of 16 realizations depending on their starting point. A crossover time inferred from hold rates is therefore an estimate, not a measured boundary for the ramp protocol. At low density, slower ramps select larger spacings in a crystal of separated droplets, and droplets survive far above the transition on reverse ramps.

Flat-band quantum geometry from embedded quench dynamics

New Submission | Quantum Gases (cond-mat.quant-gas) | 2026-10-12 20:00 EDT

Ilia Komissarov, Daniel E. Sheehy, Ahana Chakraborty

Quantum geometry underlies a wide range of correlated phenomena in electronic systems, yet accessing the geometry of individual bands remains experimentally challenging. Advances in quantum simulators, such as ultracold atoms in optical lattices, have enabled local state preparation and site-resolved measurements, offering direct access to quantum dynamics beyond conventional transport probes. These developments naturally raise the question of whether band-resolved quantum geometry can be extracted from local occupation readouts. Here, we propose an on-site quench protocol to probe the flat-band quantum geometry via local occupation measurements in an atomic lattice. Our scheme dynamically isolates the flat-band sector by coupling a one-dimensional flat-band lattice to a two-dimensional flat-band bath. The bath selectively removes dispersive excitations, while compact localized states remain trapped due to their overlap with the flat-band modes of the bath. We show that the late-time occupation pattern directly encodes the integrated overlaps between flat-band states separated by the wavevector of the initial density pattern, thereby enabling access to integrated quantum distances between the states separated by the commensurate momenta $ \delta k = 2 \pi s/r$ , $ s,r \in \mathbb Z$ and to the integrated flat-band quantum metric. Our results thereby establish a direct connection between quantum geometry and the long-time memory of an initial spatial pattern in flat-band systems.

Enhanced Coulomb pseudopotentials in high-pressure hydrides from first principles

New Submission | Superconductivity (cond-mat.supr-con) | 2026-10-12 20:00 EDT

Kieran Bozier, Chris J. Pickard

In the Eliashberg theory of superconductivity, Coulomb interactions oppose the phonon-mediated attraction and suppress the superconducting critical temperature $ T_{\mathrm{c}}$ . However, most first-principles predictions continue to treat this repulsion with an empirical Coulomb pseudopotential $ \mu^\ast_{\text{ME}}$ in the range 0.1-0.15. Here we present first-principles calculations of the Coulomb parameter $ \mu$ and pseudopotential $ \mu^\ast_{\text{ME}}$ in superconducting hydrides across a range of pressures. The screened Coulomb interaction is evaluated at the random phase approximation level with the Mantel code. We demonstrate that several hydride systems, including H$ 3$ S, YSbH$ 6$ and Mg$ 2$ IrH$ 6$ , have strong Coulomb repulsions driven by a large density of states at the Fermi energy, $ N{\mathrm{F}}$ . For Mg$ 2$ IrH$ 6$ we obtain $ \mu=0.62$ and $ \mu^\ast{\text{ME}}\approx0.39$ , leading to a 40 K suppression in $ T{\mathrm{c}}$ relative to a standard $ \mu^\ast{\text{ME}}=0.16$ . Our obtained $ \mu^\ast{\text{ME}}$ is far above the conventional range and, accounting for the energy dependence of the density of states, resolves the factor-of-two discrepancy between $ T{\mathrm{c}}$ estimates of previous studies. Across all systems and pressures studied, we find that $ \mu$ closely tracks $ N_{\mathrm{F}}$ , suggesting that commonly used $ \mu^\ast_{\text{ME}}$ values lead to overestimated $ T_{\mathrm{c}}$ in materials where the density of states at the Fermi energy is large.

Odd-parity surface magnetism in a Kondo insulator

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-10-12 20:00 EDT

Maxim Dzero, Brian M. Andersen

We formulate a theory of spin-polarized tunneling between a nanowire tip of a topological Kondo insulator SmB$ _6$ and the bi-collinear antiferromagnet Fe$ _{1+x}$ Te in which the tunneling conductance which measures magnetic contrast changes sign when the bias is reversed. First we show that no time reversal symmetric tip, however strong its spin-momentum locking is, can produce experimentally observed magnetic contrast, which implies that time reversal must be broken at the tip surface. Within a mean-field treatment of the surface theory of SmB$ _6$ we find that the interaction between conduction d-and f-electrons drives a surface phase transition near T$ _c$ ~10 K in which the time reversal odd component of the Kondo hybridization amplitude condenses in the same symmetry channel as an out-of-plane surface magnetization, gapping out the Dirac surface states. Below the transition the spin-resolved spectral function of each gapped surface Dirac cone is odd in energy about its node. For a charge-neutral apex the Fermi level lies between the surface $ \Gamma$ and $ X$ Dirac nodes, so the spin-polarized conductance, which is determined by this function at the bias energy, reverses its sign close to zero bias. This sign change is thus a spectroscopic signature of time reversal symmetry breaking on the surface of SmB6.

Entropy Production in Mesoscopic Electronic Transport Beyond Weak-Coupling

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-10-12 20:00 EDT

Ivana R. A. C. Lucena, Caio H. Lewenkopf, Alexandre R. Rocha

We investigate the full statistics of entropy production in coherent mesoscopic conductors beyond the weak-coupling regime. Using the inside-outside perspective and the Landauer-Büttiker scattering framework, we relate entropy production to the full counting statistics of particle transport and show that the corresponding cumulant-generating function satisfies the fluctuation symmetry independently of the coupling strength. We obtain closed analytical expressions for the cumulant-generating function in the weak- and strong-coupling limits, enabling a detailed characterization of entropy production distributions and higher-order cumulants. These cumulants reveal distinct signatures of resonant transport and coherent fermionic partitioning. While coupling-induced broadening substantially modifies the transport statistics, the fluctuation symmetry imposed by microscopic reversibility remains preserved in the long-time limit.

Spectral optimization of spherical lattices and applications to classical and quantum interacting spin systems

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-10-12 20:00 EDT

Jonas Völler, Grigorios Makris, Fabian Hassler, Stefan Wessel

We develop an optimization algorithm for spherical lattice discretizations based on the spectrum of the discretized Laplace operator. Starting from refined icosahedral lattices, we minimize the splitting of the $ (2\ell+1)$ , $ \ell=0,1,2,\dots$ , continuum multiplets expected from $ \mathrm{SO}(3)$ symmetry via gradient descent. This approach simultaneously produces lattices based on triangles with nearly uniform circumradii and perimeters. Higher-angular-momentum descendant states reveal weak residual symmetry breaking beyond the sectors protected by the underlying icosahedral symmetry.

A recent preprint by OpenAI resolved the spherical fermionic Laughlin spectral-gap conjecture for the full $ V_1$ interaction by a Fock-space inequality: $ H_Q^2\ge \gamma H_Q$ on the lowest-Landau-level Fock space for all large flux $ Q$ and every $ \gamma$ below $ 0.0461673\ldots$ , so that the gap above the Laughlin state at filling $ 1/3$ is at least $ 1/25$ . Bilayer graphene (BLG) is a unique electronic material offering electrostatic tunability of its band gap. The quantum geometry of the chemical bond

New Submission | Materials Science (cond-mat.mtrl-sci) | 2026-10-12 20:00 EDT

Gabrielle Carrel, Nishchhal Verma, Fatmagül Katmer, Leslie M. Schoop, Raquel Queiroz

Electronic properties of materials depend both on band energies and quantum geometry of electronic states. We relate bonding indices to the quantum geometric tensor (QGT), showing how chemical bonding dictates the momentum-space texture of bands, bridging chemical and physical descriptions of solids. We show the QGT decomposed into the three center bond index, weighted by their oriented area. Resolving these contributions by orbital pair reveals the donor–acceptor character that distinguish ionic, covalent, hypervalent, and metavalent bonding. We identify the bonding pattern for topology in materials: noncollinear three center bonds, which are the source of Berry curvature in solids. Bond fluctuation can be directly linked to electromagnetic response by resolving their energy spectrum, opening a quantitative route towards the design of physical properties of materials from bonding motifs.
