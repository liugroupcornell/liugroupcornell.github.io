Transport in multilayer graphene: resistance from stacking faults

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-10-16 20:00 EDT

C. M. Wilson, R. Ganesh, K. V. Samokhin

Multilayer graphene exhibits rich possibilities for stacking. While natural bulk graphite predominantly occurs in the Bernal structure, where layers are stacked in an $ -ABAB-$ pattern, rhombohedral stacking has sprung to prominence recently. It is well known that Bernal graphite exhibits anisotropic transport, with high resistance to currents along the stacking axis. Careful experiments using pillar geometries have measured out-of-plane resistance, attributing it to stacking faults where planes slip out of the nominal $ ABAB$ sequence. The observed resistance shows anomalously large, non-Gaussian, sample-to-sample variations. In this article, we build a theoretical framework to calculate $ c$ -axis resistance of graphene stacks with an arbitrary stacking arrangement. Our approach builds on the idea that, regardless of stacking sequence, we have in-plane translational symmetry. With in-plane momentum as a good quantum number, $ c$ -axis transport reduces to an effectively one-dimensional problem that can be addressed using the Landauer paradigm. We calculate the resistances of a variety of simple stacking faults in both Bernal and rhombohedral systems. We also calculate the resistance of a rhombohedral ‘slab’ embedded between Bernal leads. Remarkably, despite both Bernal and rhombohedral graphite being metallic, the system exhibits insulating behaviour. We attribute this to Fermi surface mismatch. We next calculate the resistance of a multilayer sample with stacking disorder. Resistance is non-self-averaging, akin to a one-dimensional wire where disorder has a strong localizing effect. The median resistance grows exponentially with stack size, indicating insulating character driven by localization. Our results demonstrate that transport measurements on mesoscopic graphitic pillars serve as signatures of stacking faults, and in particular, probe localization-driven physics.

All-temperature superfluidity in two dimensions

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-10-16 20:00 EDT

Bilal Hawashin, Soumyadeep Chaudhuri, Igor F. Herbut, Michael M. Scherer

In local theories with short-range interactions, continuous symmetries cannot be spontaneously broken in two spatial dimensions at nonzero temperature. $ \mathrm{U}(1)$ -symmetric models instead admit a Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless (BKT) phase with quasi-long-range order and nonzero phase stiffness, which is normally destroyed at high temperatures by vortex proliferation. Here, we show that a BKT phase, and with it superfluidity, can persist to arbitrarily high temperatures. Using the large-$ N$ expansion, we study a $ (2+1)$ -dimensional quantum field theory in which a complex scalar is coupled to the critical $ \mathrm{O}(N)$ model. At the biconical fixed point, where the inter-sector coupling is negative, the thermal effective potential has a circle of minima with nonzero $ \mathrm{U}(1)$ amplitude. Integrating out the gapped fluctuations yields an effective theory for the compact phase mode at distances large compared to $ 1/T$ . Since temperature is the only scale at the fixed point, the phase stiffness grows linearly with temperature, with a proportionality factor that scales linearly with $ N$ and is fixed entirely by the fixed point. This renders vortex operators irrelevant for sufficiently large $ N$ . We further show that the vortex fugacity is exponentially small in $ N$ .

Long-lived local coherences at high temperature from frozen domain walls

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-10-16 20:00 EDT

Sarang Gopalakrishnan, Romain Vasseur

Local coherences between distinct charge sectors–measured by the single-particle Green’s function or the transverse spin autocorrelation function–do not overlap with hydrodynamic modes. At finite temperature, therefore, they decay faster than any power law in generic chaotic systems. We present a mechanism by which these coherences can persist anomalously long, for integrable and chaotic spin chains (including the XXZ and Fredkin spin chains) that obey a quantum group symmetry. We show–using a Mazur bound derived from the quantum-group symmetry, together with locality and a “non-undershoot” assumption–that the transverse spin autocorrelation function $ C(t) \equiv \langle \sigma^-_x(t) \sigma^+_x(0)\rangle$ can decay at most algebraically: $ C(t) \gtrsim 1/t^2$ . This bound is not tight: in the large-anisotropy limit of the XXZ spin chain, at infinite temperature, we explicitly compute $ C(t) \sim \sqrt{3}/(18 \sqrt{t})$ . We also compute this decay rate in the XXZ spin chain for general easy-axis anisotropy, and estimate it to scale as $ C(t) \sim t^{-2/z}$ (for transport exponent $ z$ ) in the chaotic models.

Quantum oscillations in a solvable model of a Kondo insulator

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-10-16 20:00 EDT

Abhisek Samanta, Mohit Randeria, Sumilan Banerjee

We study the effects of interactions on the low-energy density of states (SdH) and magnetization (dHvA) quantum oscillations (QOs) in a Kondo insulator. Toward this end we introduce a generalization of the periodic Anderson model with Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) interactions at each site that is exactly solvable in the large-$ N$ limit, where $ N$ is the number of flavors of the heavy $ f$ electrons. We show analytically and numerically that the insulating state remains stable in terms of spectrum, thermodynamics, and transport at low temperatures even for interaction strengths much larger than non-interacting band gap of the hybridization-gap insulator. We analytically derive generalizations of Lifshitz-Kosevich (LK) formulae, distinct for the SdH and dHvA QOs, for the interacting insulator. We find that the SdH oscillation frequencies, $ F_0\pm \delta F$ , corresponding to the conduction and valence band edges, can be weakly renormalized by interaction relative to their non-interacting values. In contrast, dHvA QO frequency $ F_0$ is not renormalized by interactions, and remains the same as that for the non-interacting unhybridized bands. The QO amplitudes show a remarkable difference: the dHvA amplitude is essentially unaffected by interactions, while the SdH amplitude is strongly enhanced due to gap renormalization.

Resonant enhancement of Raman scattering in a MoS$_2$ nanotube

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-10-16 20:00 EDT

D.R. Kazanov, A.V. Poshakinskiy

The whispering-gallery modes confined in the wall of a multiwall MoS$ 2$ nanotube resonantly enhance the spontaneous Raman scattering by its own optical phonons. The enhancement is doubly resonant — pump and Stokes waves hit two adjacent modes at one pump energy, and the signal scales as $ (Q{\rm pump}Q_{\rm sc})^2$ - whenever a phonon energy matches the free spectral range of the mode ladder. Near the A exciton the excitonic dispersion compresses the ladder and sweeps its spacing through all intralayer phonon energies of MoS$ 2$ , so the match, reached for $ A{1g}$ at $ E_{\rm pump}=1551$ ~meV, requires no geometric tuning. A missed match splits the excitation peak into a pump- and a scattered-resonant doublet whose splitting measures the residual detuning. Selection rules confine the double resonance to the TM$ \to$ TM channel and exclude $ E_{1g}$ .

However, their use remains limited due to complex multi-software workflows, interdependent physical parameters, and substantial theoretical expertise. Large language model (LLM) agents, which couple a language model to software tools, show potential in automating computational tasks, but MBPT remains an unaddressed challenge. We show that LLM agents tested in our work, including frontier models such as Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5, do not reliably construct MBPT workflows in one attempt, suggesting that correct setup requires expert knowledge and consistency that current models lack. With AI-Donkey, all tested agents, from frontier proprietary models to open-weight models, produce complete, runnable MBPT workflows with no observed run-to-run variation. We further demonstrate autonomous calculations and post-processing on 1D, 2D, and bulk materials, including the many-body enhancement of Fröhlich electron-phonon coupling in polar semiconductors. Our approach provides a general framework for knowledge engineering to enable reliable agentic workflows in computational materials science.

Modeling Transport and Mechanics in Anion Exchange Membranes to Optimize Continuous CO2 Separations

New Submission | Soft Condensed Matter (cond-mat.soft) | 2026-10-16 20:00 EDT

Samaneh Bayati, Jennifer Wade, Sara Sarbaz, Heidi Feigenbaum, Horacio Lopez-Marques, Winston Wang

Water transport in charged polymer membranes is strongly influenced by hydration-dependent transport properties and polymer-network deformation. Under water-activity gradients, spatial variations in water concentration induce swelling and mechanical stresses, while the resulting deformation can influence water transport. Capturing this bidirectional interaction is important for predicting membrane behavior under nonuniform hydration conditions. This study develops a transient finite element model of water transport and mechanical deformation in a Fumasep FAA-3-50 anion exchange membrane. The framework incorporates concentration-dependent water diffusivity, hygroscopic swelling, and nonlinear viscoelastic polymer response. Uncoupled, one-way coupled, and fully coupled formulations are compared to distinguish the effects of hydration-dependent diffusivity and mechanical coupling. In the fully coupled model, local water concentration governs swelling and mechanical response, while deformation, evolving membrane geometry, and mechanical pressure feed back into water transport. Simulations predict spatially and temporally nonuniform swelling and deformation that track the evolving water-concentration field. Relative to the uncoupled concentration-dependent diffusion model, mechanical feedback alters the predicted transport response, although transport remains primarily diffusion-driven. The fully coupled model is validated against steady-state water-transport measurements across water-activity gradients and reproduces the experimentally observed hydration dependence, with strongest agreement at intermediate hydration levels. These results highlight the importance of coupled hydration, swelling, deformation, and transport processes in models of hydrated anion exchange membranes and provide a framework for evaluating membrane behavior in moisture-facilitated transport applications.

Parafermions in polarized $ν=2/3$ Fractional Quantum (Anomalous) Hall Heterostructures

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-10-16 20:00 EDT

Steffen Bollmann, Andreas Haller, Thomas L. Schmidt, Jukka I. Väyrynen, Elio J. König

Motivated by recent experimental signatures of fractional Chern insulators in 2D quantum materials, we investigate the realization of $ \mathbb Z_3$ parafermion zero modes (PZMs) on edges of spin-polarized $ \nu=2/3$ fractional quantum Hall states which are proximitized by superconductors. Unlike the spin-unpolarized Halperin state, the polarized $ \nu=2/3$ edge contains counterpropagating electron and fractional quasihole modes, giving rise to a plethora of competing tunneling and pairing processes. We focus on the vicinity of the fixed point where these modes reorganize into decoupled charge and neutral sectors, with the neutral sector describing an emergent dipole degree of freedom. Using bosonization and renormalization group analysis, we lay out the parameter space in which the interedge dipole interactions gap the neutral sector to stabilize one dominant tunneling and one dominant pairing channel resulting in a double sine-Gordon model in the charge sector which supports $ \mathbb{Z}_3$ PZMs at domain walls. We discuss both clean and disordered systems. In clean systems this mechanism requires a certain degree of control of the interaction strength and range. In contrast, disorder can provide a more generic route: While short-ranged, edge-uncorrelated disorder % on the two edges suppresses the simultaneous pinning required for stable parafermion modes, when the disorder on the two edges is sufficiently correlated, as expected for smooth disorder potentials, the parameter regime hosting PZMs increases.

Band-Resolved Superconducting Gaps via the Thermal Hall Effect in CeRu2

New Submission | Superconductivity (cond-mat.supr-con) | 2026-10-16 20:00 EDT

Darian Hall, Luke Pritchard Cairns, Nico Rowland, Yuanqi Lyu, Kohtaro Yamakawa, Josue Rodriguez, Kirin Chanteloup, Didier Perrodin, Joel E. Moore, James G. Analytis

Standard bulk probes of multiband superconductors cannot assign pairing gaps to specific electron or hole Fermi surfaces. Here we show that thermal Hall transport ($ \kappa_{xy}$ ), constrained by specific heat and normal-state magnetotransport, resolves this ambiguity in the compensated heavy-fermion superconductor CeRu$ 2$ . Fixing the gap magnitudes ($ \Delta_e/k_B\approx 13$ K, $ \Delta_h/k_B\approx 8$ K) and band mobilities completely determines the electronic degrees of freedom in $ \kappa{xx}$ and $ \kappa_{xy}$ . A low-field sign reversal in $ \kappa_{xy}$ places the larger gap on the heavy Ce $ 4f$ electron sheets and the smaller on the hole sheets.
