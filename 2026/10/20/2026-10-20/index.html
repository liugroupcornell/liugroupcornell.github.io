Supramolecular chemical ligation of helical polymers

Original Paper | Supramolecular polymers | 2026-10-19 20:00 EDT

Minyi Ma, Zimai Jiang, Na Li, Yifei Xu, Da-Hui Qu, He Tian, Ben L. Feringa, Qi Zhang

Chemical ligation of polymer segments is an essential approach to extend the length of synthetic polymers. Ligating individual polymer chains at the molecular level remains a formidable challenge owing to the entropically disfavoured collision probability between reactive end groups, and the need to overcome the activation barrier. Here we report a pathway to chemical ligation of synthetic polymers via folding-driven supramolecular polymerization followed by dynamic covalent polycondensation. Our strategy, which does not require thermal activation, uses hydrogen bonds that induce conformational folding to rigidify the polymer backbone while providing sticky ends for supramolecular pre-organization. This promotes base-catalysed covalent exchange between terminal groups (in our case, thiolate-disulfide groups). We obtain mechanistic insight in the ligation process by engineering side-chain units that determine conformational foldability and intermolecular H-bonding pre-organization. The resulting extended helical polymers fold into hierarchical globular architectures in solution and exhibit thermo-responsive macromolecular entanglements in bulk films. The combination of noncovalent assembly and dynamic covalent polymerization offers a supramolecular pathway for non-thermal and controllable polycondensation of dynamic polymers with secondary structures.

Stacking-specific two-dimensional In2Se3 for ferroelectric homojunctions

Original Paper | Two-dimensional materials | 2026-10-19 20:00 EDT

Xiaodong Zheng, Tong Yang, Xiaoyu He, Wen Zhang, Chi Shing Tsang, Yuhuan Li, Lok Wing Wong, Zhangyuan Yan, Jiafeng Zhang, Tianren Chen, Honglin Chen, Runmeng Jia, Chun-Sing Lee, Shu Ping Lau, Shijian Zheng, Jiong Zhao, Ming Yang, Thuc Hue Ly

Two-dimensional (2D) ferroelectric semiconductors offer unique opportunities for ultrathin non-volatile memory and neuromorphic devices, yet the role of stacking order in intrinsic ferroelectric systems remains largely unexplored. Here we report the stacking-controlled growth of large-area 2D β’- and α-In2Se3 with distinct 2H and 3R configurations through chemical vapour deposition by regulating selenium kinetics and strain relaxation. By combining atomic-resolution microscopy, non-linear optical characterization and first-principles calculations, we uncover a strong correlation between stacking order and ferroelectric behaviour: β’-In2Se3 undergoes an antiferroelectric-to-paraelectric transition when switching from 2H to 3R stacking, whereas in α-In2Se3, in-plane ferroelectric polarization vanishes during the 3R-to-2H transition. We further achieve the controlled synthesis of 2H/3R ferroelectric homojunctions and directly visualize polarization-dependent interfacial potential barriers using differential phase-contrast scanning transmission electron microscopy. The engineered homojunctions exhibit switchable band alignment and tunable carrier transport, yielding memristors with significantly reduced switching voltages and an on/off ratio of ∼2.6 × 104, approximately one order of magnitude higher than devices based on single-stacking α-In2Se3. These findings establish stacking order as a powerful structural parameter for tailoring ferroelectricity in 2D materials and introduce stacking-engineered polymorphic heterogeneity as a versatile platform for high-performance ferroelectric electronics and memory technologies.

Fibres and glasses are competing material states in protein condensation

Original Paper | Glasses | 2026-10-19 20:00 EDT

Liru Feng, Ryota Takaki, Iain Muntz, Lionel Ndamba, Martine Ruer-Gruß, Frank Jülicher, Louise Jawerth

Dense, well-ordered material states of proteins form the amyloid fibres that are indicative of neurodegenerative disease in the brain. In addition to forming amyloid fibres, some of these proteins comprise condensates that are initially liquid-like but evolve to a glassy, solid-like phase. Fibre growth requires a large supply of monomers, and so it is often speculated that fibres emerge from within a dense condensate as it ages and its microscopic dynamics slow into a glassy state. Here we show that, in the model system Fused in Sarcoma (FUS), fibres grow overwhelmingly in the dilute phase surrounding the condensates; concurrently, the condensates evolve to a glassy arrested solid. The resulting protein fibres and glassy condensates are both distinct solid-like phases that coexist but do not directly interconvert. These findings suggest that there may be two competitive ageing pathways in FUS condensation that are linked through phase separation kinetics.

High-temperature cuprate superconductors remain a key platform for studying the competition and coexistence of emergent quantum phases of matter. These include non-s-wave superconductivity with a high transition temperature, non-Fermi-liquid transport and the pseudogap regime. Universal electrical resistivity in iron-based high-temperature superconductors

New Submission | Superconductivity (cond-mat.supr-con) | 2026-10-20 20:00 EDT

E.F. Talantsev

Temperature-dependent electrical resistivity $ \rho(T)$ in cuprate, iron-based, and nickelate superconductors represents one of unresolved problems in modern solid-state physics, because of the high absolute value of $ \rho(T)$ , which, in many cases, exceeds the Ioffe-Rigel criterion, and a large difference in the $ \rho(T)$ shape for samples with different doping. Here I show that the nearest neighbors parallel resistivity model (arXiv: 2607.23484) can well describe the in-plane resistivity $ \rho_{ab}(T)$ in iron-based superconductors (IBS) of type 11, 111, 122, 1111, 1144 and 42214 in which the spin density wave phase or nematic order is suppressed by doping. The model is based on the postulate that $ \rho_{ab}(T)$ is a sum of two parallel conduction channels, where one channel is described by the Bloch-Grüneisen equation and the other by the Arrhenius hopping equation. Consequently, the variety of $ \rho_{ab}(T)$ in the IBS is explained as a variation of the Debye temperature $ \Theta_D$ and the hopping activation energy $ E_a$ . The derived Debye temperatures were used to estimate the electron-phonon coupling constant $ \lambda_{e-ph}$ in the studied IBS.

Resonant suppression of the $π$-type Josephson component in a Kitaev-ladder junction

New Submission | Superconductivity (cond-mat.supr-con) | 2026-10-20 20:00 EDT

Cheng-Rong Xie, Hiroki Tsuchiura, Yukio Tanaka

We investigate Josephson transport through a finite normal segment connecting two Kitaev-ladder superconductors and show that discrete-level detuning controls the sign of the equilibrium Josephson coupling through a branch-selection mechanism. Away from resonance, detuning separates two competing low-energy branches, allowing inter-leg hybridization to clamp the $ 0$ -phase-favoring branch while leaving the $ \pi$ -favoring branch dispersive. The resulting negative first harmonic evolves from a branch-switching response at weak coupling into a smooth $ \pi$ -junction regime. At resonance the two clamping thresholds coincide, so the finite interval in which only one branch remains dispersive disappears. Once the inter-leg coupling exceeds the resonant clamping threshold, both low-energy branches become independent of the Josephson phase. The remaining finite-energy states then generate a $ 0$ -type Josephson coupling. At $ \phi=\pi$ , the helicity decomposition yields an exact clamping identity for arbitrary finite normal-segment length. Within the controlled sweet-point finite-segment theory, serial propagation fixes the off-resonant fourth-order harmonic to be negative, whereas an isolated resonance leaves a positive residual harmonic within the resonant clamping regime. Numerical calculations demonstrate that the resonance-induced suppression persists away from the solvable phase choice. Thus normal-level resonance does not merely enhance Josephson transmission; it changes which low-energy branches contribute to the Josephson coupling.

Multiple propagating modes in random polycrystals

New Submission | Materials Science (cond-mat.mtrl-sci) | 2026-10-20 20:00 EDT

Ming Huang, Stanislav I. Rokhlin, Michael J. S. Lowe

Multiple-scattering theory predicts that the coherent elastic wave in a statistically isotropic polycrystal is not a single wave. Its dispersion equation admits multiple roots of a given polarization, but these extra modes have never been observed. Here, using grain-scale finite-element simulations of three-dimensional polycrystals, we resolve the coherent field in the frequency–wavenumber plane and directly observe the predicted multiplicity for both transverse and longitudinal excitations: a single mode at low frequencies gives way to two in the geometric regime as the wavelength approaches the grain correlation length. The measured modes follow the roots of the dispersion equation, which always carries a third root whose attenuation exceeds that of the others. It is this attenuation hierarchy, not the root count, that dictates how many modes propagate and can be observed. Coherent wave propagation in random media is thus inherently multimodal in the geometric regime, which we reach here for the first time in polycrystals.

Quantifying the Kinetic and Thermodynamic Origins of the Glass-Forming Ability in Metals

New Submission | Disordered Systems and Neural Networks (cond-mat.dis-nn) | 2026-10-20 20:00 EDT

Weiwei Jin, Jinpeng Fan, Mark D. Shattuck, Corey S. O’Hern

The glass-forming ability (GFA) of a metallic liquid can be quantified by the critical cooling rate $ R_c$ , below which it crystallizes upon cooling. $ R_c$ is determined by the competition between $ R$ and the crystallization rate $ \mathcal{K}{\rm X}$ . Here, we calculate $ R_c$ and $ \mathcal{K}{\rm X}$ in model pure metals and alloys with BCC/B2 and FCC equilibrium crystal structures. Using classical nucleation theory and independently calculated crystal growth rates, we evaluate $ \mathcal{K}{\rm X}=(IU^3)^{1/4}$ at $ T=0.8T_m$ , where $ I$ and $ U$ are the nucleation and growth rates and $ T_m$ is the melting temperature. We find a strong positive correlation between $ \mathcal{K}{\rm X}$ and $ R_c$ over eleven orders of magnitude. We decompose $ \mathcal{K}{\rm X}$ into a product of a kinetic contribution, involving atomic cluster attachment and diffusion, and a thermodynamic contribution. We show that the thermodynamic contribution to $ \mathcal{K}{\rm X}$ dominates the kinetic contribution over the range of cooling rates that we consider. The nucleation barrier $ \Delta G_c \sim \tilde\gamma^3/\Delta\tilde\mu^2$ , where $ \tilde\gamma$ is the reduced effective liquid–crystal interfacial free energy and $ \Delta\tilde\mu$ is the reduced bulk driving force, determines the magnitude of the thermodynamic contribution. The relative importance of $ \tilde\gamma$ and $ \Delta\tilde\mu$ depends on the material: a large interfacial free energy can suppress nucleation in some systems, whereas a weak bulk driving force can inhibit crystallization in others. Analyses of the structural and chemical order show that better glass formers have enhanced structural frustration, more icosahedral-like and less crystal-like local environments, and a larger chemical-order mismatch between the supercooled liquid and crystal phases.

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-10-20 20:00 EDT

Noah Reich, Richard Kim, Thomas Koschny, Jigang Wang

Terahertz scanning near-field optical microscopy (THz-SNOM) enables spatially and spectrally resolved investigation of low-energy collective and topological states in quantum materials and nano-structured devices by exploiting the strongly localized near field generated by a polarized atomic force microscopy (AFM) tip. Because the THz near-field response is highly sensitive to tip geometry, understanding how geometric parameters control field confinement and decay is essential for selecting tip designs that provide the spatial resolution required for specific experiments. In this work, we develop a boundary element method (BEM) for modeling the electrostatic near-field of a conducting AFM tip in response to an external background field. The tip is represented by a simplified closed, blunt-cone geometry with its surface discretized into panels using increased mesh density near the apex. A modified Green’s function is used to calculate interactions between surface panels, while an augmented linear system simultaneously enforces the equipotential boundary condition on the conducting surface and overall charge neutrality to determine the induced surface-charge distribution. We validate the BEM by comparing the resulting near-field decay with that obtained from a three-dimensional quasi-static COMSOL Multiphysics simulation. The BEM accurately reproduces the COMSOL field-decay behavior while requiring orders of magnitude fewer mesh elements and substantially reducing computational cost. These results demonstrate that the BEM provides an accurate and computationally efficient framework for systematically investigating the dependence of THz near-field confinement on AFM tip geometry. This framework can be extended to more complex and experimentally realistic tip geometries, providing a route toward rational tip selection and design for application-specific spatial-resolution requirements.

Proximity Transient Absorption in van der Waals Heterostructures

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-10-20 20:00 EDT

Neema Rafizadeh, Ting Zheng, Hui Zhao

Transient absorption spectroscopy is a widely used technique for investigating ultrafast electronic dynamics, where transient optical signals are generally interpreted as arising from excitations within the material hosting the probed optical transition. A distinct form of transient absorption spectroscopy, termed proximity transient absorption (PTA), is demonstrated, in which the probed optical transition and the monitored photoexcited carriers reside in different materials. Using graphene/hBN/WS$ _2$ heterostructures, photoexcited carriers in graphene induce a strong transient optical response at the excitonic resonance of a WS$ _2$ monolayer through Coulomb screening, even when the two layers remain electronically isolated by an atomically thin hBN spacer. The resulting signal follows the carrier dynamics of graphene rather than those of WS$ _2$ , demonstrating that the WS$ _2$ exciton acts as an optical transducer of electronic processes in a neighboring material. The signal decreases systematically with increasing hBN thickness, confirming its proximity origin while remaining substantial for separations approaching 2 nm. PTA provides a noncontact approach for optically modulating one material using photoexcited carriers in another and enables probing of carrier dynamics in materials that are otherwise difficult to investigate directly. More broadly, PTA establishes a new modality of ultrafast spectroscopy and reveals that transient optical signals in van der Waals heterostructures can originate from excitations residing in spatially separated layers.

Collective diffusion of anisotropic hard-particle mixtures

New Submission | Soft Condensed Matter (cond-mat.soft) | 2026-10-20 20:00 EDT

Anouar El Moumane, G. H. Philipp Nguyen, Klaus Mecke, Hartmut Löwen, René Wittmann

Predicting the dynamics of anisotropic mixtures of different species is a challenging theoretical task. We show that Dynamical Density Functional Theory (DDFT), a theory predicting time evolution of the one-particle-density through a free energy functional, can be simplified to a diffusion equation. To quantify the dynamical relaxation behavior, we deduce corrections to the regular long-time collective diffusion coefficients from the direct correlations between the interacting particles and determine these effective diffusion coefficients analytically for a number of hard-particle systems, including mixtures of hard spheres, as well as isotropic and nematic phases of uniaxial spherocylinders and biaxial spherotriangles. Finally, we discuss how to generalize our approach to systems with positional order and suggest a possible application to predicting relaxation times of orientational order parameters.

Phase-sensitive quantum-state texture during thermalization of a Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya Heisenberg dimer

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-10-20 20:00 EDT

Onofre Rojas, Zhirayr Adamyan, Fadwa Benabdallah, Vadim Ohanyan

We investigate quantum-state texture during the thermal relaxation of an anisotropic spin-$ 1/2$ Heisenberg dimer subject to a longitudinal magnetic field and a Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya (DM) interaction. Starting from local transverse and longitudinal couplings to a bosonic bath, we construct a global weak-coupling Lindblad equation, identify its transition channels, and discuss the applicability and limitations of the secular approximation. The Gibbs state is obtained explicitly in the local spin basis, revealing that the DM coupling controls both the modulus and phase of the single-excitation coherence by modifying the energy splitting and rotating the chiral phase. For heating and cooling protocols, we compare rugosity, fidelity-based texture, and trace-distance texture with coherence, vector spin chirality, and purity. The relaxation dynamics separates population thermalization from the decay and precession of deliberately prepared energy-basis coherence. Their combined contribution to local coherence produces DM-dependent texture overshoots and, during cooling, damped oscillations and sign changes of the coherence quadratures. These transients differ from the evolution of coherence magnitude and purity, while trace-distance texture weights them differently from the overlap-based quantifiers. Calculations at reduced dissipation preserve the texture maxima within the secular model. The results identify a phase-sensitive diagnostic of relaxation.

A McLennan formula for equiaccessible steady states

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-10-20 20:00 EDT

Jacob Calvert

Near equilibrium, the Boltzmann distribution is corrected by the transient irreversible entropy flux, a result known as the McLennan formula. By expanding around equiaccessible steady states instead, we derive an analogue that can hold arbitrarily far from equilibrium. At zeroth order, steady-state occupation is determined solely by local escape rates, yielding a strong form of the active-matter principle of “low rattling.” The first-order correction measures the impact of traffic imbalances on the accessibility of each state, marking the onset of nonlocal determinants of relative occupation.

Capillary forces between two identical spherical particles

New Submission | Soft Condensed Matter (cond-mat.soft) | 2026-10-20 20:00 EDT

Stefano Onofri, Thejas Hulikal Chakrapani, Stefan Luding, Elke Scholten, Ahmed Jarray, Wouter K. den Otter

The properties of colloidal suspensions and granular matter change drastically upon adding a small amount of liquid capable of forming capillary bridges between the solid particles. The attractive forces exerted by these liquid bridges induce the formation of particle aggregates and networks, which radically changes the macroscopic mechanical and rheological behaviour. Predicting these forces across bridge sizes, particle separations and wettability is therefore central to particle-scale modelling of capillary suspensions and wet granular matter. While the Young-Laplace equation governing the properties of the liquid bridges is well known, it lacks a convenient analytical solution. Current simulations therefore employ approximate analytic expressions, typically developed for contact angles below ~40° and bridge volumes below ~2.5% of the solid particle’s volume. We compare several frequently used expressions from the literature with numerical solutions of the theoretical capillary forces. An overall good agreement is observed in the aforementioned parameter ranges, while the approximate expressions start to deviate outside their intended domains. We propose accurate and computationally efficient expressions for the capillary force that model this behaviour, for contact angles from 5° to 80° and bridge volumes from $ 10^{-6}$ to 0.15. We also explore mesoscopic ‘fluid-scale’ simulations of bridges by Multi-body Dissipative Particle Dynamics (MDPD), to ascertain whether this coarse-grained fluid model faithfully captures the continuum capillary forces. Remarkably, a good agreement is observed even for liquid bridges described by a mere few dozen fluid particles. Together, these results offer two approaches, at different resolution scales, for simulating bridges over a wide range of bridge volumes and contact angles.

Kondo Lattice model for field induced triplet pairing in CeRh$_2$As$_2$

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-10-20 20:00 EDT

Indra Gankhuyag, Piers Coleman

Spin triplet superconductors are of interest because of their topological properties, and their robustness to high magnetic fields. CeRh$ _2$ As$ _2$ is a unique heavy fermion superconductor with both even-parity and candidate odd-parity superconducting phases with a special feature enabling a field induced phase transition between the two. The field induced transition which switches the parity of the Cooper pairs is enabled by a staggered antisymmetric spin orbit coupling (ASOC) interaction. However, the heavy fermion origins of the superconductivity in CeRh$ _2$ As$ _2$ and the origin of the antisymmetric spin-orbit coupling are currently unexplored. Here, we develop a Kondo lattice model for CeRh$ _2$ As$ _2$ and demonstrate how local hybridization between Cerium $ d$ - and $ f$ -orbitals, driven by the locally broken inversion symmetry, at the Cerium sites generates the ASOC. The resulting Kondo lattice model leads to nearly degenerate even and odd parity superconducting states that display a field induced parity transition between an even-parity state and an odd-parity pseudospin triplet state. We discuss the wider implications of our results extended to other heavy fermion systems like CeSiI.

Boundary-Sensitive Quantum Order on Non-Amenable Lattices

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-10-20 20:00 EDT

Xingzhi Wang, Zohar Nussinov, Gerardo Ortiz

We show that quantum systems on non-amenable lattices, whose boundaries constitute an extensive fraction of the system, host novel forms of bulk ordering with no Euclidean counterpart. Combining boundary-retaining iDMRG with an exact symmetry-fixing duality, we investigate $ p<5$ state clock models on the coordination-3 infinite Cayley tree. We find that between the ordered and disordered phases lies an emergent intermediate phase with bulk order induced by an arbitrarily weak boundary field rather than by conventional spontaneous symmetry-breaking. This establishes boundary-sensitive order as a distinct quantum ordering mechanism defying Landau’s spontaneous symmetry-breaking paradigm. The intermediate phase realizes a bulk-boundary correspondence in which the unpolarized boundary selects a specific ordered bulk state.

On Higher-Form Fracton Hydrodynamics with Momentum Conservation

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-10-20 20:00 EDT

Ben Bobell, Rahul Nandkishore, Andrew Lucas

Momentum conservation is common in fluids, yet it may be incompatible with the hydrodynamics of theories with exotic conservation laws (such as fracton hydrodynamics) unless symmetries are spontaneously broken. Using the Martin-Siggia-Rose (MSR) effective field theory formalism, we develop a representation-theoretic framework for the hydrodynamics of translationally invariant systems with exotic conservation laws without spontaneous symmetry breaking. Under mild assumptions on the form of hydrodynamics, we show that such theories must satisfy two representation theoretic consistency conditions and a generalized Gauss’s law constraint. Higher-form symmetries satisfy these conditions and we derive their ideal hydrodynamics; in three dimensions, the one-form case produces ideal non-relativistic magnetohydrodynamics. Finally, we prove that no other theories are compatible with momentum conservation in SO(3) nor SO($ d$ ), for sufficiently large odd $ d$ .

Topological-Insulator Heterophase Gate Stacks for Transistor Electrostatics

Physical Sciences - Article | Electronic devices | 2026-10-19 20:00 EDT

Hoon Hahn Yoon, Minuk Song, Jiwan Kim, MD Gius Uddin, Wonseok Kim, Jihun Park, Han Uk Lee, Dong Won Jeon, Dohyung Lee, Lide Yao, Jouko Lahtinen, Gyunghyun Jang, Soohyun Min, Yonas Tsegaye Megra, Xiaoqi Cui, Seungwoo Choi, Yunyun Dai, Sang Hoon Chae, Keun Su Kim, Dong-Ho Kang, Hyeon-Jin Shin, Wooseok Song, Seth Ariel Tongay, Chul-Ho Lee, Manish Chhowalla, Sung Beom Cho, Zhipei Sun, Kibog Park

Conventional gate-stack scaling reduces dielectric thickness and increases permittivity while largely treating the position and electronic character of the gate-side screening boundary as fixed. Here we show that this boundary can be engineered by converting the surface of the topological insulator Bi2Se3 into insulating high-κ BiF3. Position-resolved calculations reveal a gap-opened immediate amorphous-BiF3/crystalline-Bi2Se3 interface and a reconstructed gap-closed Bi2Se3-derived state in the adjacent subinterface layer, accompanied by a localized interfacial dipole. Capacitor measurements independently resolve a finite series response consistent with electronic compressibility at this boundary; this response lowers the nominal stack capacitance. Despite this capacitance penalty, MoS2 transistors with closely matched BiF3 thicknesses and a common BiF3/MoS2 channel-side material interface exhibit near-thermionic-limit switching, negligible hysteresis and substantially weaker subthreshold drain-bias dependence than BiF3-only controls. These results identify the position and electronic character of the gate-side screening boundary as additional design variables for transistor electrostatics beyond nominal dielectric capacitance.

Quantum oblique eigenprojection

Article | Computer science | 2026-10-19 20:00 EDT

Yuan Su, Alexander Dalzell

Every square matrix decomposes its underlying Hilbert space into generalized, nonorthogonal eigensubspaces. We show that a quantum computer can perform such an oblique eigenprojection Π given block encoding access to the input matrix. Our approach has a query complexity nearly linear in the inverse gap and a normalization factor close to ||Π|| under a spectral-set condition on the input. This covers common assumptions on the numerical range or diagonalizability and matches known results for orthogonal eigenprojections. We achieve this with a two-sided block preconditioning that uses a discrete Fourier transform of the matrix resolvent. As the oblique eigenprojection serves as a fundamental primitive in the design of quantum algorithms, our result can be used to extend and improve a variety of prior results. Specifically, we describe applications to: (i) preparing eigenstates of matrices with complex eigenvalues, extending the quantum eigenvalue transformation algorithm of Low and Su beyond real spectra; (ii) solving continuous-time algebraic Riccati equations, cubically speeding up a prior solver of Rodenas-Ruiz, Zhao, and Lee; and (iii) solving ordinary Sylvester equations, quadratically improving a direct augmented method of Wang and Liu. Our result suggests a promising route to applying nonanalytic matrix functions on quantum computers.
