Three-dimensional cell morphology is mediated by curvature sensing and interfacial stress

Original Paper | Biophysics | 2026-10-25 20:00 EDT

Yi Liu, Baoce Sun, Shuhuan Hu, Ngoc Luu, Jifeng Ren, Lelin Liu, Jingqian Zhang, Yun Qu, Ruolin Zhang, Jialei Song, Mo Yang, Ao Zhou, Hanbai Wu, Dong Sun, Jinlian Hu, Denvid Lau, Weiqiang Chen, Raymond H. W. Lam

Curvature sensing of cells critically governs three-dimensional cell morphology, which is integral to tissue and organ development as well as the wound-healing processes. However, the underlying mechanism by which sensing occurs remains unclear. Here we investigate the physical principles that govern curvature sensing at both single-cell and monolayer levels. We find that the cell-substrate interfacial stress ratio–defined as the balance between the cell contractile stress and the cell-substrate adhesion stress–determines the two predominant three-dimensional cell morphology modes, namely, complete adhesion and partial interior detachment. When the interfacial stress ratio is much higher than unity, a rapid morphological shift from complete adhesion to partial interior detachment occurs. This process is irreversible, as it proceeds towards a lower-energy state. We observe this mode switching for various cell types, under pharmacological perturbations and across multicellular layers, offering a universal biophysical framework for regulating three-dimensional cell morphology.

Programmable mRNA vaccine materials for temporal control of antitumour immunity

Review Paper | Biomaterials - vaccines | 2026-10-25 20:00 EDT

Seong Dong Jeong, Chaoyang Tang, Xiaotian Wang, Antoni J. Szeglowski, Yujin Kim, B. J. Kim, Padmanee Sharma, James J. Moon, Wen Jiang, Betty Y. S. Kim

Advances in mRNA and nanomaterial design have transformed vaccine technology, enabling precise control of antigen expression, delivery kinetics and immune activation. However, several leading clinical-stage mRNA cancer vaccine programmes are being evaluated in the adjuvant setting, in which loss of the primary tumour antigen reservoir and evolving postoperative immune states may alter the conditions for effective priming. Here, we present a materials-driven framework for temporal immunoengineering, in which programmable mRNA vaccine materials are designed to align antigen availability, innate sensing and adaptive priming with changing immune states across the perioperative window. Using the perioperative period as an illustrative model of immune flux, we discuss how distinct mRNA nanocarrier architectures can be engineered to tune the timing, magnitude and anatomical context of innate activation, antigen expression and adaptive priming. A universal neoadjuvant mRNA ‘prime’, formulated in an immunostimulatory nanocarrier, could condition innate and myeloid compartments and remodel the tumour microenvironment, whereas a personalized adjuvant ‘boost’, delivered in translationally efficient formulations, could reinforce clonal expansion and memory of tumour-specific T cells. By treating biomaterials as programmable regulators of immunodynamics rather than passive carriers, this Perspective establishes temporal control as a complementary design axis for next-generation mRNA vaccine systems.

Lattice Symmetry Remnants in Spatial and Temporal Entanglement of Quantum Critical Spin Chains

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-10-26 20:00 EDT

Xiao-Han Yang, Rui-Zhen Huang

Quantum critical lattice models carry universal information beyond the bulk data of their infrared conformal field theory (CFT): microscopic lattice symmetries constrain how the CFT is realized. We probe these remnants through the spatial and temporal entanglement of two Euclidean-evolved states from the same critical model. In the finite-length scaling regime, a conformal boundary encodes the long-distance effect of the short-range initial state, and boundary CFT (BCFT) organizes the universal low-lying entanglement spectrum. We formulate the temporal state as a uniform infinite matrix product state evolved by a symmetry-preserving infinite matrix product operator, using a local factorization regular even at arbitrarily small Trotter steps. For the Ising and three-state Potts chains, whose relevant symmetries are onsite, the spatial and temporal constructions approach the same BCFT structure; varying the initial state engineers conformal boundaries distinguishable spectroscopically. The spin-1/2 XXZ chain instead realizes a Lieb–Schultz–Mattis (LSM) constraint between internal U(1) symmetry and one-site translation. A U(1)-preserving short-range spatial state accommodates it through a doubled unit cell and inequivalent entanglement cuts, whereas the temporal state remains uniform along Euclidean time. Successive spatial transfer steps pump the temporal entanglement spectrum between translation-related Dirichlet sectors, equivalently between integer and half-integer compact-boson zero modes. Breaking U(1) removes both the zero mode and the alternation. Continued evolution at fixed bond dimension crosses over from the finite-length BCFT regime to a finite-entanglement regime generated by symmetry-allowed relevant deformations. Thus microscopic symmetry and anomaly data remain visible in universal entanglement by constraining the boundary realization of the infrared CFT.

Altermagnetism in Folded Iron Chain Systems

New Submission | Materials Science (cond-mat.mtrl-sci) | 2026-10-26 20:00 EDT

Ling-Fang Lin, Yang Zhang, Satoshi Okamoto, Adriana Moreo, Lun-Hui Hu, Elbio Dagotto

Altermagnetism, a moment-compensated magnetism with momentum-dependent spin splitting, has so far been realized primarily in Néel-type antiferromagnets. Here, we demonstrate altermagnetism in folded iron-chain systems with block-type magnetic order (e.g., $ \uparrow \uparrow \downarrow \downarrow$ ). In the strictly one-dimensional (1D) case, altermagnetism is forbidden by the $ [C_2||{\cal T}]$ symmetry, but it can emerge when such chains are stacked into a quasi-1D array. To generate altermagnetic spin splitting via this chain-stacking mechanism, two essential ingredients are required: a block-type spin order that spontaneously breaks the combined parity-time reversal symmetry, and finite inter-chain hopping. Using first-principles calculations, we identify the K$ _3$ Fe$ _2$ Se$ 4$ family as a promising platform that realizes this mechanism, hosting a $ d$ -wave altermagnet even without lattice $ C{4}$ symmetry. Moreover, owing to its lower symmetry, the block-type folded chain exhibits an isotropic altermagnetic spin splitting, which may manifest as an altermagnetic wrapping effect. Our findings unify real-space block magnetism and reciprocal-space altermagnetism, establishing folded iron chains as a tunable platform for zero-moment spintronic applications.

Low-energy collective electronic excitations in a gold monolayer

New Submission | Other Condensed Matter (cond-mat.other) | 2026-10-26 20:00 EDT

Vyacheslav M. Silkin, Maxim Yu. Kagan

The low-energy plasmonic properties of a free-standing Au(111) monolayer, frequently referred to as goldene, are investigated by time-dependent density functional theory in the random phase approximation. In the evaluation of dielectric and loss functions the first-principle band structure characterized by a single $ s-p$ energy band at the Fermi level is taken into account. In this system with apparently simple band structure around the Fermi surface the calculations show the presence, besides the standard two-dimensional (2D) plasmon mode, at even lower energies of three anomalous branches in the momentum-energy region usually assigned to incoherent electron-hole pairs. This is an unexpected result since the electronic system of the gold monolayer is characterized by a single energy band crossing the Fermi level. The dispersion of these anomalous plasmonic branches is strongly anisotropic in-plane and gapless. A general feature is that at small in-plane momentum transfers the modes have a sound-like dispersion in certain momentum directions. We rely the appearance of these anomalous modes to a notable deviation of the Fermi surface of a gold monolayer from a circular shape. As a result of its almost perfect hexagon shape, there is possibility to realize in a {\it single} electronic energy band a scenario of a multi-component electron system with the carrier groups moving with different Fermi velocities in certain momentum directions. The found modes correspond to the out-of-phase charge oscillations of the carriers at different sides of the hexagonal Fermi surface. Hence our work presents evidence how in a metal system with apparently simple band structure around the Fermi level its shape can result in conversion of single-particle charge excitations into collective charge excitations.

Unifying Low-Frequency Raman and Infrared Signatures across Perovskites

New Submission | Materials Science (cond-mat.mtrl-sci) | 2026-10-26 20:00 EDT

Ethan Berger, Erik Fransson, Apinya Ngoipala, Lucas Svensson, Priyanka Deswal, Mehmet Baskurt, Paul Erhart, Julia Wiktor

Perovskites have been studied for a wide range of applications, from oxide-based ferroelectrics to halide-perovskite photovoltaics. Despite their diverse functionalities, these materials share strong lattice anharmonicity arising from structural instabilities such as polar off-centering and octahedral tilting. These anharmonic distortions strongly influence the low-frequency vibrational response and can be observed in infrared (IR) and Raman spectra. However, low-frequency IR and Raman signatures are typically investigated separately, hindering the development of a unified understanding of vibrational spectroscopy across perovskite classes. In this work, we use machine-learning-based molecular dynamics to simulate the IR and Raman spectra of oxide perovskites (SrTiO$ _3$ and BaTiO$ _3$ ), halide perovskites (CsPbBr$ _3$ , CsSnBr$ _3$ , and CsGeBr$ _3$ ) and double halide perovskites (Cs$ _2$ AgAlBr$ _6$ and Cs$ _2$ AgBiBr$ _6$ ). By comparing materials exhibiting polar, tilt-driven and mixed instabilities, we find that polar off-centering leads to enhanced low-frequency IR and dielectric responses, whereas nonpolar modes, particularly octahedral tilting, are associated with Raman central peaks. The strength of the low-frequency features also depends on how strongly the underlying modes couple to polarization or dielectric susceptibility. Finally, we show that the dynamics of these instabilities play an important role, with slow polar reorientations shifting the dielectric response to very low frequencies and overdamped tilting modes producing persistent Raman central peaks. These results establish a unified framework connecting low-frequency IR and Raman spectra to the symmetry, coupling and dynamics of their underlying anharmonic lattice instabilities.

In crossed fields, the in-plane electric field generates an effective spin-orbit drift field that modifies the Landau spectrum and spinor states, while at zero magnetic field the problem maps onto Landau–Zener spin this http URL zero external fields, the total joint density of states above the band gap remains unchanged, while characteristic subgap structures appear. Various types of electrohydrodynamic instabilities in liquid crystals (LCs) are a recurring theme in LC research. The nonlinear stage of the flexoelectric instability in nematics remains the least studied aspect of this phenomenon. This work highlights a key advancement in nonlinear physics, providing a compelling example of nonlinear dynamical system behavior. Robustness of Skyrmion-Mediated Magnetization Switching in Perpendicular Magnetic Tunnel Junctions (p-MTJs) to Material Disorder and Elevated Temperature

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-10-26 20:00 EDT

Prosenjit Das, Md Mahadi Rajib, Jayasimha Atulasimha

Transient-skyrmion mediated voltage-controlled magnetic anisotropy (VCMA) switching is a fast and energy efficient way to magnetization reversal, but its reliability under realistic material disorder and thermal fluctuations remain unclear. In this work, we use stochastic micromagnetic simulations of a 100 nm perpendicular magnetic tunnel junction to study switching reliability under grain-scale variations in Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction, perpendicular magnetic anisotropy, easy-axis orientation, and intergrain exchange coupling. We find that switching remains robust under moderate disorder, with 100% switching observed in 10^4 stochastic simulations after pulse width optimization at both 300 K and 350 K. Increasing the temperature from 300 to 350 K shifts the optimum VCMA pulse width from ~475 to ~450 ps, indicating that an apparent reduction in switching probability at a fixed pulse duration can be primarily due to pulse detuning. In contrast, strong easy-axis disorder reduces the maximum switching probability even after pulse-width optimization, indicating degradation of the switching dynamics. We further show that, under strong easy-axis disorder a transient skyrmion still forms while the subsequent post-pulse dynamics fails to complete the magnetization reversal. Our study therefore establishes the distinction between recoverable reliability due to timing shifts and permanent disorder-induced degradation that simply cannot be recovered by pulse re-tuning. Finally, for no disorders but at room temperature, 100% switching is observed with 10^6 stochastic simulations with correct pulse shaping.
