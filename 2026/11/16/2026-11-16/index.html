Architecture of a portal complex embedded in the poxvirus core

Original Paper | Virus structures | 2026-11-15 19:00 EST

Ye Hong, Shiyuan Liu, Keren Wang, Rui Liang, Junxia Zhang, Cheng Peng, Zheyuan Zhang, Zefeng Zhu, Weiping Zhang, Baoying Huang, Chen Song, Wenjie Tan, Sai Li

Gene transcription within the viral core is a distinctive feature of poxvirus replication. Following entry into the cytoplasm, the poxvirus core dissociates from the lateral bodies and undergoes expansion, functioning as a compartment for early transcription1. However, how molecules are exchanged between the viral core and the host cytoplasm remains poorly understood. Here, using cryo-electron tomography and sub-tomogram averaging, we determine the structures of the portal complex and its central pore on the vaccinia virus core at 6.7 Å and 4.4 Å resolution, respectively. Guided by structural predictions and experimental density, we identify three viral proteins, E8, E6 and L3, as the principal components of the pore and construct a structural model of their organization. The pore contains a narrow, positively charged constriction formed by conserved lysine residues in L3, substitutions of which impair viral gene expression and propagation. The dimensions and electrostatic properties of the channel are consistent with a potential role in molecular exchange across the core wall. Together, our findings establish a structural framework for understanding the organization and potential function of the poxvirus portal complex.

Two-dimensional material membranes with precisely tunable interlayer spacing from nanometre to submillimetre

Original Paper | Two-dimensional materials | 2026-11-15 19:00 EST

Qixiang Zhang, Minmin Xue, Yongxin Wang, Junjie Shi, Dandan Lei, Shulong Li, Xiaoyang Zhang, Mengjie Wang, Yanan Ma, Yang Yue, Zhuhua Zhang

Precise and reversible control over ion channel size in membrane materials is crucial for realizing optimized device performance, but conventional approaches to channel regulation suffer from limited tunability, reversibility and precision. Here we report a deprotonation-ion coordination strategy for the precise, reversible modulation of interlayer spacing over five orders of magnitude in layered material membranes. The interlayer expansion is driven by electrostatic repulsion due to the deprotonation of intercalated cellulose nanofibers and is reversibly tunable via charge compensation through cation coordination. As a result, we achieve an unprecedented degree of structural tunability: porosity from 0% to 78%, interlayer spacing from 1.39 to 152,000 nm and membrane thickness from 43 to 5,400 μm. Our strategy enables on-demand performance customization with the same membrane platform. For example, a slight interlayer expansion (to 1.48 nm) boosts zinc-ion capacitance by 67% relative to non-expanded membranes; a 21,583-fold expansion (to ~30 μm) raises osmotic energy conversion power by 63%, and a 100,719-fold expansion (to ~140 μm) unlocks exceptional pressure sensing. These results establish a generalizable paradigm for nanometric microstructure engineering of various van der Waals materials.

Controlling the flow of information in optical metrology

Original Paper | Nanophotonics and plasmonics | 2026-11-15 19:00 EST

Maximilian Weimar, Huanli Zhou, Luca Neubacher, Thomas A. Grant, Jakob Hüpfl, Kevin F. MacDonald, Stefan Rotter, Nikolay I. Zheludev

Optical metrology has surpassed the Abbe-Rayleigh limit by leveraging nonlinear phenomena and statistical accumulation, and has recently unlocked subatomic precision using artificial intelligence estimators trained on measurand variations. Here we argue that further gains can be achieved by optimizing the flow of Fisher information carrying metrological content. We show that Fisher information flow need not follow the optical energy flow and that it exhibits resonance, diffraction and interference, singular points, and vorticity. It can exhibit a Talbot-like effect in which Fisher information density patterns repeat periodically with propagation distance, and it can be focused beyond the diffraction limit, mirroring the effect of optical superoscillation. Material composition, resonances, geometry and environment determine where information is generated, how it propagates, and how it can be enhanced and directed to improve precision. This perspective recasts metrology as information flow engineering, for applications ranging from atomic-scale diagnostics and smart manufacturing to remote sensing, radar and LiDAR.

Quantum error correction aims to mitigate the loss of quantum information to the environment and is a central requirement for scalable quantum computing. Existing implementations are mainly based on measurement-based feedback, but constraints on read-out fidelity, hardware latency and system complexity can limit performance and scalability. Autonomous quantum error correction offers a complementary route based on engineered drives and dissipation, without repeated measurements or classical feedback. Here we implement an autonomous quantum error correction protocol, derived from quantum channel simulation, that repeatedly restores a photon-loss-corrupted logical qubit by transferring error information to ancilla qubits and resetting them. This result shows that drives and dissipation can extend the coherence time of an error-corrected bosonic logical qubit beyond the breakeven point.

Extending qubit lifetime beyond break-even and enhancing quantum sensing by autonomous quantum error correction

Original Paper | Quantum information | 2026-11-15 19:00 EST

Zhongchu Ni, Ling Hu, Yanyan Cai, Libo Zhang, Jiasheng Mai, Xiaowei Deng, Pan Zheng, Song Liu, Shi-Biao Zheng, Yuan Xu, Dapeng Yu

Extending the lifetime of a logical quantum bit (qubit) beyond that of the best physical qubit available within the same system–the break-even point–is a prerequisite for building practical quantum computers. Previous demonstrations have crossed this threshold using active quantum error correction (QEC), which measures an error syndrome and then applies an adaptive correction. Reaching the break-even point with autonomous QEC, which removes the need for resource-intensive measurement and feedback, remains challenging. Here we demonstrate beyond-break-even autonomous error correction in a circuit quantum electrodynamics system. We encode a photonic logical qubit in a superconducting microwave cavity and protect it against photon loss using engineered dissipation. The protected logical qubit achieves a lifetime of about 2.0 ms, 18% longer than that of the best physical qubit available in the system. We then use the protocol to measure a small frequency shift, obtaining a metrological gain of 6.3 dB relative to the most robust Fock-state superposition. These results show that autonomous QEC not only represents a necessary step towards fault-tolerant quantum computation but also offers advantages for robust quantum sensing.

Neutrinos and the origins of particle dark matter

Review Paper | Dark energy and dark matter | 2026-11-15 19:00 EST

Jaco de Swart

Despite four decades of null results, the experimental quest to discover a dark matter particle continues. Although confidence in the leading candidate – the weakly interacting massive particle – is waning, its early successes still shape current experimental approaches and theoretical expectations: from underground detectors in Europe, China and the USA to modern theories of how the large-scale cosmic structure formed. This Perspective traces the origin of this particle-based paradigm to a critical predecessor: the neutrino-dominated Universe. Proposed in the early 1970s as a solution to anomalies in the understanding of extra-galactic mass, neutrinos became the leading explanation of ‘missing matter’ following experimental hints of neutrino mass in 1980. Although short-lived, the model’s coherent picture of cosmic evolution and structure formation set strong conceptual and methodological standards – providing critical impetus to the field of particle cosmology. Examining this history invites renewed reflection on how such past aims and expectations continue to guide the search for dark matter today.

Narrow-Band Magnetism Allowed by Quantum Geometry: Altermagnetism and Beyond

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-11-16 20:00 EST

Minxuan Wang, Pei-Han Lin, Yang Ge, Jiabin Yu

Quantum geometry plays a central role in shaping correlated phases in narrow bands, yet its influence on the competition between distinct magnetic orders, especially the emergence of altermagnetism, remains largely unexplored. To address this question, we study the quadrupole insulator model with strictly onsite generalized Hubbard-Kanamori interactions, in which both Néel antiferromagnetism and altermagnetism are symmetry-permitted. Depending on the parameters, the isolated narrow lowest band of the model can either be obstructed atomic with strong quantum geometry or trivial atomic with weak quantum geometry, while the dispersion is identical in the two cases. By partially filling the lowest band, our Hartree-Fock calculations show only conventional ferromagnetic phases for the weak quantum geometric case, while we find two collinear altermagnetic phases, as well as nematic phases, for the strong quantum geometric case. Such a dramatic difference can be understood by the distinct characters of the projected interaction: the projected interactions acquire sizable longer-range channels only for the strong quantum geometric case, while they are dominated by the on-site Hubbard channel in the weak quantum geometric case. These results establish the essential role of quantum geometry for altermagnetism to emerge from interacting narrow bands where crystalline symmetries cannot sufficiently constrain the magnetic order.

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-11-16 20:00 EST

André L.G. Mudry, Ömer M. Aksoy

Invertible topological phases of matter are described by gapped ground states of local Hamiltonians that are non-degenerate on any closed spatial manifold. Classification of invertible topological phases realized by bosonic or fermionic degrees of freedom is well-understood, particularly in low dimensions. In one-dimensional space, the classification can be understood through the boundary projective representations. We extend this paradigm to one-dimensional invertible topological phases of parafermionic degrees of freedom with symmetries. We define cohomological invariants that characterize distinct boundary projective representations and compute their stacking rules. Among other results, we show (i) how these cohomological invariants can be used to compute protected boundary degeneracy and (ii) how, in contrast to bosons or fermions, invertible phases of parafermions can form a non-Abelian group under stacking. We show that under stacking they generate a non-Abelian group of order $ 2N^3$ . When $ N$ is an odd prime, this group is $ \mathrm{Heis}(\mathbb{Z}N)\rtimes \mathbb{Z}^{,}{2}$ , where $ \mathrm{Heis}(\mathbb{Z}_N)$ is the Heisenberg group of order $ N^{3}$ .

Chiral or unidirectional? Multiterminal current partition in the quantum Hall regime from self-consistent screening theory

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-11-16 20:00 EST

Afif Siddiki

Multiterminal current measurements on a six-terminal Hall bar were recently used to argue that the current flow is chiral on the quantized Hall plateaus and non-chiral between them, and, in a companion analysis, that the imposed current flows along one edge only. We examine these conclusions with the self-consistent screening theory of the quantum Hall effect. The plateau values of the current partition follow from a vanishing longitudinal conductivity and a quantized Hall conductivity alone: every two-dimensional conductor with $ \sigma_\ell=0$ and $ \sigma_{\rm H}=\nu e^2/h$ has the conductance matrix of the “coherent and chiral” model, whatever the spatial distribution of the current. Terminal currents fix the current density only up to circulating currents, made explicit here with a stream function, and the chirality of the current density is a property of exactly this part. A two-dimensional local transport calculation for the measured device, including its two interior contacts, reproduces the plateau partition for all contact configurations and the ordering of the suppression between the plateaus without edge channels. The self-consistent current density separates into a chiral equilibrium (magnetization) current, which carries no net current, and a transport current that flows in the same direction in both edge strips; the “one-edge” imposed current is a bookkeeping choice of the reference chemical potential. The floating potentials of the two interior contacts are predicted to be equal when the bulk around them is quantized and to differ by up to half the Hall voltage when the current flows in edge strips. Multiterminal current partition measures the sign of the Hall conductivity and the absence of dissipation, not the location or the chirality of the transport current; the edge channels are the premise of the interpretation, not a result of the measurement.

Understanding and controlling band alignment in lattice-mismatched group-IV semiconductor-based heterostructures is fundamental to rational band-structure engineering, as it governs the electronic structure and carrier transport in heterointegrated semiconductor systems. Optimal heterogeneous diffusivity profiles for mean first-passage minimization

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-11-16 20:00 EST

Luiz Menon

We investigate how spatially varying diffusivity can be optimized to minimize the mean first-passage time for escape from a spherical shell with an inner reflecting boundary and an outer absorbing boundary. Under a fixed volume-averaged diffusivity, we derive a variational condition for arbitrary spatial dimension and stochastic integration prescription. For particles starting at the inner boundary, we obtain exact optimal profiles in one dimension, with homogeneous diffusion being optimal under the Stratonovich prescription. In higher dimensions, the optimal Stratonovich profile becomes decreasing. The optimal Itô profile is decreasing in all dimensions, whereas the optimal Hänggi–Klimontovich profile is increasing in one and two dimensions and can exhibit a maximum in dimensions greater than two. For particles starting beyond the inner boundary, optimal profiles can exhibit a discontinuity at the initial position, with constant diffusivity in the inner region. Numerical optimization complements these results, showing how geometry, stochastic prescription, and initial position jointly determine the diffusivity distribution that minimizes the mean escape time.

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-11-16 20:00 EST

David J. Perello, Marco Coraiola, Minje Park, Cliff Chen, Hanho Lee, Abhishek Banerjee, Gil Refael, Yuki Sato

State-of-the-art superconducting qubits rely on Al/AlO$ _x$ /Al Josephson junctions (JJs) as a nonlinear element with an ideal sinusoidal current-phase dependence, with its inductance set by the junction area and oxidation conditions. This idealization holds only if the many conduction channels through the amorphous AlO$ _x$ barrier transmit weakly and almost identically. Here, we measure the second-order subgap Andreev current, which sets the second Josephson harmonic, in Al/AlO$ _x$ /Al JJs. For junction sizes relevant to qubits, we find that this current is dominated by $ \lesssim 300$ effective channels whose transparencies exceed the ideal tunneling limit ($ \lesssim 10^{-6}$ ) by orders of magnitude. For small and high-aspect-ratio junctions, increasing the junction size does not average out the effect of these high transparency channels but instead adds additional channels of even greater transparency. The trend inverts once both the width and length of the junction exceed a critical length scale, above which the effective transparency decreases with area. This non-ergodic scaling of the Andreev current is reproduced by simulations and attributed to non-self-averaging statistics of the transparency distribution, which arise from variations in oxide thickness. The effect is largely invisible to the normal-state conductance used to target the inductance and reveals unexpected geometric and size-related trade-offs for scaling superconducting qubit systems to large numbers of junctions.
