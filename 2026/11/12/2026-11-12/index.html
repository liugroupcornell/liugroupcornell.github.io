Proximal redox coupling enables oxidation damping for stable water electrolysis

Original Paper | Electrocatalysis | 2026-11-11 19:00 EST

Tengyu Chen, Kaiwen Wang, Lu Xia, Wulyu Jiang, Bruna Ferreira Gomes, Linlin Yang, Kaiqi Zhao, Hao Huang, Carlos M. S. Lobo, Ranit Ram, Junmei Chen, Ren He, Alba Garzón Manjón, Kapil Gupta, Zhouhao Zhu, Marinos Dimitropoulos, Viktoria Golovanova, Laís Gimenes Vernasqui, Sven Hörnig, Prathama Haldar, Bárbara Polesso, Aparna M. Das, Sunil Kadam, Andreu Cabot, Jordi Arbiol, F. Pelayo García de Arquer

The design of atomically precise catalysts requires strategies that regulate their electronic and structural dynamics under operating conditions. While conventional doping can effectively tune bulk materials, it translates poorly to single-atom catalysts (SACs), in which isolated atoms lack extended coordination and are prone to oxidative instability. Here we design atomically dispersed Ru-O-Cr motifs that enable oxidation damping during acidic oxygen evolution. In situ X-ray absorption spectroscopy shows attenuated Ru oxidation synchronized with anticorrelated and partially reversible Cr redox evolution, consistent with proximity-dependent redox coupling. This buffered redox response is accompanied by bounded and partially reversible changes in the local metal-oxygen coordination environment. These coupled dynamics support proximity-coupled Ru-O-Cr redox units, enabling a 55.7-fold increased resistance against oxidation-induced Ru dissolution compared with conventional SACs. In proton exchange membrane water electrolysis, catalysts incorporating proximal Ru-O-Cr configurations exhibit degradation rates that are 6.8-fold and 14.4-fold lower than those of Ru SACs and commercial RuO2, respectively, at 1 A cm-2. These results suggest proximal oxidation damping as a potential design principle for stabilizing atomically precise electrocatalysts.

Optimal learning of quantum channels in diamond distance

Original Paper | Quantum information | 2026-11-11 19:00 EST

Antonio Anna Mele, Lennart Bittel

The evolution of quantum states can be described by quantum channels. The task of estimating an unknown quantum channel, known as process tomography, is a central problem in quantum information theory. A long-standing open question is how many uses of an unknown channel are required to learn it to a precision quantified by the standard metric for distinguishing quantum processes, known as the diamond distance. Here we address this by establishing the query complexity of channel tomography with optimal dependence on the dimensions of the input and output spaces and the channel’s Kraus rank at any fixed constant accuracy in diamond distance. We design an algorithm that establishes an upper bound and prove a matching lower bound for learning a channel to the given accuracy. Our algorithm follows the strategy of performing optimal tomography on the Choi-state representation of the process, and we show that channel tomography can be reduced to the well-studied problem of pure-state tomography. As channels subsume states, unitaries and measurements as special cases, our protocol provides a unified framework for tomography of these tasks.

Electron hydrodynamics is an emerging framework that describes dynamics of interacting electron systems as conventional fluids. Although evidence for hydrodynamic-like transport has been reported in a variety of two-dimensional materials, precise quantitative measurement of the core parameter, electron viscosity, remains challenging.
