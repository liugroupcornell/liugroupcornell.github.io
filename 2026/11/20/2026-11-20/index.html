High-loading all-solid-state lithium-sulfur batteries enabled by tailored sulfur-sulfide and sulfur-carbon interfaces

Yukio Cho, Hongchang Hao, Miso Kim, Madison King, Zisheng Zhang, Shripad Patil, Jason Hwong, Haoyu Liu, Duhan Zhang, Yifan Quan, Xueli Zheng, Yan-Kai Tzeng, Bonggeun Shong, John Watt, Frank Abild-Pedersen, William C. Chueh, Yi Cui, Stacey F. Bent, Jagjit Nanda

High energy density promised by solid-state batteries requires overcoming fundamental limitations in carrier transport. Although tortuous carrier pathways in high-loading cathodes are often viewed as the dominant bottleneck, we show that interfacial ion and electron transport is an even more critical lever for enhancing energy density in sulfide-based all-solid-state lithium-sulfur batteries. By enabling partial lithiation at the sulfur-sulfide interface and eliminating the electron-impeding oxidation layer at the sulfur-carbon interface, we achieve high-loading all-solid-state lithium-sulfur battery cathodes with areal capacities near 15 mAh cm-2 and sulfur utilization above 90% with a moderate stack pressure of 15-25 MPa. This work highlights the power of interfacial control within composite cathodes, and demonstrates another approach for designing high-energy-density all-solid-state lithium-sulfur batteries for next-generation energy storage.

Four-dimensional X-ray nano-holotomography reveals multiscale chemomechanics in composite graphite-silicon battery electrodes

Original Paper | Batteries | 2026-11-19 19:00 EST

Victor Vanpeene, Olga Stamati, Francois Cadiou, Quentin Jacquet, Julie Villanova, Sandrine Lyonnard

Linking electrode microstructure to electrochemical performance is essential for optimizing Li-ion batteries. However, this requires mechanistic four-dimensional observations at the ultimate spatiotemporal scales, which remain elusive. Here we demonstrate the use of interrupted-operando synchrotron X-ray nano-holotomography combined with digital volume correlation to track chemomechanical dynamics at both particle (local) and electrode (averaged) scales. Quantitative scale-bridging image analysis is applied to composite graphite-silicon electrodes during their electrochemical formation cycle. Our findings reveal that local ionic diffusion properties, graphite particle morphology and position in the electrode, distance to silicon clusters, surface contact with the liquid electrolyte solution, and mechanical deformations all directly affect local electrochemical activity and irreversibility, although these parameters are not equally important. In particular, we identify fast ionic diffusion channels that play a key role and counterbalance intrinsic depth-dependent reaction heterogeneities arising from ionic and electronic diffusion limitations. The various structural factors that determine the electrochemical performance of composite graphite-silicon electrodes in Li-ion batteries beyond ensemble properties are classified using a scale of influence, providing a practical framework for the optimization of materials and electrode manufacturing.

Antireflective bilayer metasurfaces with complete wavefront control

Original Paper | Metamaterials | 2026-11-19 19:00 EST

Jaewon Oh, Davide Cassara, Alfonso Palmieri, Lorenzo Piatti, Janderson Rocha Rodrigues, Ahmed H. Dorrah, Paulo Dainese, Federico Capasso

Antireflection coatings are widely used in optical systems to increase transmission and suppress unwanted reflections by matching the optical impedance across a uniform interface. Applying the same idea to metasurfaces is fundamentally more challenging because their wavefront control relies on arrays of geometrically distinct meta-atoms, presenting as a spatially varying optical impedance. Here we show that vertical integration can overcome this limitation by allowing bilayer meta-atoms to act simultaneously as phase shifters and impedance-matching stacks. By treating meta-atoms as multilayer thin films, we derive a design rule that links the effective refractive indices of two independently patterned layers and identifies antireflective bilayer libraries with full 0-2π transmission phase coverage. These results establish bilayer metasurfaces as a platform for jointly engineering optical impedance and wavefront response at the meta-atom level.

A Liquid Film Can Survive without Being Bulk-Like: Rupture and Mechanical Isotropy Set Different Thickness Limits in Lennard-Jones Films

New Submission | Soft Condensed Matter (cond-mat.soft) | 2026-11-20 20:00 EST

Jose Luis Altamirano-Corona, Alfonso Lemus-Solorio, Francisco Villanueva-Mejia, Mariana Ramos-Estrada, Jose L. Rivera

How thick must a liquid film be before its middle behaves like ordinary bulk liquid? Free-standing films are the standard geometry for computing surface properties by molecular simulation and the simplest model of soap and foam films, coatings and polymer layers, yet the question is seldom answered quantitatively. We answer it for Lennard-Jones films by comparing two criteria: whether the film survives without rupturing, and whether the lateral pressure at its center has relaxed to the bulk value. Covering three interaction ranges (cutoffs rc = 3.5-7.5 times sigma), three temperatures and lateral system sizes from 20 to 100 times sigma, we find that the two criteria give different thicknesses and respond to different parameters. Films thinner than 6-7 times sigma (about 2 nm for argon) rupture within 105 tau (~0.2 microseconds); this kinetic limit hardly depends on the interaction range but grows with the lateral size of the system, and close to it rupture is an activated event. The center becomes mechanically bulk-like only at a larger thickness h\ast, where the tangential anisotropy computed with the Irving-Kirkwood definition falls below 0.003 epsilon x sigma^3; h\ast grows from ~11 times sigma (3.8 nm) to ~15 times sigma (5 nm) as the cutoff increases from 3.5 to 7.5 times sigma, but it is nearly independent of temperature and of lateral size. Between the two limits films are long-lived yet perturbed throughout. Neither the surface tension, which shows no thickness dependence, nor the central density, whose relative deficit is about 16 times smaller than the relative anisotropy, reveals this perturbation: the stress detects confinement before the structure does.

Kristoffer Leraand, Asle Sudbø

We show that magnon exchange can induce nodal topological superconductivity in a microscopic model of a $ p$ -wave magnet. Solving the weak-coupling multiband gap equation for itinerant electrons exchange-coupled to localized spins, we find a nodal $ p$ -wave gap and a critical temperature that rises sharply as the Fermi surface approaches the magnetic Brillouin-zone boundary. New Submission | Superconductivity (cond-mat.supr-con) | 2026-11-20 20:00 EST

Vera Mikheeva, Elio J. König

We study the fate of the Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless (BKT) transition in a two-dimensional topological superconductor whose vortex cores each host an isolated Majorana zero mode, energetically well separated from the bulk gap and other bound states of the vortex. When the Majorana localization length $ \xi_F$ exceeds the vortex core size $ \xi$ , nearby vortices acquire an additional short-range attractive channel from Majorana hybridization, modifying the vortex-antivortex interaction at short distances and generating a metastable minimum in the two-vortex free energy above the nominal $ T_{BKT}$ . Finally, we discuss experimental signatures of this first-order transition, in particular a hysteresis loop whose width is set by the coexistence window.

Chern Numbers from Symmetry-Enforced Zeros and Dispersion Shifts

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-11-20 20:00 EST

Yoonseok Hwang, Henry Davenport, Frank Schindler

Wave-function zeros and their local vorticities provide a complementary characterization of Chern topology to more commonly used Berry-curvature formulations. Using wave-function overlaps between a Chern band and freely chosen reference states, we show that crystalline symmetry can enforce their zeros and fix their vorticities modulo the rotation order from the angular momenta of the band and reference state. We further show that weak local perturbations produce band-dispersion shifts proportional to the absolute square of the overlap, allowing its zeros and local structure to be read off directly from the dispersion. The dispersion shift near an overlap zero further provides a lower bound on the quantum metric at the zero.

Topological (in)equivalence encoded in zeros of wavefunction overlaps

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-11-20 20:00 EST

Carolina Paiva, Bruno Mera, Roni Ilan, Raquel Queiroz

Topological equivalence between two gapped band structures is determined by whether their occupied states can be continuously deformed into one another without closing the energy gap or breaking the protecting symmetries. We show that this relative topology can be read directly from the overlap matrix between the two occupied subspaces, in a way that treats Chern and $ \mathbb{Z}_2$ invariants within a single framework. The determinant of the overlap matrix defines a global section of a line bundle over the Brillouin zone, which vanishes at the momenta where the map between the subspaces fails to be invertible. A singular connection, whose curvature is concentrated at these zeros, assigns each zero an integer charge given by its phase winding. In two-dimensional class A, the total charge equals the difference between the Chern numbers of the two systems. In class AII, time reversal pairs zeros of opposite charge, and the relative $ \mathbb{Z}_2$ invariant is the parity of the total charge in half the Brillouin zone; the Kane-Mele Pfaffian is recovered as a special case. The zeros coincide with the gap closings of an interpolation between the two systems, and crystalline symmetries constrain their positions and charges. We illustrate the construction in the Haldane, Kane-Mele, and Qi-Wu-Zhang models, including cases where rotation symmetry alone enforces the zeros.
