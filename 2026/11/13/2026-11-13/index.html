Quantitative Gaussian limits of periodic O(N) models at and below the critical temperature

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-11-13 20:00 EST

Weiqi Jiang, Pengcheng Hou, Zhiyi Li

We prove a volume-uniform Gaussian approximation for local spin coordinates in the zero-field nearest-neighbour $ O(N)$ model on periodic boxes of side $ M$ in $ d\ge3$ , at and below the critical temperature of the limiting spherical model. On a fixed spatial window, for sufficiently large $ M,N$ , the unnormalized $ 1$ -Wasserstein error for the first $ p\le N$ coordinates is at most $ C\sqrt p (N^{-1/2}+s_M)$ , where $ s_M\to0$ is the spectral shift of the finite-volume mean spherical model. The estimate is uniform on compact ranges of positive directional couplings and inverse temperatures. The target has independent coordinate fields, each a scaled anisotropic Gaussian free field with an independent spatially constant Gaussian term below criticality.
On a window containing a nearest-neighbour star (a site and its $ d$ positive-direction neighbours), the error tends to zero as $ M,N\to\infty$ exactly when $ p=o(N)$ and $ \sqrt p s_M\to0$ . For any choice of periodic infinite-volume accumulation states, the error on any nonempty fixed window tends to zero as $ N\to\infty$ exactly when $ p=o(N)$ . In $ d\ge5$ , when the inverse temperature exceeds its critical value by at least a fixed positive amount, the bound improves to $ C(p/N+\sqrt p M^{-d})$ . For fixed $ p$ , the sharp order is $ N^{-1}+M^{-d}$ on star-containing windows and $ N^{-1}$ on every nonempty fixed window in periodic accumulation states.

Little-Parks Oscillations and Parity Effects in Mesoscopic Superconducting Rings

New Submission | Superconductivity (cond-mat.supr-con) | 2026-11-13 20:00 EST

Iogann Tolbatov, Luca Salasnich

We analytically investigate the Little-Parks (LP) effect, the periodic oscillation of the superconducting transition temperature $ T_c$ driven by an applied magnetic flux $ \Phi$ , from the weak-coupling Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer (BCS) limit to the intermediate-coupling regime in a mesoscopic, quasi-one-dimensional ring. Utilizing a Ginzburg-Landau (GL) expansion with twisted boundary conditions, we correctly incorporate both the discrete finite-size topological parity interference and the continuous center-of-mass (CM) kinetic energy shift induced by the supercurrent. We derive a closed-form expression for $ T_c(\Phi)$ valid within the GL framework across variable interaction strengths. We demonstrate that the LP response comprises two distinct physical contributions: a discrete topological parity effect governed by the correlation length ratio $ \xi/R$ , and a continuous kinetic energy term scaling as $ 1/R^2$ . As the coupling increases and the spatial correlation length of the pairs contracts ($ \xi \ll R$ ), the constituent pairs become geometrically blind to the global boundary constraint, leading to an exponential suppression of the discrete parity modulation. However, the requirement that the condensate acquires a flux-dependent macroscopic CM momentum persists. This ensures that the critical temperature $ T_c$ maintains an algebraic LP modulation driven predominantly by the macroscopic kinetic energy of the supercurrent. We bound our theoretical approach by discussing the breakdown of mean-field GL validity before phase fluctuations dominate near the strong-coupling limit.

R. Gabbrielli, P. Pais, P. Castorina, T. P. C. Klaver, D. Legut, O. De Luca, R. G. Agostino, A. Iorio

Singlet-triplet superconductivity from repulsive interactions in a $p$-wave magnet

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-11-13 20:00 EST

Hanbit Oh, Laura Classen, Daniel F. Agterberg

Motivated by recent proposals of $ p$ -wave magnets, which host odd-parity spin splitting, we study electrons near Van Hove singularities with local repulsive interactions. We demonstrate how the symmetry of the $ p$ -wave spin splitting governs the competition among instabilities. Using a parquet renormalization group (pRG) analysis of a simple one-sublattice model of a $ p$ -wave magnet, we find that superconductivity is the leading instability over the entire phase diagram for purely repulsive interactions. The resulting superconducting state is generically a mixture of spin-singlet and spin-triplet pairing and approaches an equal-weight mixture when $ T_c$ is small compared with the energy offset between each Van Hove electron and its inversion partner. Extending the pRG analysis to a more realistic two-sublattice model, we find that superconductivity remains the leading instability, while the sublattice degree of freedom leads to a competition among various singlet-triplet pairing channels and even a finite-momentum equal-spin pair-density wave. Our mechanism applies broadly to odd-parity magnets and opens a natural route to nodal topological superconductivity.

Symmetry-Enabled Rolling of Composite Bosons: A Route to Superconductivity from Strong Repulsion with $T_c$ Proportional to the Electron Bandwidth

New Submission | Superconductivity (cond-mat.supr-con) | 2026-11-13 20:00 EST

Zhiyu Dong

Motivated by superconductors discovered in systems with a strong Coulomb repulsion, we ask whether such a repulsion can give rise to superconductivity with a transition temperature $ T_c$ bounded only by the electron bandwidth. Coulomb repulsion can produce pairing as it binds electrons to holes. If each hole binds three electrons into a three-electron-one-hole (3e1h) composite, the condensation of these bosonic composites can give rise to superconductivity. However, the resulting $ T_c$ falls far below the electron bandwidth in the controlled regime where the binding is much stronger than the carriers’ kinetic energy. This is because, on a lattice, a tightly bound composite usually has to move through a higher-order process where the intermediate state costs an energy of order the binding energy, so that the strong binding makes the composite heavy, limiting the superfluid stiffness and hence $ T_c$ . Here, we show that symmetry offers a way around this tradeoff. In lattices satisfying certain symmetry requirements, a composite can move by single-particle hops without any costly intermediate state, a motion we call rolling. Such rolling keeps the mass of the composite finite even for an infinitely strong binding, so that a $ T_c$ proportional to the electron bandwidth can be achieved in the controlled regime. We derive a criterion for rolling and apply it to construct all fourteen minimal lattice models that support such rolling. We show that rollable 3e1h composites can indeed be stabilized in the ground state. We then propose to realize such superconductivity in a dual-gate bilayer, and use exact numerics to show that $ T_c$ can reach a few percent of the electron hopping. These results establish rollability as a guiding principle in the search for and design of superconductors in strongly repelling electron systems, with the minimal models constructed here as prototypes.

Nikolaos G. Fytas, Víctor Martín-Mayor, Attilio L. Stella, Gianluca Teza, Alexandros Vasilopoulos, David Yllanes

The central limit theorem is among the most fundamental principles in the natural sciences, governing the fluctuations of sums of weakly correlated random variables through Gaussian statistics. At critical points, however, long-range correlations invalidate this paradigm, raising a fundamental question: does any general limit theorem survive at criticality? Although scale invariance has long been recognised as the defining feature of typical critical fluctuations, the behaviour of rare fluctuations has remained largely unexplored, as their extraordinarily small probabilities place them beyond the reach of conventional numerical methods. Recent theoretical advances suggest that universal asymptotic laws {may govern large deviations in systems at criticality}, yet direct numerical evidence has been lacking. This collapse is governed by a universal amplitude, whose numerical extraction is compatible with the conjectured exact result we derive from conformal field theory, and points towards a generalised central limit theorem for critical fluctuations.

Fatigue crack growth without plasticity under cyclically reversed shear

New Submission | Soft Condensed Matter (cond-mat.soft) | 2026-11-13 20:00 EST

E. A. Jagla

We show that cyclic fatigue can occur in a system whose elements behave elastically up to an irreversible failure threshold, without plasticity, friction, or any time-dependent process. The model represents a weak interface between two elastic bodies loaded in shear. Under sufficiently large cyclic loading below the static failure threshold, a pre-existing flaw advances progressively, by a few lattice spacings per cycle. Two conditions are needed for this behavior: each bond fails more easily under one sense of the shear than the opposite, and the shear must be reversed during the cycle by an amount large enough to break bonds that have not yet failed. The model thus identifies the minimal conditions for an intrinsic, plasticity-free fatigue mechanism. Such conditions may be met at interfaces and weak planes subjected to strongly reversed shear, as in fretting, rolling contact, and reversed torsion, or in cyclic shear of cemented granular materials.
