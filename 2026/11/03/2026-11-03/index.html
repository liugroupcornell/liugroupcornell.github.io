Three-dimensional imaging of individual carbon atoms

Original Paper | Two-dimensional materials | 2026-11-02 19:00 EST

Na Yeon Kim, Hanfeng Zhong, Jianhua Zhang, Colum M. O’Leary, Yuxuan Liao, Ji Zou, Haozhi Sha, Minh Pham, Weiyi Li, Yakun Yuan, Ji-Hoon Park, Dennis S. Kim, Huaidong Jiang, Jing Kong, Miaofang Chi, Jianwei Miao

Ptychography is a computational microscopy method that unifies crystallography and microscopy by combining coherent diffraction with iterative phase retrieval. Although multislice electron ptychography achieves deep-sub-ångström lateral resolution, its depth resolution is typically at the nanometre scale. Ptychographic atomic electron tomography (pAET) has enabled three-dimensional (3D) atomic structure determination in nanomaterials, yet reliably resolving individual light atoms remains challenging. Here, using twisted bilayer graphene as a model moiré system, we demonstrate pAET for 3D localization of 6,648 carbon atoms with a precision of 0.12 Å. The resulting 3D atomic structure reveals stacking-dependent interlayer spacing and corrugation across the moiré supercell, as well as chiral lattice distortions with meron-like, anti-meron-like and anti-skyrmion-like textures. Density functional theory calculations based on the experimentally derived structure predict electronic band narrowing and a gap at the K point relative to a relaxed model. Multislice pAET simulations further support extending the method to diamond containing a nitrogen-vacancy centre and to amorphous carbon. These results establish pAET for 3D imaging of individual carbon atoms and open a route to linking 3D atomic structure with the properties of light-element materials.

Weak-coupling selection and finite-torus entropy in the triangular Ising antiferromagnet

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-11-03 20:00 EST

Weiqi Jiang, Pengcheng Hou

An antiferromagnetic second-neighbor interaction favors stripe order in the triangular-lattice Ising antiferromagnet, but uniform stripes cannot satisfy odd periodic boundaries. At each fixed odd size $ L\geq5$ , we minimize this interaction over the ground states of the zero-field nearest-neighbor model on an $ L\times L$ torus. We prove a minimum total interface length of $ 4L$ and classify all $ L^2(L^2-1)/2$ selected states. Under the uniform measure on these states, the area fractions of the three stripe orientations approach a uniform distribution over all nonnegative triples summing to one. Thus selection reduces an exponential degeneracy to polynomial growth, while the whole-sample orientation fractions remain broadly distributed. We also derive explicit two-sided entropy-density bounds for periodic spin ground states and honeycomb dimer coverings on $ M\times N$ tori, where $ M,N$ are the periods along the two primitive lattice directions. For all $ M,N\geq3$ , the errors relative to the bulk residual entropy are bounded by explicit constants times $ \min(M,N)^{-2}$ , without an aspect-ratio restriction. These bounds give finite-size accuracy guarantees and control the exponentially small fraction of unperturbed ground states that survive the selection.

On-the-fly $Δ$-machine learned force fields for warm dense matter using a Yukawa reference model: Application to magnesium

New Submission | Materials Science (cond-mat.mtrl-sci) | 2026-11-03 20:00 EST

Sayan Bhowmik, John E. Pask, Phanish Suryanarayana

We develop a framework for on-the-fly $ \Delta$ -machine learned force field ($ \Delta$ -MLFF) molecular dynamics (MD) simulations of warm dense matter (WDM). In particular, we employ a Yukawa reference model that captures the dominant screened-Coulomb interactions between the ions, with its parameters fit on the fly to Kohn–Sham density functional theory (DFT) data, and an MLFF scheme based on the kernel method and Bayesian linear regression that learns the differences in the energies, atomic forces, and stresses between the Yukawa model and Kohn–Sham DFT. We find that the pressure, self-diffusion coefficient, and shear viscosity obey power laws in temperature and density, that the dynamic structure factor exhibits a well-defined ion-acoustic mode whose negative dispersion is characteristic of strongly coupled liquids, and that the Stokes–Einstein relation holds to within 10% over the conditions considered.

Physical vapor deposition can produce organic glasses with enhanced stability and pronounced structural anisotropy (layering) even when the constituent molecules are nearly spherical. However, the microscopic origin of the layering in spherical molecular glass formers remains unclear. We investigated vapor-deposited films of 9-(3,5-di(naphthalen-1-yl)phenyl)anthracene ({\alpha},{\alpha}-A) by grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering, atomistic molecular dynamics, and coarse-grained simulations. The findings connect structural anisotropy in vapor-deposited molecular glasses to the generic interfacial organization of simple liquids.

Chemical bonding is the language through which chemistry rationalizes materials structure, stability, properties, and reactivity. The age of machine learning shifts the focus from whether a bonding model is physically motivated to whether it captures the information required to explain and predict materials behavior. If a materials genome exists, it should be encodable through a representation of chemical bonding. If no bonding representation can capture this information, our understanding of the chemical bond may require revision.

Impact of Surface Preparation on the Propulsion of Catalytic Janus Colloids and Implications for Reproducibility

New Submission | Soft Condensed Matter (cond-mat.soft) | 2026-11-03 20:00 EST

Muhammad Haroon, Mitchell Lau, Christopher L. Wirth (Case Western Reserve University)

Platinum-coated polystyrene (PS) Janus particles (JPs) propelled by the catalytic decomposition of hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) are a widely used model system for active matter, yet reported speeds for similar particles and fuel concentrations differ by more than an order of magnitude. Broad adoption of these materials requires increasing consistency in the preparation and achieving improved repeatability in their behavior. Herein, we focused on two routine, but rarely reported, preparation steps that we found to alter the particle speed. First, we found that pre-exposing the platinum cap to H2O2 before cell assembly increased the mean speed by a factor of 2.4. The size of this effect depended on batch history, where an aged batch responded strongly, while a freshly prepared batch sped up by only 16%. Second, we found that plasma cleaning of the glass substrate decreased the speed 2.7 times. In a 120 $ \mu$ m cell the suppression was the same at both walls even when only one wall was treated. The suppression grew with the treated glass area per unit fluid volume, which pointed to species released into the bulk solution rather than to a change at the wall. Plasma-cleaned glass released more ions into water than untreated glass, and XPS detected an ionic metal fluoride on the plasma-cleaned surface. A model in which the released ions lower the speed through the solution conductivity reproduced the measured speeds in all four cell geometries. Of the 17 published studies compared here, pre-activation is reported in only four studies and substrate cleaning in only six studies. We propose a minimum reporting standard for the preparation and associated speed measurements of catalytic Janus particles.

Quantum dynamics of ultrasmall magnetic skyrmions in atomic-defect landscapes

New Submission | Materials Science (cond-mat.mtrl-sci) | 2026-11-03 20:00 EST

Samir Lounis

Magnetic skyrmions are prime candidates for future information technology, and their fate in devices is settled by the defects they encounter. In ultrathin films, skyrmions can be as small as a couple of nanometers, carrying only a few tens of magnetic moments, while atomic defects reshape their energy landscape on the atomic scale. Here, we demonstrate that such ultrasmall skyrmions can behave quantum mechanically and that their quantum dynamics is unusual. Since the gyrotropic force dominates, the two coordinates of a ferromagnetic skyrmion are canonically conjugate and the skyrmion behaves as a particle in the lowest Landau level of an effective magnetic field, with a magnetic length of 0.6Å. Utilizing skyrmion-defect interaction profiles of Cr and Fe adatoms and dimers in PdFe/Ir(111), obtained from atomistic spin simulations parametrized from first principles, we unveil quantized gyrotropic orbits with level spacings of 20–85$ \mu$ eV, bound not only by pinning defects but also, counter-intuitively, by repulsive ones. Saddle points of the energy landscape act as quantum beam splitters for skyrmions, and quantum depinning reaches rates of 1–100Hz below the critical current. Embedded impurities, in contrast, bind the skyrmion so strongly that it ceases to behave as a rigid particle, defining a strong-coupling regime. For the Gilbert damping typical of ultrathin films, the quantized levels remain well resolved. The thermal-to-quantum crossover lies between 40 and 160mK, which is within reach of millikelvin scanning tunneling microscopy, where tip-induced escape statistics and electron spin resonance STM can reveal the quantized gyrotropic motion of skyrmions pinned at defects crafted atom by atom.
