Two-dimensional liquid-like order in a confined population of swimming Eudorina colonies

New Submission | Soft Condensed Matter (cond-mat.soft) | 2026-11-10 20:00 EST

Nicholas Tufillaro

Swimming microorganisms accumulate at boundaries, but the spatial statistics of the resulting layers are rarely measured. We report a population of colonial green algae, of order 71 $ \mu$ m across, that accumulates against the rigid upper wall of a closed 1-2 mm chamber and holds a two-dimensional non-contacting arrangement for about 105 min. The pair correlation function has a first shell at $ r/\bar{D} = 1.73$ , $ g = 4.8$ ; a hard-disc null at the same area fraction reaches only $ g = 2.1$ , so the ordering is not excluded volume alone. Thermal convection is excluded on Rayleigh-number grounds. Caveats: the length calibration is nominal rather than metrological, and this is a single population observed once. Interception Focusing of Colloid Transport in Granular Porous Media

New Submission | Soft Condensed Matter (cond-mat.soft) | 2026-11-10 20:00 EST

William P. Johnson (1), Noah G. Willis (1), Luis A. Ullauri (1), Bashar M. Al-Zghoul (2), Diogo Bolster (3) ((1) University of Utah, Geology and Geophysics, Salt Lake City, Utah, USA (2) University of California Davis, Civil and Environ. Eng., Davis, California, USA (3) University of Notre Dame, Civil and Environmental Engineering and Earth Sciences, Notre Dame, Indiana, United States)

Colloid transport and retention in environmental granular porous media underlie groundwater resource protection from, and remediation using, biological and non-biological nano- and micro-particles (colloids). During transport, colloids periodically intercept grain surfaces and translate along them at separation distances across several tens of nm corresponding to the secondary minimum between the opposing forces of longer-range of van der Waals (VDW) attraction and shorter-range electric double layer (EDL) repulsion. Relatively weak diffusive translation of colloids supports their continued translation in secondary minimum association until they are expelled to bulk fluid at rear flow stagnation zones on grain leeward sides. We herein show that this dynamic focuses colloids into a network of fluid streamlines (incomplete mixing lamellae) that enhances their subsequent likelihood of interception with downgradient grains, i.e., increases the so-called collector efficiency (typically denoted by Greek letter eta). Interception focusing of colloids in granular porous media explains well-observed outcomes of colloid transport under environmental conditions, including nonmonotonic nonexponential profiles of retained colloids and the non-Markovian nature of extended tailing of low colloid concentrations following initial elution. Interception focusing is a fundamental process that distinguishes colloids from solutes and renders inapplicable to colloids the assumption that is often invoked for solutes of complete pore scale mixing in granular porous media.

Conventional manufacturing constructs three-dimensional geometries through localized material deposition, removal, or shaping, with fabrication effort greatly increasing with geometric complexity. Here, we introduce a manufacturing approach in which target geometry is encoded into sparse wireframe scaffolds that guide liquid self-organization through interfacial forces. New Submission | Soft Condensed Matter (cond-mat.soft) | 2026-11-10 20:00 EST

Jacob Bair, Zachery Gottshall, Matthias Kuehne

The scaling of electrical conductance with the number of nanopores, $ N$ , in an array is of fundamental importance for applications in membrane separation and ion transport across biological membranes, and has been the subject of longstanding disagreement in the field. Combining finite element calculations with theory, we resolve this disagreement for pores with negligible surface charge by showing that two previously conflicting predictions are each correct in their own limiting reservoir geometry: cuboidal reservoirs commensurate with the interpore spacing, for which the conductance scales linearly with $ N$ , and infinite reservoirs, for which the scaling is sublinear in $ N$ for closely spaced pores. Our work provides a general framework for predicting nanopore array conductance as a function of pore arrangement and reservoir geometry, of importance to nanofluidics research and the targeted design of functional devices.
