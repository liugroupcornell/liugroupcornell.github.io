Band-Edge-Extended Heterointerfaces for Perovskite Solar Cells and Tandems

Original Paper | Solar cells | 2026-11-17 19:00 EST

Bitao Dong, Meijie Cui, Cong Chen, Ruoting Zhao, Huanxin Ju, Jingwei Xue, Yingguo Yang, Likai Zheng, Ling Zeng, Yanbiao Ran, Taixiang Li, Zhenyin Zhang, Gengsheng Chen, Jinggang Lan, Qunping Fan, Long Jiang, Shanglei Feng, Jiaxin Li, Wenyan Su, Yuxiang Li, Shaik Mohammed Zakeeruddin, Mingyang Wei, Wei Ma, Jie Kong, Michael Grätzel, Yuhang Liu

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have emerged as a promising photovoltaic technology, with efficiencies rapidly approaching those of established crystalline-silicon solar cells. Further efficiency gains, however, are increasingly limited by charge extraction and recombination at the interface between the perovskite absorber and the electron-transport layer. Interfacial modifiers are widely used to suppress these losses, yet many conventional ammonium-based modifiers are weakly electronically coupled to the perovskite surface and can introduce barriers to electron extraction 1-5. Here we realize electronically coupled heterointerfaces by introducing a family of heteroatom-containing, fully conjugated formamidinium interfacial modulators, establishing band-edge-extending conjugated heterointerfaces that overcome the bottleneck of inefficient interfacial electron transfer. The resulting band-edge-extended heterointerface establishes delocalized electron connectivity at the perovskite/electron transporting layer (ETL) interface and suppresses interfacial non-radiative recombination. We report a certified power-conversion efficiency (PCE) of 27.96% and a stabilized power output (SPO) of 27.84% for inverted PSCs. The corresponding devices retained over 95% of their initial efficiency after 2000 h continuous maximum-power-point tracking under 1-sun irradiation (ISOS-L). Extending this strategy to monolithic perovskite-silicon tandems enables a certified SPO of 34.71%, establishing band-edge extension as a broadly applicable approach to overcoming the conventional trade-off between interfacial passivation and charge extraction.

Widespread deciduousness reveals diverse Amazonian phenological strategies

The Amazon rainforest is a cornerstone of regional and global carbon cycling1 and is widely regarded and modelled as a perennially evergreen ecosystem2,3. This prevailing view, however, masks substantial fine-scale variation in leaf phenology, limiting our capacity to predict ecosystem responses to climate change. Here by combining 10-m Sentinel-2 observations with machine learning, we uncover pervasive, spatially structured seasonal deciduousness across the basin. In 70.0% of the Amazon, leaf shedding precedes leaf flushing by at least 3 months, creating pronounced asynchrony between deciduousness and greenness seasonality, whereas the remainder shows near-synchronous phenology. These findings challenge the conventional view of Amazonian phenology, demonstrating that previously overlooked deciduousness influences Amazonian carbon dynamics. Incorporating these insights into process-based models is essential for refining projections of climate-forest feedbacks in the Amazon and for understanding its ecological functions under accelerating global change.

Observation of tunable superradiant frequency combs

Original Paper | Single photons and quantum effects | 2026-11-17 19:00 EST

Tian Xie, Rikuto Fukumori, Wai-Keong Mok, Jiahui Li, Joonhee Choi, Andrei Faraon

Cavity quantum electrodynamics1 with quantum emitters coupled to resonators provides a powerful platform for engineering light-matter interactions2,3 and exploring many-body phenomena4,5. In particular, superradiance6–arising from collective quantum interference among emitters–has been explored as a route to ultrastable continuous radiation7. However, engineering superradiance in the time domain to realize periodic pulsed sources or frequency combs remains largely unexplored. Here we investigate the non-equilibrium many-body dynamics of a driven spin ensemble coupled with an on-chip superconducting resonator and uncover a dynamical phase transition from continuous-wave to periodic pulsed superradiant emission. Our results not only open new opportunities for atom-based frequency-comb sources and programmable superradiant generation, but establish a fundamental connection between periodic pulsed superradiance and the emergence of a continuous time crystal8,9,10 as a new non-equilibrium phase in driven open systems.

Quantum resistance paradox of low-dimensional superfluids

Original Paper | Quantum simulation | 2026-11-17 19:00 EST

Simon Wili, Meng-Zi Huang, Tommaso Bonaccorsi, Michael U. Mühlematter, Mohsen Talebi, Yaakov Yudkin, Alex Gómez-Salvador, Filip Marijanovic, Eugene Demler, Tilman Esslinger

Resistance in standard conductors decreases with increasing cross-section. Yet, in low-dimensional superconductors and superfluids, residual resistance arises from topological fluctuations of the order parameter, manifesting as phase slips in one-dimensional (1D) and vortices in two-dimensional (2D) systems. How resistance and dissipation evolve as geometry interpolates between these regimes remains an open question. This evolution is masked in solid-state experiments by disorder, impurities and geometric imperfections1,2,3, and poses theoretical challenges due to competing dissipative processes and pronounced finite-size effects. Here, we use a defect-free unitary Fermi gas in a digitally programmable transport geometry to isolate geometric effects on superfluid dissipation and discover a paradox: in the crossover from one dimension to two dimensions, the resistance reaches a minimum. There, widening a channel increases its resistance. Narrower, quasi-1D channels show dissipation described by Langer-Ambegaokar-McCumber-Halperin theory of phase slips4. In this regime, varying the channel width yields the predicted exponential scaling of the activation factor over many orders of magnitude. Wider, quasi-2D channels show dissipation consistent with phase-slip events described by a finite-size vortex model5. The minimal dissipation in the dimensional crossover reflects a transition in the dominant dissipative mechanism, with both 1D and 2D events simultaneously suppressed. Our measurements suggest a route to minimizing dissipation in superconducting devices and provide a benchmark for theoretical efforts aimed at describing the dimensional crossover.

Although the ecological and social conditions that favour cannibalism have been well documented2, the molecular signals that trigger cannibalistic attacks have remained poorly understood. Guaiacol is synthesized by haemolymph symbiont Enterobacter hormaechei.
