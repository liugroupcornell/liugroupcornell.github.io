Breaking symmetry in nanoparticle superlattices via spontaneous valency

Original Paper | Self-assembly | 2026-11-01 19:00 EST

Nicholas Sbalbi, Stephen Kronenberger, Hana Shinzawa, Matthew Ye, Arthi Jayaraman, Robert J. Macfarlane

Nanoparticle superlattices enable programmable material structure through ligand-mediated interactions, but attractive ligand interactions typically limit assembly to densely packed lattices that maximize nearest-neighbour bonding. Intentionally programmed directional bonding and valency are therefore typically assumed to require particle-level anisotropy in shape or surface patterning. Here, using assemblies of flexible, electrostatically active isotropic nanoparticles, we show that valency need not be encoded by particle geometry, but can instead emerge spontaneously during assembly. When ligand coronas are sufficiently deformable, ligands reconfigure to align along interparticle interfaces, generating an effective valency. Using a combination of experiment and simulation, we show that increasing ligand deformability lowers lattice coordination numbers, and identify design considerations for assembling isotropic building blocks into lattices with lower coordination, packing density and symmetry than would be predicted from hard-sphere packing. The work presented here therefore establishes ligand flexibility as a design parameter for programming coordination in nanoparticle assembly.

Spin currents in polariton waveguides driven by bright and dark excitons

Original Paper | Electronic properties and materials | 2026-11-01 19:00 EST

Qiuyu Shang, Mingyang Zhang, Maria V. Maggi, Nathan W. E. Seet, Kevin Dini, Tianshu Zhao, Zexin Feng, Dong Yang, Maksym Gromovyi, Sebastien Chenot, Blandine Alloing, Abdullah Rasmita, Guillaume Malpuech, Timothy C. H. Liew, Dmitry Solnyshkov, Weibo Gao, Jesús Zúñiga-Pérez

The optical spin Hall effect enables the spatial separation of four domains of circularly polarized light from a linearly polarized source through photonic spin-orbit coupling, which arises from non-degenerate transverse-electric and transverse-magnetic modes. This mechanism is ineffective for incoherent photoluminescence. In transition metal dichalcogenide monolayers, bright excitons possess in-plane electric dipoles and strong oscillator strengths, whereas dark excitons have out-of-plane dipoles. Here, by embedding WS2 monolayers in waveguide microcavities, we demonstrate strong coupling between bright excitons and photonic modes, forming exciton-polaritons. Under non-resonant pumping, dark excitons become strongly populated and feed both transverse-magnetic- and transverse-electric-like exciton-polariton modes, each with an out-of-plane electric-field component. We observe the optical spin Hall effect in waveguide polaritons, leading to the formation of eight polarization domains and spin-polarized polariton currents propagating up to 92 ± 1 μm at room temperature. These results establish transition metal dichalcogenide-based polaritonic waveguides as a promising platform for room-temperature spin-optoelectronic devices.

Laser-based additive manufacturing (LAM) provides a transformative route for fabricating Cr-Fe-Co-Ni-Al-Ti-based multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs). We bound the first crossing of their relative entropies by treating relaxation as an entropy-production race. SKFitter: Symmetry resolved Slater Koster tight-binding models from first principles for s, p, d and f electrons, spin orbit coupling and magnetism

New Submission | Materials Science (cond-mat.mtrl-sci) | 2026-11-02 20:00 EST

Sundeep Kapila, Bhaskaran Muralidharan, Satadeep Bhattacharjee

Constructing accurate and physically interpretable tight binding (TB) Hamiltonians from first principles calculations remains challenging, particularly for materials exhibiting complex orbital hybridization, spin orbit coupling (SOC), and magnetic order. SKFitter is an open-source Python framework for constructing and optimizing symmetry resolved Slater Koster TB models directly from density functional theory (DFT) calculations performed using Quantum ESPRESSO or VASP. The framework supports s to f orbitals, incorporates on site SOC and site resolved magnetic exchange interactions, and accommodates both orthogonal and nonorthogonal basis. A central feature is the symmetry resolved parameterization of orbital manifolds, which allows independent two centre hopping integrals between symmetry allowed orbital groups and across distinct neighbour shells. SKFitter also identifies missing orbital contributions and guide systematic basis set refinement and optimizes parameters through weights. The resulting Hamiltonian can be used to evaluate anomalous (AHC) and spin Hall conductivities (SHC), and topological invariants. We demonstrate the framework using eight representative materials spanning semimetals, semiconductors, transition metals, f-electron compounds, and magnetic systems. Symmetry resolved models achieve band energy errors of 9 to 41 meV within 1 eV of the Fermi level. The fitted Hamiltonians reproduce characteristic features of the SHC response of Pt, the SOC dependence of the AHC of Fe, and the momentum dependent altermagnetic spin splitting of MnTe without any altermagnet specific interaction. For EuMn2Bi2, projection guided orbital refinement reduces the near Fermi level fitting error from 120 to 32 meV. Together, these results establish SKFitter as a flexible and reproducible framework for developing symmetry consistent, physically interpretable TB models of complex materials.

Kibble-Zurek dynamics beyond the thermodynamic limit

New Submission | Materials Science (cond-mat.mtrl-sci) | 2026-11-02 20:00 EST

Max Haas, Guy C. Moore, Isaac M. Craig, Kristin A. Persson, Jiali He, Michael E. Hjelmstad, Dennis Meier, Sinéad M. Griffin, Jan Schultheiß

The Kibble-Zurek mechanism (KZM) predicts the universal scaling of topological defect densities generated during continuous phase transitions, a result derived in the thermodynamic limit of homogeneous systems. Yet real materials are intrinsically finite and heterogeneous, raising the question of how nonequilibrium universality survives outside this idealized limit. Here we establish an ensemble-finite-size extension of the KZM that describes critical dynamics in collections of independent finite subsystems with distributed sizes. We test this framework using a polycrystalline model system undergoing a symmetry-breaking transition. We find that the density of topological vortices follows an apparent power law with an exponent approximately half that observed in single crystals. This reduced exponent emerges quantitatively from averaging over the experimentally measured grain-size distribution, without fitting to the measured vortex densities. Our results demonstrate that finite-size effects do not simply suppress KZ scaling but reorganize it into an emergent statistical scaling regime governed by system-size heterogeneity. This establishes microstructure and geometry as control parameters of nonequilibrium critical dynamics and provides a general strategy to engineer scaling behaviour in complex materials.

Interfacing quantum materials with photonic cavity architectures is at the vanguard of materials engineering. The structured electromagnetic fluctuations of a dark cavity environment can renormalize fundamental thermodynamical material properties and energy scales, holding promise to create a rich ecosystem of cavity-induced ground states and functionalities absent in the bare material. However, direct experimental demonstrations that cavity coupling can stabilize or enhance genuinely new phases of matter have remained scarce. In this work, we establish the presence of cavity-induced and cavity-enhanced ferroelectric phases in free-standing SrTiO$ _3$ membranes coupled to dark THz-frequency photonic crystal cavities. Using THz spectroscopy, we observe ultrastrong coupling (USC) between the ferroelectric soft mode in SrTiO$ _3$ and the cavity resonance. The structural response of SrTiO$ _3$ to this USC is measured via second harmonic generation imaging, which reveals a substantial (greater than 400%) enhancement of signal in the cavity environment and a distinct modification of the crystalline symmetry, indicating the onset and enhancement of ferroelectricity. We interpret these observations as resulting from renormalized piezoelectric and flexoelectric couplings, which in turn arise from the cavity-modified dielectric environment. This interpretation is supported with microscopic modeling via DFT-based multi-scale dynamics simulations and by macroscopic Ginzburg-Landau theory. Altogether, our experimental and theoretical results firmly confirm that cavity engineering can indeed be used to create new ground states in materials and enhance existing ones. This work establishes a new paradigm for materials engineering with direct applications for quantum transduction, quantum sensing, and nonlinear integrated photonics.

Moiré metals and continuum models beyond a single valley

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-11-02 20:00 EST

Matthew Albert, Andrew Klekovkin-Hardy, Valentin Crépel

We derive continuum models of moiré materials valid over the full monolayer Brillouin zone, as required for metals whose large Fermi surfaces are not confined to a single valley. To this end, we introduce an environment-resolved two-center approximation, in which each hopping amplitude depends not only on the distance between the orbitals it connects but also on its position within the moiré cell. Taking the local environment to be set by the disregistry of the two layers alone, these moiré couplings and their full set of harmonics can be learnt from commensurate bilayers at uniform stackings, without fitting. The method shows excellent agreement with ab initio band structures on moiré cells containing a few hundred atoms. Applied to twisted NbSe$ _2$ , it yields a continuum model in which moiré effects concentrate at the van Hove singularities, a single-particle starting point for studying moiré-tuned superconducting, charge-density-wave instabilities and their competition.

Singlet-Triplet Transition of Rotating Moiré Atoms in a Magnetic Field

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-11-02 20:00 EST

Conor Smith, Yubo Yang, Shiwei Zhang, Miguel A. Morales

Moiré superlattices can bind several electrons to each potential minimum, forming few-electron “moiré atoms.” Using neural quantum states that obey magnetic periodic boundary conditions, include full Landau-level mixing, and treat both spin sectors, we map the ground state of two-electron moiré atoms in a superlattice as a function of moiré depth and magnetic field strength. At strong field each atom is a spin-triplet maximum-density droplet with canonical angular momentum $ \langle L_z\rangle=-1$ and a persistent circulating current: the rotating counterpart of the “Wigner molecular crystal” recently imaged in moiré superlattices. Deepening the wells drives a singlet-triplet transition to non-rotating spin-singlet atoms whose only current is a weak diamagnetic counter-circulation. The transition moves to deeper wells as the field grows, so depth and field together open a window for the formation of rotors. This is the collective analog of the singlet-triplet transition of two-electron quantum dots. At the transition, the orbital magnetic moments of the atoms reverse sign as their spin polarization switches, a joint signature accessible to magnetometry. If the atoms are instead polarized by other means, such as magnetic proximity coupling, the rotors lock at weak field into a spontaneously ordered crystal of dimers in a pinwheel pattern.

Theory of Convective Diffusion

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-11-02 20:00 EST

Žiga Krajnik, Takato Yoshimura, Alvise Bastianello, Enej Ilievski

We obtain a hydrodynamic theory of diffusive-scale dynamics in one-dimensional systems with interacting sound modes in the absence of self-interactions. Chaotic systems generically feature three distinct Gaussian relaxation processes: besides normal diffusion, driven by microscopic noise stemming from fast-decaying non-hydrodynamic modes, collisions between sound modes lead to two additional channels: convective diffusion manifesting as an effective multiplicative temporal noise, and an effective additive noise generated by the surrounding hydrodynamic modes. Our theory also includes, as a subclass, integrable systems described by generalized hydrodynamics, representing a pure realization of convective diffusion. Focusing on convective diffusion, we obtain an exact closed-form expression for the higher-point dynamical correlation functions and contrast their structure with those of normal diffusion. Additionally, we show that in convective diffusion each sound mode features a universal non-Gaussian distribution of charge transfer in its comoving frame, thereby establishing anomalous fluctuations as a characteristic feature of diffusive-scale dynamics in integrable systems.

Ferromagnetism and altermagnetism both break time-reversal symmetry, yet their distinct transformation properties under crystalline symmetries forbid direct bilinear coupling between their order parameters. Our results reveal how two symmetry-distinct magnetic orders can retain separate ordering tendencies while remaining strongly coupled, providing a route towards internal control of altermagnetic domain states.

Observation of light-induced Haldane gap in graphene

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-11-02 20:00 EST

Fei Wang, Xuanxi Cai, Wanying Chen, Xiao Tang, Jinze Li, Benshu Fan, Tianshuang Sheng, Jinxi Lu, Jiansong Li, Hongyun Zhang, Pu Yu, Peizhe Tang, Wenhui Duan, Angel Rubio, Zhiyuan Sun, Shuyun Zhou

Symmetry and topology, encoded in electronic wavefunctions, play a central role in the electronic properties and emergent phenomena of quantum materials. While these properties are conventionally determined by the equilibrium Hamiltonian, strong optical fields offer a fundamentally different route by reshaping the Hamiltonian on ultrafast timescales, turning symmetry and topology into dynamically controllable degrees of freedom. Graphene provides a paradigmatic platform for realizing this concept, where circularly polarized light has long been predicted to dynamically break time-reversal symmetry (TRS) and open a topologically nontrivial gap at the Dirac point. Despite extensive experimental efforts, direct observation of this Dirac gap-the defining spectroscopic signature of the dynamical TRS-breaking induced topological phase-has remained missing. Here we directly observe this light-induced Dirac gap in monolayer graphene. Time- and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy reveals that the gap emerges simultaneously with the optical field and exclusively under circularly polarized driving, directly linking its opening to dynamical TRS breaking. Together with Floquet theory, we identify the gap with a light-induced Haldane mass and reveal the associated nontrivial Floquet Berry curvature and Chern topology. By demonstrating dynamical TRS breaking in a prototypical non-magnetic material, our results establish optical driving as a route to ultrafast engineering of symmetry and topology, opening opportunities for dynamically tunable quantum matter beyond equilibrium.

Resolving the spinon hierarchy of the Heisenberg antiferromagnet

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-11-02 20:00 EST

A local spin fluctuation in the spin-$ \tfrac12$ Heisenberg antiferromagnet creates an even number of fractional spinon excitations. We resolve its zero-temperature dynamical structure factor through six spinons directly in the thermodynamic limit. Beyond the established two-spinon contribution of $ 72.89%$ , the four-spinon response gives approximately $ 21.90%$ of the integrated intensity and six spinons contribute a further $ 4.3%$ , bringing the response to about $ 99%$ of both the intensity and first-frequency-moment sum rules.
