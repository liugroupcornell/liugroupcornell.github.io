Carbon radical generation by irreversible metal-to-ligand charge transfer

Original Paper | Synthetic chemistry methodology | 2026-11-03 19:00 EST

Zhiqiang Wei, Xiang Li, Lin Xiao, Yu He, Zhao Liu, Kaidi Zhu, Chuanfa Ni, Xiao-Song Xue, Jinbo Hu

Metal-to-ligand charge transfer (MLCT) is a fundamental photoinduced process that underpins a broad range of energy- or electron-transfer events1,2. In the absence of productive substrate engagement, the MLCT excited state typically relaxes to the ground state via photophysical deactivation, reflecting its largely reversible character3,4. Here we report a class of isolable copper-heteroaryl sulfone complexes that, upon visible light irradiation, undergo an irreversible MLCT-driven fragmentation through C-S bond cleavage within the coordinated sulfone ligand, generating carbon-centered radicals. Spectroscopic and computational studies support the formation of MLCT-derived excited states and a low-barrier pathway for C-S bond cleavage. This reactivity is largely independent of the reduction potentials of the corresponding free sulfones, enabling the generation of fluoroalkyl and non-fluorinated tertiary, secondary, and primary (even methyl) alkyl radicals. We demonstrate the utility of this platform in copper-catalyzed C-H fluoroalkylation of arenes and terminal alkynes. Leveraging these MLCT-generated trifluoromethyl radicals to initiate halogen-atom transfer (XAT) from perfluorocyclobutyl iodide (I-FCB), we also achieve radical perfluorocyclobutylation-iodination of alkenes, addressing a longstanding challenge in the incorporation of perfluorocyclobutyl motifs.

A two-dimensional reticular triaxial molecularly woven fabric

Fraser Stoddart, Jonathan L. Sessler, Guangfeng Li, Feihe Huang

Inspired by macroscopic weaving techniques1,2,3,4, molecular weaving, which interlaces molecular strands into ordered networks, has emerged as a powerful strategy for designing functional materials with emergent properties5,6. However, so far, molecular weaving has relied mainly on linear building blocks and biaxial structures. Higher-order weaving (≥3 axes) of nonlinear building blocks is expected to produce intriguing architectures and bring new and unforeseen functions to the resulting materials; however, their construction and property investigation remain underexplored. Here we report a two-dimensional reticular triaxial woven polymer network constructed by incorporating C3-symmetric macrocycles into the woven structure through dative B-N polymerization. The resulting material not only exhibits unique topological chirality and robust mechanical properties but it also serves as a porous material that permits the separation of representative aromatic/aliphatic compounds. This functional utility highlights how higher-order triaxial woven topologies can provide unique advantages in terms of regulating material properties and introduces an extra topological dimension for the construction of functional materials.

Observation of prethermal dynamical phase transitions and resonant melting

Original Paper | Quantum simulation | 2026-11-03 19:00 EST

Siva Darbha, Alexey Khudorozhkov, Pedro L. S. Lopes, Fangli Liu, Ermal Rrapaj, Jan Balewski, Majd Hamdan, Pavel E. Dolgirev, Alexander Schuckert, Katherine Klymko, Sheng-Tao Wang, Mikhail D. Lukin, Daan Camps, Milan Kornjača

The dynamics of isolated quantum systems following a sudden quench has a central role in many areas of material science, high-energy physics and quantum chemistry1,2,3,4. Such far-from-equilibrium quantum dynamics–which features complex phenomena with implications for thermalization, non-equilibrium phase transitions and Floquet phase engineering5,6,7,8–is challenging to study numerically, in particular, in high-dimensional systems9,10. Here we use a programmable neutral-atom quantum simulator11 to systematically explore quench dynamics in spin models with up to 180 qubits. By initializing the system in a product state and performing quenches across a broad parameter space, we discover several stable, qualitatively distinct dynamical regimes. We trace their robustness to Floquet-like prethermal steady states8 that are stabilized over exponentially long emergent timescales by strong dynamical constraints. We further observe sharp peaks in the dynamical response that are quantitatively explained by the structured melting of prethermalization through resonances. In two dimensions, we uncover a prethermally driven, first-order dynamical phase transition linked to the proliferation of Néel-order defects6,12, and probe it quantitatively using a generalized Clausius-Clapeyron test. Together, these results establish a programmable route to high-energy prethermal phase structure: long-lived dynamical phases, their resonant breakdown and a first-order dynamical phase transition that is not inherited from the equilibrium phase boundaries.

Ecological and social cues orchestrate concerted courtship in Drosophila

Original Paper | Social behaviour | 2026-11-03 19:00 EST

Philipp Brand, Katharine Keller, Rory T. Coleman, Noelle B. Eghbali, Sarah Zylka, Lucia L. Prieto-Godino, Vanessa Ruta

Mating decisions are often attributed to the sensory signalling between prospective sexual partners. Yet these interactions are also shaped by the broader environmental context in which they unfold, to appropriately align sexual arousal with reproductive opportunities1,2,3,4,5,6. Here we show that in Drosophila erecta mating is strictly contingent on the ecological and social environment generated as flies densely aggregate in groups on a food patch. We find that food volatiles directly promote male sexual arousal, triggering individuals to sample and pursue potential mates, giving rise to dynamic interactions across the group. Together, our findings highlight how environmental cues not directly tied to sexual signalling can profoundly influence reproductive behaviour and reorganize the social landscape to ensure mating occurs in contexts where reproductive opportunities are abundant.

The load-bearing capacity of intervertebral discs (IVDs) of the spine depends on a hierarchical extracellular matrix, where plywood-like lamellae of collagen fibres in the annulus fibrosus contain nanometre-scale fibrils built from staggered triple-helical monomers4,9,10. Tracing the spread of Celtic languages using ancient genomics

Original Paper | Population genetics | 2026-11-03 19:00 EST

Hugh McColl, Guus Kroonen, Thomaz Pinotti, William Barrie, John Koch, Johan Ling, Jean-Paul Demoule, Kristian Kristiansen, Martin Sikora, Eske Willerslev

Celtic languages are today restricted to Europe’s Atlantic fringes but were widely spoken across the continent during the Iron Age1,2,3. How Celtic achieved this early distribution remains contested4, with alternative archaeological and linguistic models proposing different homelands and dispersal routes. Here we analyse genome-wide data from 4,587 ancient individuals, focusing on Bronze and Iron Age Europe, to test the extent to which these models fit reconstructed demographic histories. We identify three main processes shaping European population structure that are relevant to the Celtic dispersal. First, the arrival of Steppe-related ancestry with Bell Beaker-associated groups after around 4400 bp (calibrated years before present or 1950 ce) across the region in which Celtic and related languages were later spoken. Second, a movement from southwestern Europe into Britain during the Bronze Age (3800-3200 bp), consistent with scenarios in which Celtic formed along the Atlantic façade. Third, a westward expansion of ancestry related to Late Bronze Age Knovíz-Urnfield-associated populations from eastern central Europe (3200-2800 bp), affecting France, the British Isles, northeastern Iberia and Italy and later associated with Iron Age Hallstatt and La Tène contexts5,6,7,8. Although all three movements have parallels with proposed models, we argue that the final movement, from eastern central Europe, is most parsimonious for the spread of Celtic. Although human history does not necessarily follow a parsimonious trajectory9 and language shifts need not track genetic change, these results delimit the demographic scenarios that are plausible for the emergence of Celtic in prehistoric Europe.
