Niobium oxide dihalide films show a second-harmonic generation (SHG) efficiency scaling with their thickness, owing to their weak interlayer electronic coupling, but their use in integrated nonlinear photonics has been limited by the lack of robust electrical tuneability. Here we integrate ferroelectric NbOBr2, of thicknesses ranging from 20 nm to 40 nm, with a silicon nitride microring resonator to realize electrically reconfigurable SHG on a photonic chip. NbOBr2 combines strong second-order susceptibility from intrinsic inversion-symmetry breaking with electrically switchable in-plane ferroelectric polarisation. Using the fundamental and frequency-converted outputs as complementary channels, we further demonstrate dual-channel edge detection.

Bound states in the continuum through quantum interference in ultracold atomic collisions

Original Paper | Matter waves and particle beams | 2026-11-08 19:00 EST

Alexander Guthmann, Louisa Marie Kienesberger, Felix Lang, Eleonora Lippi, Artur Widera

Quantum-mechanical interaction potentials support localized bound states below the dissociation threshold or delocalized scattering states above it. Although bound states are energetically isolated, scattering states couple a quantum system to environmental modes, making dissipation and loss intrinsic to open systems. Bound states in the continuum are an exception, as destructive interference keeps them localized within the scattering continuum. Theory predicts that such states can arise through interference between two Feshbach resonances coupled to a common continuum. Here we form such an interference-stabilized state in ultracold collisions of 6Li atoms by coherently coupling two tunable Feshbach resonances using Floquet engineering. At a critical parameter point, elastic and inelastic coupling to the continuum vanish, producing a molecular state above the dissociation threshold. Consequently, resonant scattering features disappear and the colliding atoms become effectively transparent to one another. Full coupled-channel calculations and a minimal non-Hermitian model quantitatively reproduce our observations and identify the state as a Friedrich-Wintgen bound state in the continuum. These results show how quantum interference can control openness in quantum matter and enable the engineering of non-Hermitian Hamiltonians.

Active-flow-driven DNA remodelling generates millimetre-scale mechanical oscillations

Original Paper | Self-assembly | 2026-11-08 19:00 EST

Maya Levanon, Noa S. Goldberg, Dvir Cohen, Eran Bouchbinder, Ram M. Adar, Alexandra M. Tayar

DNA undergoes continuous and rhythmic mechanical remodelling through condensation, looping and disentangling to regulate gene expression, segregate chromosomes and guide morphogenesis. Here we demonstrate a purely mechanical route to rhythmic DNA reorganization in a minimal active composite of microtubules, kinesin motors and DNA. We embed DNA polymers in an active-turbulent microtubule-kinesin fluid, creating a self-morphing material. The active flows stretch and entangle the DNA, forming a self-organized viscoelastic network that resists active stresses and affects flow over large length scales. This mechanical feedback loop progressively amplifies velocity correlations and drives a sharp non-equilibrium transition tuned by DNA contour length: from disordered flow to synchronized, millimetre-scale oscillatory flow. We rationalize the sharp transition with an active-gel model that predicts a growing length scale and an oscillatory instability emerging from the interplay between activity, orientational order and self-generated viscoelasticity, rather than chemical signalling. The theoretical dependence of the oscillation frequency on system size and activity is in agreement with observations. Thus, flow-driven DNA remodelling provides a minimal physical route to autonomous, system-spanning oscillations in three dimensions and suggests design principles for programmable soft matter that coordinates, actuates and reshapes itself.

Transformer-Based Characterization of Criticality in Directed Percolation

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-11-09 20:00 EST

Bozheng Dou, Wei Li

Directed percolation (DP) provides a canonical framework for studying nonequilibrium phase transitions into absorbing states, yet extracting critical behavior directly from high-dimensional spatiotemporal configurations remains challenging. Here, we investigate (1 + 1)- and (2 + 1)-dimensional bond DP using a supervised Transformer encoder. Supervised learning results demonstrate that the Transformer can distinguish between the active and absorbing phases of directed percolation with high accuracy. The critical point and critical exponents estimated from the model’s classification probabilities align closely with reference values from existing literature, indicating that the model not only achieves phase classification but also yields outputs containing information related to critical scaling behavior. To probe the model’s internal representation, we employ attention rollout and define an attention center that quantifies the temporal centroid of the cumulative attention distribution. The attention patterns and the attention center reorganize systematically near the critical region and exhibit pronounced finite-size dependence. Finally, we examined the model’s transferability between configurations generated from fully occupied initial states and those from single-seed initial states. The results indicate the presence of transferable information between the two types of data, though the transfer performance exhibits directional asymmetry. Furthermore, when using an equal amount of target-domain data, fine-tuning yielded only a marginal improvement in accuracy compared to training from scratch.

Nonequilibrium Fluctuations and Intrinsic Stability of Nanostructured Solar Cells: Microscopic Derivation and Universal Laws

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-11-09 20:00 EST

Rivo Herivola Manjakamanana Ravelonjato, Ravo Tokiniaina Ranaivoson, Philipe Manjakasoa Randriantsoa, Raoelina Andriambololona, Wilfrid Chrysante Solofoarisina

We study nonequilibrium fluctuations of efficiency in nanostructured solar cells. Our contribution is threefold. First, we derive a microscopic stochastic model of efficiency from a two-state master equation describing absorption, radiative recombination, and extraction. This derivation establishes an explicit link between efficiency fluctuations and entropy production, and justifies the thermodynamic affinity through local detailed balance. Second, we review universal fluctuation laws established in the recent literature and apply them for the first time to the real parameters of nanostructured solar cells. We show that the total entropy production behaves as a submartingale, and that its negative exponential is a martingale. For a biased discrete random walk, the exact infimum law is verified numerically in the near-equilibrium regime, then applied to realistic solar cell configurations. Third, our simulations show that nanostructured solar cells operate in a strongly nonequilibrium regime, where negative entropy fluctuations, and hence efficiency fluctuations, are exponentially rare. Over all simulated trajectories, most configurations show no detectable negative fluctuation, consistent with the theoretical prediction. These results explain the intrinsic statistical stability of the devices and provide a conceptual and quantitative framework for understanding efficiency fluctuations around the mean values. They do not modify the mean efficiencies obtained previously, but clarify their statistical robustness in the strongly nonequilibrium regime.

Interacting particles crossing barriers: spatial statistics, choice of route taken and role of interactions

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-11-09 20:00 EST

Gulzar Ahmad, Andrew J Archer, Sergey Savel’ev, Marco G Mazza

We consider the statistics of pairs of particles moving in a potential energy landscape exhibiting a choice of different pathways from a local minimum start point to the global energy minimum destination. We examine the impact of both attractive and repulsive interactions between the particles, treating these via either a soft Gaussian or a Lennard-Jones potential. We investigate the statistics of which particle arrives at the destination first, arrival-time distributions and where in the system the second particle is located when the first arrives. We find that the typical arrival time of the first particle can be significantly different to that of a single particle, sometimes being much longer and in other cases being shorter, depending on the nature of the particle interactions. In the case of soft Gaussian attractive interactions, which effectively bind the pair of particles together to form a single larger particle, the arrival time can be much longer, since bigger particles cross barriers more slowly. In contrast, the arrival time is shorter when there is a repulsive core interaction between the particles, due to excluded-area and particle-ordering effects. We investigate the probability distribution of particles’ positions through the Jensen–Shannon divergence which unveils the dependence of the spatial distribution of the particles within the confining potential on the parameters of the particle interaction potentials.

Devitrification: crystal nucleation and growth mechanisms in glasses

New Submission | Soft Condensed Matter (cond-mat.soft) | 2026-11-09 20:00 EST

Max Brown, Peter Wolynes

If a liquid is cooled rapidly, it may fall out of equilibrium before it can crystallize. It may, however, crystallize later from the nonequilibrium glassy state. Using the Random First-Order Transition (RFOT) theory of glasses, we discuss a variety of mechanisms of crystallization as a route to devitrification. Ordinary nucleation is sped up by the frozen in energy of the glass relative to the liquid. At sufficiently low temperatures, a mechanism of nanonucleation appears, producing nanometer scale crystal inclusions in the glass. Nanocrystallization depends strongly on the local fluctuations in stability, and the transition from classical nucleation and growth to nanocrystallization occurs dominantly through rare, less stable glassy regions that percolate through space, producing an additional dramatic speedup in the crystal nucleation and growth rates. Similar fluctuations in local stability also explain anomalies of crystal growth in deeply supercooled systems.

New Submission | Materials Science (cond-mat.mtrl-sci) | 2026-11-09 20:00 EST

James R. Kermode

Machine-learned interatomic potentials are misspecified, so Bayesian posterior variances understate their errors. For linear atomic cluster expansion (ACE) potentials we derive calibrated uncertainties from one misspecification-robust parameter covariance, the delete-one-configuration jackknife. Scaled by one conformal quantile from a held-out part of the training set, it gives per-atom force uncertainties close to their target coverage on held-out silicon data and on cracks left out of the fit; screw dislocations left out are under-covered by the smaller of the two models. With one scale fitted to the forces of a held-out test split, the same parameter distribution is propagated to 21 properties of silicon, from elastic constants to defect and surface energies, by implicit differentiation through each relaxation and by committees, and is tested against density functional theory (DFT) without tuning on any property. The Bayesian posterior with one calibrated scale has the wrong shape: it covers fewer properties and has far lower joint evidence. Lucy A. Valdez, Juan D. Ruiz Díaz, Rodolfo H. Romero, Víctor J. Toranzos

Memristors based on electrochemical metallization with many filaments are usually modeled by microscopic simulations with many parameters or by phenomenological laws whose parameters are hard to relate to physical quantities. We present an analytical mean-field model for a population of Ag filaments confined in the pores of a nanoporous matrix, built on Ohm’s law, Faraday’s law and a power-based rupture criterion. The filaments have a truncated normal distribution of cross-sections and rupture leaving lognormally distributed gaps whose median grows with the dissipated power.
