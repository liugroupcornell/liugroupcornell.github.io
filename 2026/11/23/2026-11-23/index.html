Hexa-substituted benzene synthesis with non-classical carbocations

Original Paper | Chemistry | 2026-11-22 19:00 EST

Guillaume Mollaert, Hang-Fei Tu, Jacob R. King, Mihai V. Popescu, Zexian Li, Quan Zhang, Eric Palomo, Robert S. Paton, Marcos G. Suero

Benzene rings are the most frequently encountered ring system in small-molecule drugs1. However, benzenes with six substituents are notably absent from small molecule libraries, owing to challenges associated with their synthesis and the limited availability of commercially available aromatic building blocks2. Therefore, new chemical processes that enable their synthesis with predictable regiochemical control and broad substituent diversity could enable medicinal chemists to explore this uncharted aromatic chemical space in hit-to-lead and lead optimization campaigns. Herein, we describe a highly modular and programmable platform for accessing hexa-substituted benzene rings from aromatic cyclopropenium cations and (trialkylsilyl)allenes, with precise control over all six benzene positions encoded in the starting materials. The exceptional regioselectivity observed originates from the selective evolution of an unusual non-classical carbocation featuring four-centre four-electron bonding as revealed by density functional theory calculations. Our benzannulation enables the selective installation of synthetic handles that were exploited for the synthesis of previously inaccessible penta- and hexa-substituted analogues of the anticancer drug sonidegib and the antihypertensive drug telmisartan, as well as the polyaromatic hydrocarbon triphenylene. Notably, the use of enantiopure allenes unlocks a new route to enantioenriched multi-substituted biaryls through our benzannulation occurring with “memory of chirality”.

Continuous N-to-C Peptide Synthesis

Original Paper | Synthetic chemistry methodology | 2026-11-22 19:00 EST

The method enabled the synthesis of a tirzepatide heptapeptide fragment, degarelix, a 14-mer engineered macrocyclic peptide, and semaglutide. Overall, continuous NCPS has the potential to transform peptide manufacturing.

Architecture of the poxvirus early-transcription machinery and portal complex

Original Paper | Cryoelectron tomography | 2026-11-22 19:00 EST

Benoît Arragain, Lukas W. Bauer, Victor-Valentin Hodirnau, Florian K. M. Schur

Poxviruses enclose a complete viral transcription machinery alongside their dsDNA genome within the viral core, a transcription-competent compartment delimited by a permeable multi-layered protein shell. Upon infection of a cell and core release, this machinery almost immediately starts synthesizing early mRNAs, which are extruded across the core wall into the cytoplasm for translation. While in vitro structures of the isolated viral DNA-dependent RNA polymerase (vRNAP) have been described1-3, its native in virio architecture, spatial organization, and physical coupling to the genome remain unknown. Using cryo-electron tomography and sub-tomogram averaging on purified Vaccinia virus cores, we reveal that packaged vRNAP exists not as monomer, but as massive 1.8 MDa resting-state dimer bridged by fully ordered vaccinia virus early transcription factor (VETF). We also identify a subpopulation of these dimers bound to the essential kinase VPK2 and the core protein L4, which directly tether the transcribing engines to the packaged genome near the inner core wall. Furthermore, we determine a sub-nanometer structure of the putative poxvirus mRNA extrusion portal complex, revealing a rigid assembly integrated into the core wall comprising an E8 hexameric crown, an E6 hexameric central hub, and a basal L3 dodecameric channel suited for nucleic acid export. Together, these structures provide a comprehensive architectural blueprint of important components of the poxvirus core and open new structural and functional avenues for investigating poxvirus genome packaging, transcription activation, and mRNA extrusion.

Depth-robust super-resolution nanofabrication for 3D mesoscopic photonic structures

Mesoscopic optical systems underpin photonic interconnects, on-chip metrology, quantum computing and miniature microscopy, but monolithic integration of these components is currently limited by fabrication methodologies that only offer limited depth control. A unified closed-form theory for elastic waves in polycrystals of any crystal symmetry

New Submission | Materials Science (cond-mat.mtrl-sci) | 2026-11-23 20:00 EST

Ming Huang, Stanislav I. Rokhlin, Michael J. S. Lowe

The coherent elastic wave in random polycrystals is described by the first-order smoothing approximation, whose mass operator remains an integral evaluated only numerically. Here we derive its closed form for equiaxed, untextured polycrystals of any crystal symmetry with an exponential correlation function, giving an explicit dispersion equation at all frequencies. Unlike the integral form, which fails beyond one inverse correlation radius from the real axis, the closed form holds deep in the complex wavenumber plane, where heavily attenuated roots lie. It is compact, reproduces the dispersion equations of Stanke and Kino for cubic grains, extends them to any symmetry and contains the Born and far-field approximations as special cases. It reveals the structure of the roots and their partitions of the coherent field: three roots per polarization, one from the incident wave and one from each of the two scattering channels, without and with mode conversion. It also yields closed-form limits of their velocity, attenuation and partition in the Rayleigh, stochastic and geometric regimes, which show why a single coherent wave has been observed at long wavelengths, whereas more than one can be observed at short wavelengths. These results underpin further theory and quantitative characterization of polycrystalline microstructure.

Topologically Obstructed Kondo Hybridization: A Route to Kondo Semimetals

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-11-23 20:00 EST

Yue Liu, Junze Deng, Haoyu Hu

The interplay of strong correlations, topology, and symmetry can reshape the low-energy electronic structure of quantum materials and drive the emergence of novel quantum phases. In heavy-fermion systems, the Kondo effect entangles localized $ f$ moments with conduction electrons, giving rise to coherent heavy-electron bands at low temperatures, typically characterized by a Kondo hybridization gap. Here we show, however, that the topology and symmetry of the conduction-electron Fermi surface can obstruct the opening of the Kondo hybridization gap at certain momenta. Treating the Fermi surface of a three-dimensional system as an effective two-dimensional manifold, we demonstrate that nontrivial topological invariants defined on this manifold impose a topological obstruction to opening a full hybridization gap, forcing the $ fc$ hybridization to vanish at certain momenta on the Fermi surface. These hybridization zeros lead to crossings between $ c$ and $ f$ bands, giving rise to nodal points or lines near the Fermi energy within the Kondo phase. Our results establish topological obstructions to Kondo hybridization as a route to Kondo semimetals, revealing a fundamental connection between Fermi-surface topology and the emergence of correlated electronic phases.

Off-diagonal long-range order and $p$-wave pairing in one dimension from critical density fluctuations

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-11-23 20:00 EST

Matthew D. Thomson, Simon M. Martin, Frank Marsiglio, Joseph Maciejko

A central theme in the theory of unconventional superconductivity is that Cooper pairing can be mediated by bosonic collective modes other than lattice vibrations. However, collective fluctuations in reduced dimensions can also have a destabilizing effect on superconductivity, by suppressing long-range order or by destroying Fermi-liquid quasiparticles in the normal state. In this work, we study a one-dimensional (1D) model of spinless fermions with local interactions that exhibits a continuous quantum phase transition from a Luttinger liquid to a phase-separated state. Combining field-theoretic arguments with numerical exact diagonalization and matrix-product-state methods, we find that the quantum critical point exhibits two-particle off-diagonal long-range order and $ p$ -wave pairing. Our work shows that critical density fluctuations at a phase-separation instability can act as pairing glue for unconventional superconductivity in 1D, overcoming the disordering effect of phase fluctuations and the absence of long-lived fermionic quasiparticles in the normal state.

Chen-Shen Lee, Itamar Kimchi

Chiral (or sublattice) symmetry describes bipartite systems such as the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) chain where same-sublattice A-A and B-B hoppings are forbidden, enabling the protection of topological phases. When the magnetic force theorem fails: dressed exchange in the induced-moment magnets FePt, FePd, CoPt, and CoPd

New Submission | Materials Science (cond-mat.mtrl-sci) | 2026-11-23 20:00 EST

Vladislav Borisov, Maryna Pankratova, Rohit Pathak, Pamela Costa Carvalho, Erna K. Delczeg-Czirjak, Sagar Sarkar, Anders Bergman, Qichen Xu, Nezhat Pournaghavi, Manuel Pereiro, Hermann Dürr, Mikhail I. Katsnelson, Olle Eriksson, Anna Delin

Effective spin Hamiltonians for itinerant magnets are commonly parameterized by magnetic-force-theorem (MFT) exchange constants, which are subsequently used to compute the behavior of complex and delicate spin textures, for example skyrmions. We show that for systems containing atoms with large spin susceptibility, such as Pd or Pt, this approach misses important parts of the energetics. For FePt, it even predicts the wrong ground state. Benchmarking against fully self-consistent spin-spiral density-functional calculations - in which the electronic structure, including the induced Pd/Pt moments, relaxes for every spin configuration - we quantify the missing energetics for the ordered L1$ _0$ alloys FePt, FePd, CoPt, and CoPd. We derive a general bound showing that no on-site (intra-atomic) refinement can supply the missing strength. The dressing decides the magnetic ground state in FePt, where the interlayer ordering rests entirely on the Pt-mediated interactions, and shifts the Curie temperatures of the other alloys by several hundred kelvin. Computed ordering temperatures, magnon spectra and spin wave stiffness for all four compounds are provided.

Large deviation theory provides a natural framework to characterize rare fluctuations of time-averaged observables in deterministic and stochastic systems. Geometric properties of Wilsonian flows of periodic potentials in two dimensions

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-11-23 20:00 EST

Daniel G. Barci, Nathan O. Silvano

Periodic interactions can possess multiple competing minima and barriers, whose evolution under coarse-graining offers a geometric perspective complementary to the scaling of Fourier harmonics. We study this evolution in two dimensions by deriving a functional Wilsonian renormalization-group flow for a general smooth periodic potential and its running sine-Gordon frequency within a constant-kinetic projection. Reaction–diffusion and Burgers representations reveal two key properties of the flow: discrete translation symmetries are preserved, and the number of extrema can only remain constant or decrease. Consequently, the nonlinear generation of Fourier harmonics may accompany a progressive simplification of the potential landscape. We illustrate the role of symmetry with two complementary examples. A weak breaking of the $ \pi$ -translation symmetry allows a reduction from four extrema to two, whereas a non-sinusoidal interaction retaining this symmetry evolves from eight extrema to the four protected by its periodicity. These results highlight how symmetry and coarse-graining jointly reorganize competing minima and barriers in periodic interactions.

Prediction of lattice thermal conductivity of half-Heusler compounds by machine learning and transfer-function calibration

New Submission | Materials Science (cond-mat.mtrl-sci) | 2026-11-23 20:00 EST

Shivam Randive, Ojaswini Randive, Heet Lungaria, Himanshu Pandey

High-throughput searches for half-Heusler thermoelectrics increasingly rely on lattice thermal conductivity ($ \kappa_L$ ) from first-principles calculations, or from machine-learning models trained on them. These calculations describe an ideal single crystal, whereas measurements are made on polycrystalline samples, in which grain boundaries and defects scatter heat as well. The resulting gap between calculated and measured $ \kappa_L$ has been noted for individual compounds but not, to our knowledge, quantified across a materials class, so the values used for screening carry an unknown, systematic bias. Here we quantify and correct this bias for cubic half-Heusler compounds with 18 valence electrons. Using a curated literature corpus that keeps calculated and measured values apart, we show that calculations overestimate measured $ \kappa_L$ in most bonding families, and that the excess shrinks with temperature. Scattering by defects, added to the intrinsic scattering between lattice vibrations, would give such a trend, but the data do not single out one cause. A simple transfer function with this temperature dependence removes the median offset, and refitting its magnitude within a bonding family lowers the median error further, though not consistently compound by compound. Combined with a machine-learning model that predicts the calculated value from crystal structure, and tested on chemistries withheld from all fitting, the method predicts measured $ \kappa_L$ in this class about as closely as independent laboratories agree with one another, and provides testable predictions for compounds not yet measured.

New Submission | Materials Science (cond-mat.mtrl-sci) | 2026-11-23 20:00 EST

Pravan Omprakash, Nicholas Crnkovich, Nathan Curtis, John Cavin, Adrien Couet, Rohan Mishra

Refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCAs) offer a high-dimensional composition space for designing either robust single-phase solid solutions or controlled multiphase microstructures. However, most RCCA studies have examined isolated compositions, often near the equimolar point, rather than determining how solid-solution stability varies across the surrounding composition space. Here, we use rapid thermodynamic phase-field calculations to map the topology of solid-solution stability in RCCAs composed of Cr, Hf, Mo, Nb, Ta, Ti, V, W, and Zr. Benchmarking the model against 109 experimentally reported quaternary-to-senary compositions produced through diverse processing routes yields an overall classification accuracy of 80%. We then introduce percentage miscible region, a metric that separates robust solid-solution-forming systems from those exhibiting compositionally tunable microstructures. Analysis of ternary, quaternary, and quinary RCCAs reveals that higher-order miscibility is strongly inherited from constituent binary subsystems, indicating the importance of choosing suitable binary interactions when designing RCCAs. Element-specific trends further show that Ti, Ta, Nb, and Mo generally stabilize solid solutions, whereas Cr and Zr promote ordered precipitation and phase segregation.
