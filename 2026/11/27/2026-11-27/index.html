Supramolecular protein block copolymers encode presynaptic active-zone nanolattices

Original Paper | Nanostructures | 2026-11-26 19:00 EST

Hirokazu Sakamoto, Shunsuke F. Shimobayashi

Designing biological nanomaterials with programmable geometry and function remains a central challenge in nanotechnology. Presynaptic active zones offer a biological model of nanoscale architecture as their scaffold proteins spatially organize the machinery for neurotransmitter release. Here we show that the active-zone scaffold proteins ELKS and RIM form periodic nanodot lattices upon reconstitution in non-neuronal cells, with ELKS nanodot diameters of about 60 nm and a typical lattice spacing of about 130 nm. By combining block-copolymer theory with super-resolution nanoscopy and quantitative spatial analysis of reconstituted assemblies and neuronal synapses, we identify ELKS and RIM as a minimal scaffold module sufficient to generate hexagonal lattices through orthogonal self-association coupled with specific ELKS-RIM binding. We further demonstrate that changes in scaffold stoichiometry and incorporation of client proteins, including RIMBP2 and Liprin-α3, systematically alter lattice spacing and morphology. These findings establish molecular principles underlying active-zone architecture and provide a framework for engineering self-assembling nanomaterials with programmable spatial organization.

Dual-cation solvation-tailored electrolyte solutions for low-temperature 48-V/30-Ah lithium-ion battery modules

Original Paper | Batteries | 2026-11-26 19:00 EST

Jia-Yan Liang, Xiaowei Liu, Yanyan Zhang, Ya You, Yang Xia, Bo-Zheng Liu, Qian Li, Yu Zhang, Ji-Lei Shi, Ji-Ling Yue, Yao Zhao, Bo Guan, Jun-Feng Xiang, Fuyi Wang, Wenkui Zhang, Chunhui Yang, Sen Xin, Jiecai Han, Yu-Guo Guo

Weakly solvated electrolyte solutions are promising candidates for low-temperature rechargeable Li-based batteries. Their weak Li+-solvent interactions facilitate solvent removal from the primary solvation sheath, whereas strong Li+-anion interactions often lead to sluggish Li-ion transport and salt aggregation at subzero temperatures. Here, through comprehensive physicochemical and electrochemical investigations, we identify two key solvation structures that overcome the long-standing trade-off between Li+ solvation and desolvation. First, the formation of dual-cation aggregates induced by the addition of 15 vol% of a strongly coordinating solvent, preferentially at the electrode|electrolyte interface, lowers the effective charge density of Li+ and reduces the desolvation energy barrier. Second, a weakly solvated salt-assisted core-shell primary solvation sheath enhances Li+ conductivity through rapid anion decoupling without compromising Li+ solvation. Leveraging these nanoengineered solvation structures, we formulate an electrolyte solution by introducing triethyl phosphate (TEP) and lithium difluoro(oxalate)borate into a conventional carbonate-based weakly solvated electrolyte. The resulting primary solvation sheath comprises a stable 2Li++TEP inner core surrounded by a loosely packed outer shell of DFOB- anions and carbonate solvents. This hierarchical solvation architecture enables rechargeable non-aqueous Li-based batteries with diverse electrode chemistries and cell configurations, ranging from coin cells to 48-V/30-Ah lithium-ion battery modules, as well as maintaining stable electrochemical operation down to -50 °C.

Differential active adhesion and a capillary instability drive global eversion and dissemination of bacterial colonies

Original Paper | Biophysics | 2026-11-26 19:00 EST

Stephan Wimmi, Isabelle Wielert, Kai Zhou, Marc Hennes, Benedikt Sabass, Berenike Maier

Attractive forces between cells can determine the shape and sorting behaviour of bacterial colonies. During colony development, chemical gradients form within the colony, but it is unclear how they affect cohesion. Here we discover that such gradients trigger global eversion of proliferating colonies formed by the human pathogen Neisseria gonorrhoeae. Like a jet, the inner core flows towards the periphery where it is partially dispersed and partially spreads around the core of the colony. Living dispersed cells reform colonies, leading to rapid dissemination. The eversion depends on local oxygen depletion, which reduces the activity of the molecular motors that govern self-attraction: before the eversion, the colony consists of a weakly cohesive spherical core surrounded by a strongly cohesive shell. An idealized computational model reveals that, in this configuration, an instability analogous to a capillary instability emerges when the thickness of the strongly interacting shell falls below a critical value. We show that the probability for eversion strongly depends on the motor-generated attractive force between bacteria. Overall, our results indicate that chemical gradients induce spontaneous symmetry breaking in a mechanically active colony, thereby triggering a nonlinear shape instability. This instability generates large-scale cellular fluxes that redistribute bacteria towards environments more favourable for their growth.

Theory of Anomalous Diffusion and Superdiffusion in Integrable Spin Chains

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-11-26 20:00 EST

Jitendra Kethepalli, Andrew Urilyon, Jacopo De Nardis

Integrable systems can exhibit diffusive or Kardar–Parisi–Zhang (KPZ) scaling in two-point correlations even when their current full counting statistics (FCS) differ from those usually associated with these universality classes. We explain this separation using a multispecies particle gas that preserves the scattering structure of integrable spin chains. For anisotropy $ \Delta>1$ , large particles are advected by a mobile bath. The resulting FCS has a Gaussian-mixture form and approaches the M-Wright distribution at late times, with finite-time rounding due to mobile species. At the isotropic point $ \Delta=1$ , all particles are mobile, and the charge transfer by large particles is discrete. A self-consistent Poisson distribution describes the FCS, with a nearly Gaussian bulk and model-dependent, non-Gaussian tails. By contrast, two-point correlations follow from the statistics of a large tracer particle. For $ \Delta>1$ , it undergoes Brownian motion, yielding Gaussian spreading. At $ \Delta=1$ , we provide evidence from fluctuating hydrodynamics for the emergence of the KPZ Prähofer–Spohn scaling function in dynamical correlations and trace it to the finite coupling of the largest particles to a continuum of slower ones, with long-ranged velocity fluctuations. Our framework shows how universal dynamical two-point correlations coexist with distinct model-dependent current statistics.

Pairing from anti-Hund’s coupling under infinite Hubbard repulsion

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-11-26 20:00 EST

Donggyu Kim, Ya-Hui Zhang

Electron-phonon coupling is a natural source of superconductivity, but in strongly correlated materials the phonon-mediated interaction is often much weaker than the on-site Hubbard repulsion $ U$ . Whether, and how, it can still produce pairing when $ U$ dominates is a basic open question. We address this question by studying a minimal model with an anti-Hund’s coupling $ J_A$ : an on-site spin exchange between two orbitals or two valleys, mediated by optical phonons, that favors a local inter-orbital spin singlet. In the limit $ U=+\infty$ , the two-orbital Hubbard model on the triangular lattice, doped away from the insulator at $ n=2$ electrons per site, reduces to a four-flavor $ t$ -$ J_A$ model. Density-matrix renormalization group calculations find robust pairing throughout $ 1\le J_A/|t|\le10$ , with the largest pairing gap at $ J_A\sim|t|$ . The Cooper pairs reside on bonds and do not come from a direct attraction provided by $ J_A$ , since infinite $ U$ excludes on-site pairs from the Hilbert space. For $ J_A\gg|t|$ , the normal state is a second Fermi liquid whose small Fermi surface violates the perturbative Luttinger theorem, and a strong-coupling expansion in $ t/J_A$ provides a controlled effective theory of superconductivity from this unconventional metal. Our results provide strong evidence for a strongly coupled superconductor beyond BCS theory, driven by the interplay of the kinetic energy, the phonon-mediated anti-Hund’s coupling, and the strong Hubbard $ U$ . Finally, we discuss possible applications to magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene.

Spontaneous Time-Reversal-Symmetry-Breaking State in Proximity-Induced Superconductivity

New Submission | Superconductivity (cond-mat.supr-con) | 2026-11-26 20:00 EST

Jonathan Schirmer, Wei Pan, Javad Shabani, Enrico Rossi

Recent experiments on superconducting heterostructures formed by an $ s$ -wave superconductor and a Dirac semimetal suggest that in these systems the superconducting state can break time-reversal symmetry even though no magnetic order or unconventional pairing is present in any of the structure’s components. We present a minimal mechanism for a time-reversal-symmetry-breaking superconducting state in multiband systems proximity-coupled to a conventional $ s$ -wave superconductor. We derive the conditions for this state, find that it is the ground state wherever it exists, and obtain the phase diagrams. We find that in some situations the phase diagrams describe reentrant behavior: by continuously varying a parameter the system can undergo multiple transitions between a time-reversal-symmetric state and a state that spontaneously breaks time-reversal symmetry. The results help identify heterostructures that can be engineered to obtain nonreciprocal superconducting transport effects even when no magnetic field or magnetization is present.

Can the chemical potential and the many-body correlated phases of a two-dimensional dipolar fluid be approximated analytically? A Quantum Monte-Carlo study of a universal behavior of dipolar excitons

New Submission | Quantum Gases (cond-mat.quant-gas) | 2026-11-26 20:00 EST

Yotam Mazuz-Harpaz, Michal Zimmerman, Snir Gazit, Ronen Rapaport

A central challenge in quantum many-body physics is to connect microscopic interactions to experimentally accessible macroscopic states of matter, where correlations extend across multiple length scales. Using quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) simulations of two-dimensional repulsive dipolar bosons, recently realized across various experimental platforms, we show that correlations within the dipolar fluid lead to an unexpected simplification. Specifically, we find that these correlations induce a pronounced depletion region around each particle that suppresses short-range physics and yields an emergent universality, where the fluid is governed by dipolar interactions rather than material-specific details. Exploiting this universality, we establish a unified, quantitatively validated analytical framework for the dipolar fluid, taking indirect (interlayer) excitons as a model system. We show that analytical interaction energies derived in an earlier study agree with exact QMC results across three correlation regimes: a classical gas, a quantum gas, and a high-density liquid. In contrast, the widely used uncorrelated plate capacitor formula severely overestimates the numerical values. Building on these expressions, we show that the chemical potential is accurately captured by a three-component decomposition: an ideal Bose-gas kinetic term, a regime-dependent dipolar interaction term, and a high-density quantum confinement term. Finally, we address a key experimental challenge: extracting exciton densities from measured energy shifts. Our QMC-benchmarked calibration curves for typical gallium arsenide (GaAs) and transition metal dichalcogenide (TMD) heterostructures offer a reliable tool for experimentalists to determine both the fluid’s density and collective state. This universal framework extends to other two-dimensional systems, including ultracold dipolar atoms and polar molecules.

Spin superfluid transport of magnons in altermagnetic insulators

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-11-26 20:00 EST

Dele Li, Meng-Han Zhang, Dao-Xin Yao

We study the spin superfluidity for Lieb and inverse Lieb lattices in altermagnets, which enables coherent transport of spin angular momentum carried by the phase of an easy-plane Néel order. The easy-plane anisotropy supplies the $ U(1)$ platform, while the nonrelativistic spin splitting generates sublattice-alternating exchange imbalance with $ B_{1g}$ symmetry. Via a continuum theory and Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert simulations, we find that the Goldstone mode becomes quadratic at the critical stability boundary. An axial phase gradient transfers phase-channel spectral weight to the gapped mode, whereas a diagonal flow leaves these signatures absent. The spin superfluid exhibits momentum-space anisotropy due to the chiral-splitting magnon dispersion, while strong fields or elevated temperatures suppress phase coherence. Accounting for the contribution of thermal magnons at the interface, we present that the critical spin flow varies with the injection angle under the altermagnetic crystal-field effects. The spin-superfluid decay length scales with the interfacial spin-mixing conductance, which can be measured by the spin Hall magnetoresistance and spin Seebeck effect. Our findings establish altermagnetic insulators, particularly $ \mathrm{La_2O_3FeMnSe_2}$ , as promising platforms for low-dissipation spin transport.

Bosonic Magic Resources at Quantum Criticality

New Submission | Quantum Gases (cond-mat.quant-gas) | 2026-11-26 20:00 EST

Paolo Stornati, Piotr Sierant, Emanuele Tirrito

Non-Gaussianity is a resource for bosonic quantum information processing, providing a necessary ingredient for universal quantum computation in continuous-variable systems. Here we quantify non-Gaussianity in the ground state of the one-dimensional extended Bose-Hubbard model using quantum relative entropy (QRE), a measure determined solely by one-body correlations. The scaling of the QRE with system size reveals quantum phase transitions involving hidden and conventional order. We connect QRE scaling in the weakly interacting superfluid phase to Luttinger-liquid correlations and identify distinct critical signatures in its extensive and subleading contributions. At the Mott-Haldane transition, subtracting the extensive background reveals a pronounced peak in the subleading contribution, providing a clear signature of the onset of hidden string order. At the Haldane-density-wave transition, the extensive contribution to the QRE instead exhibits scaling consistent with Ising criticality. These results establish non-Gaussianity as a probe of many-body criticality, linking bosonic quantum resources to strongly correlated matter.

Ying-Cheng Chen, Ahmad Ahmad, Anter El-Azab

Many multi-phase thin films grown by self-assembly involve co-deposition of both metallic and oxide phases that grow into the same film. Under such conditions, the oxide species may be present in stoichiometric molecular form on the surface of the metallic phase. Quantifying the stability, binding, and diffusion of such molecular oxide states on metallic surfaces is critical for understanding self-assembly. In this work, we investigate the binding and stability of CeO2 molecules on Au surfaces using spin-polarized density functional theory calculations. Specifically, we examine how the configuration of the CeO2 molecule, its binding energy and binding free energy, structural stability, effective charge, and projected electronic density of states vary among various binding sites on Au(001), Au(110), and Au(111) surfaces. Finally, three different cases of how the CeO2 molecule binds to Au(001), Au(110), and Au(111) were identified: case 1: chemisorption with symmetric bonds, case 2: chemisorption with asymmetric bonds, and case 3: physisorption with symmetric bonds. These three cases of CeO2 binding are present on Au(001) and Au(110), but only cases 2 and 3 occur on Au(111).

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-11-26 20:00 EST

We present a family of exact and efficient recurrence relations for the frequency moments of the single-electron addition spectral function of a generic single-band electron-phonon Hamiltonian with linear coupling to harmonic phonons. Extrinsic thermodynamic geometry, symmetry, and the cost of constraint

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-11-26 20:00 EST

Jordan R. Sawchuk, David A. Sivak

Driving a stochastic system in finite time costs excess work; exactly how much depends on which system parameters, and hence which paths in distribution space, a controller can access. In the friction-tensor formalism for slow driving, the space of equilibrium distributions is a Riemannian manifold and constraints on a controller define submanifolds. Whether a constrained controller is forced onto suboptimal paths is a question of the extrinsic thermodynamic geometry of the submanifold, which we develop for discrete systems. We show that submanifolds with zero extrinsic curvature can be identified from symmetries of the transition network, before any optimization. A minimum-work protocol on such a submanifold is therefore stationary in the excess work even when the constraints are lifted. The stationarity of symmetric optima holds exactly, beyond slow driving. In the slow-driving regime, we show that the ambient curvature transverse to a symmetric submanifold determines whether nearby symmetry-broken protocols have lower excess work. This curvature, which separates into independent contributions from each pattern of symmetry breaking, thus acquires an operational meaning as a measure of the thermodynamic incentive to break a given symmetry. A thermodynamic comparison theorem provides an inexpensive test of local minimality that weighs the extremal curvatures along the symmetric protocol against its excess work. There is no need for such tests under certain convexity conditions on the transition rates and control parameters, which guarantee that a symmetric optimum is a global optimum. These results certify the global optimality of a five-parameter protocol previously observed to match 15-parameter full control of a four-spin Ising system, and explain why partial symmetry breaking is optimal for the same system under local-field control.

Electron–phonon interactions in a pseudo–atomic orbital basis: superconductivity, transport, spectroscopy and thermal emission from one interpolated vertex

Electron–phonon interactions govern superconductivity, electrical resistivity, optical absorption and electronic excitation lifetimes in solids. First–principles calculations require interpolating this coupling onto dense Brillouin–zone grids, traditionally using maximally localized Wannier functions that demand material–specific tuning. Here we show that a fixed basis of pseudo–atomic orbitals–automatically generated from pseudopotentials and implemented in the open–source code PAOFLOW captures the electron–phonon vertex with equivalent accuracy and no manual tuning. Emergent Chern metal in strongly correlated moiré bilayers

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-11-26 20:00 EST

Kaushal Kumar Kesharpu, Evgenii A. Kochetov

We investigate the topological properties of a strongly correlated moiré transition metal dichalcogenide (TMD) bilayer (such as twisted $ \mathrm{MoTe}_2$ or $ \mathrm{WSe}_2$ ) hosting a spin-charge separated state coupled to a layer-polarized helical spin texture. Analyzing an effective low-energy Hamiltonian for mobile spinless holons moving through this non-coplanar spinon background, we reveal pronounced asymmetric band warping. This warping closes the global indirect band gap across most of the parameter space, restricting true topological insulators to fine-tuned critical trajectories. However, a finite local direct gap persists, stabilizing broad regions of a topological Chern metal ($ C = \pm 1$ ). Computing the Chern number and density of states, we demonstrate that this phase naturally yields a sizable, non-quantized anomalous Hall response accompanied by finite longitudinal conduction, providing a purely electronic strong-coupling mechanism for dissipative anomalous Hall states in moiré systems.

Leah Tom, John Nichol, Chris Anderson, Mark F. Gyure

Charge noise from two-level fluctuators (TLFs) limits gate fidelities in semiconductor spin qubits, yet locating individual fluctuators within a device remains challenging. Here, we demonstrate a spatial triangulation method that combines the gate dependence of a TLF’s detuning with the chemical potential shifts it induces in a sensing dot. Applying this method to previously reported measurements from a gated Si/SiGe quantum dot device, we use electrostatic simulations to jointly constrain the fluctuator’s three-dimensional position and the magnitude of its electric dipole moment. Air-sensitive chalcogenide films are routinely protected by a capping layer, which must then be removed, or contacted through, before electronic transport can be measured, destroying the surface the cap was meant to preserve. Terahertz time-domain spectroscopy requires neither, but using it quantitatively demands a transmission model in which cap, film and substrate are treated on an equal footing. We formulate the cap/film/substrate problem exactly using a transfer matrix with a time-gated substrate, and show that the intuitive construction in which each thin layer contributes an independent Fabry–Pérot factor fails for two adjacent thin films. In the thin-film limit we obtain a closed-form inversion together with a criterion for the error incurred by ignoring the cap. In this matched set, the capped films form an ordered series, monotonic in thickness in both conductance magnitude and lineshape, whereas the uncapped films do not. The capping layer therefore presents no obstacle to terahertz characterization, and in this set it coincides with a more reproducible film response.

Monotonic probes of asymmetry and robustness of the quantum Mpemba effect

New Submission | Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech) | 2026-11-26 20:00 EST

Riccardo Travaglino, Michele Mazzoni, Pasquale Calabrese

Entanglement asymmetry has emerged as a powerful probe of symmetry restoration and the quantum Mpemba effect. However, its conventional Rényi generalizations, defined as differences of Rényi entropies, are not monotones in the resource-theoretic sense. Here, we investigate an alternative family of asymmetry quantifiers based on sandwiched Rényi divergences, which satisfy monotonicity under symmetry-preserving quantum channels. Developing a two-parameter replica approach, we show how these quantities can be computed from charged moments and establish a remarkable correspondence with conventional Rényi asymmetries under the inversion of the Rényi index, $ \alpha\leftrightarrow 1/\alpha$ . This relation, exact for pure states, holds at leading order in the relevant scaling limits for homogeneous quantum quenches, including interacting integrable systems, and for Haar-random states. Applying these results to quenches from tilted ferromagnetic states, we demonstrate that the quantum Mpemba effect is remarkably robust, occurring throughout the entire family of sandwiched Rényi asymmetry monotones. We identify dynamical regimes in quenches from spatially inhomogeneous initial states where conventional Rényi asymmetries and genuine monotones exhibit qualitatively different behaviours. We also investigate a solvable toy model illustrating the possible dependence of Mpemba crossings on the Rényi index. Our findings establish a connection between conventional Rényi asymmetries and quantum resource theory, explaining their effectiveness in broad classes of nonequilibrium protocols while highlighting the additional information provided by genuine asymmetry monotones.

The effect of moiré modulations on two-dimensional materials flattens their electronic energy bands, which enhances the effects of interactions and has been seen to give rise to several emergent electronic phases. We demonstrate this behavior with a moiré modulation extends to a quasiperiodic hopping model of interacting bosons on the honeycomb lattice. Without interactions, the velocity vanishes at a magic-angle transition, where a sharp onset of the zero-energy density of states and multifractal low-energy states appear. We discuss how our results can be explored in ultracold atom experiments using momentum space lattices or tweezer arrays.

Magnons in solids with correlated atomic and nanoscale disorder

New Submission | Disordered Systems and Neural Networks (cond-mat.dis-nn) | 2026-11-26 20:00 EST

Alex Moorhouse, Joseph Barker, Michele Simoncelli

Atomic defects and disorder affect both the microscopic magnon excitations and the macroscopic physical properties of real-world magnetic materials. The rationalization of this relation has hitherto remained elusive, because of the intertwined challenges of simulating with quantum accuracy atomic- and nanoscale inhomogeneities and correlations, and their effect on the magnon spectral function determining observables such as spin stiffness. Here, we introduce a theoretical and computational framework to address these challenges. First, we develop the Coupled Optimization of SpIN Environment (COSINE) algorithm for efficiently relaxing the atomic structure and magnetic configuration of a solid with defects or disorder to a local energy minimum. Then, we discuss how to efficiently obtain exchange interactions accounting for local compositional, structural, and magnetic-moment inhomogeneities. Finally, we develop a paraunitary unfolding approach for Bogoliubov bosons, which allows us to predict the magnon spectral function in materials with any magnetic order, including systems with canting and full, partial, or no magnetic-moment compensation. We showcase our approach in a representative defective ferrimagnet for generality, and then combine it with state-of-the-art spin-informed machine-learning potentials to quantitatively predict how nonuniformities in the spatial distribution of vacancy defects control blurring of effective magnon bandstructures.

A microscopic continuum model for moiré transition metal dichalcogenides

Julian Ingham, Harshitra Mahalingam, Raquel Queiroz

Twisted transition metal dichalcogenides (tTMDs) have emerged as a fascinating platform for many-body phases — displaying fractional Chern insulators and superconductivity as a function of doping. Here, we give a microscopic derivation of a continuum model which accurately describes their bandstructure across a range of twist angles with angle-independent parameters. This angle-independent description rests on retaining both the monolayer valence and conduction bands, which introduce tunnelling paths through AB-stacked regions; upon downfolding to the valence band, these paths supply a substantial twist-tunable contribution to the interlayer coupling. We show that the model is a perturbation of a chiral model with a magic angle, explaining the near-ideal quantum geometry of the moiré bands. The model further produces a strongly different orbital magnetic moment to the single-orbital theory — predicting an anomalous Landau level slope — and a redistribution of charge between the layers, modifying the moiré ferroelectric potential.

Frequency-dispersive magnetic anisotropy dissolves the Snoek trade-off

Physical Sciences - Article | Ferromagnetism | 2026-11-26 19:00 EST

Jianguo Guan, Yifan Xu, Ning Xu, Zhihong Chen, Yunhao Luo, Renjunyi Zhao, Yuchen Hu, Chenxi Liao, Shuiqing Lu, Miaomiao Bi, Yajiao Ke, Yanli Li, Wei Li

High magnetic permeability enables compact and efficient electromagnetic devices, but the Snoek trade-off associates high permeability with low resonance frequency. Interphase exchange coupling is conventionally assumed to be constant and frequency-independent. Here we show that exchange coupling among magnetic regions with distinct local anisotropies can make the effective anisotropy field frequency-dependent. As a proof of concept, Mn addition to FeSiAl produces two coherent D03 phases with perpendicular easy axes. At low frequency their moments rotate together and partially compensate the local anisotropy; at high frequency their rotations become less collective and intrinsic anisotropies dominate the precession. The element-substituted alloys significantly improve the initial permeability while showing a retained or increased apparent resonance frequency compared with the FeSiAl. Ferromagnetic-resonance measurements and joint fits of the complex permeability support a dispersive effective anisotropy rather than a frequency-independent constant. A composite absorber of FeMnSiAl dispersed in silicone resin shows microwave absorption exceeding 90% over 0.96-1.98 GHz at a thickness of 2.7 mm, only 8.2% of the band-limited Rozanov-equivalent thickness of a nonmagnetic absorber. This design of coherence among moment precessions establishes a degree of freedom for extending strong magnetic response to higher frequencies without the usual permeability-resonance penalty.

Nanostructure-engineered telecom quantum platform with room-temperature optical quantum coherence

Article | Quantum information | 2026-11-26 19:00 EST

Achieving μs-scale quantum coherence at room temperature in the telecom C band remains a central challenge for solid state quantum platforms, previously demonstrated only under vacuum cryogenic conditions. Here we introduce a CMOS-compatible nanoengineering approach that deterministically controls the number of erbium ions within a nanoscale volume and their electromagnetic environment using sub 5 nm silicon based hollow nanopillars. Individually addressable single-ion devices exhibit a record room temperature optical coherence time of T2*= 32 μs, limited by laser coherence, while photon-echo measurements yield coherence exceeding 60 μs at few-ion sites. The results establish nanostructure-engineered control of the local ion environments as a pathway toward scalable, manufacturable, telecom-compatible quantum networks, distributed sensing, and cryogenic-free quantum photonic integrated circuits.
