Opportunistically Detecting Signs of Hypertension on a Consumer Smartwatch

Original Paper | Hypertension | 2026-11-04 19:00 EST

Paolo Di Achille, Lawrence Cai, Jiang Wu, Mingwu Gao, Bhavna Daryani, Jonathan Wang, Utkarsh Khanna, Ho Ko, Anupam Pathak, Mark Malhotra, Shwetak Patel, Jacqueline B. Shreibati, Stephen P. Juraschek, Pramod Rudrapatna, Matthew Thompson, Ming-Zher Poh

Hypertension is a silent killer, with over half of affected adults unaware of their condition1,2. This lack of awareness is a major concern, as early intervention is critical for preventing major adverse cardiovascular events3,4. Here we show that applying artificial intelligence (AI) to multimodal waveforms (photoplethysmography and accelerometry) recorded using a widely available consumer smartwatch (AI-PPG-ACC-HTN), without any cuff calibration, can detect hypertension with accuracy levels comparable to traditional cuffed blood pressure (BP) devices in the existing clinical framework. We validated AI-PPG-ACC-HTN in a prospective, multicenter study of 196 diverse participants free from known cardiovascular disease and antihypertensive medication against gold-standard 24-hour ambulatory BP monitoring. Over seven days of real-world monitoring, AI-PPG-ACC-HTN detected hypertension with a sensitivity of 65.8% (95% CI, 54.0%-76.3%), specificity of 90.0% (95% CI, 83.2%-94.7%), and positive predictive value (PPV) of 80.6% (95% CI, 68.6%-89.6%). In comparison, initial office BP screening achieved a sensitivity of 55.3% (95% CI, 43.4%-66.7%), specificity of 90.0% (95% CI, 83.2%-94.7%) and PPV of 77.8% (95% CI, 64.4%-88.0%). These results highlight an opportunity for consumer smartwatches to facilitate population-level opportunistic hypertension screening by prompting at-risk individuals toward clinical assessment and confirmatory diagnosis using established cuff-based methodologies, offering an accessible tool to address this major public health challenge.

Hydrogen Derivatives Enable Cost-Effective Carbon Neutrality in China

Original Paper | Energy supply and demand | 2026-11-04 19:00 EST

Xinyu Chen, Xinyang Guo, Xi Yang, Jiashuo Li, Michael McElroy

The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change recommended 1.5°C carbon reduction target, requiring powerful enforcement from major economies. China, the largest CO2 emitter, is facing severe difficulties for both variable renewable accommodation and decarbonization of hard-to-abate sectors. Hydrogen, as a critical green energy carrier bridging renewable to end-users, faces significant obstacles in real-world deployment such as costly transportation, storage and fuel-cell adoption. Affordable and coordinated hydrogen deployment plan to accelerate the carbon-neutral transition remains ambiguous. Here, we present cost-effective carbon-neutral transition pathways for entire energy economy of China emphasizing the significant role of hydrogen and derivatives as green energy carriers, considering high-resolution renewable deployments, hourly power system operations in 29 provincial regions, over 46,000 rail/road/marine/pipeline transfer routing of hydrogen derivatives, process-level fuel adoption strategies in all end-use sectors. Hydrogen derivative based transition pathway offers a vital direction for affordable and secure decarbonization for China and beyond under volatile international energy markets.

Mechanical adaptation drives out-of-equilibrium network reorganization and self-strengthening in adapt-ring networks

Original Paper | Gels and hydrogels | 2026-11-04 19:00 EST

Dan Zheng, Zhaohan Yu, Yufei Chen, Cameron D. Smith, Mingliang Bai, Esther H. R. Tsai, Jiabin Liu, Wenlin Zhang, Shaoting Lin, Chenfeng Ke

Synthetic polymer networks typically lack the ability to autonomously remodel and strengthen under mechanical stress, a capability commonly observed in natural materials. Here we introduce adapt-ring networks (ARNs) that establish a general design principle for adaptive polymer materials. ARNs couple a covalent polyethylene glycol network with reconfigurable α-cyclodextrin crystalline domains whose strain-directed fragmentation, translocation and alignment form kinetically trapped architectures encoding local mechanical history. Mechanical training enhances Young’s modulus, tensile strength, fracture energy and fatigue threshold by up to an order of magnitude, with full reversibility through solvent-induced crystalline-domain reformation. Spatially resolved adaptation and 3D printability enable gradient materials and customized architectures. Incorporating polyethylene glycol side chains to form dangling-chain ARNs enables strain-controlled out-of-equilibrium growth through threading and crystallization of external α-cyclodextrins. These hydrogels autonomously generate spatially self-organized, strain-dependent reinforcement, including non-local, environment-fed stiffening conceptually analogous to biological reinforcement. These features establish a general approach to bio-inspired, non-equilibrium soft materials that adapt, remember, regrow and readapt.

Dendritic cells release extracellular vesicles (DEVs) that amplify antigen presentation while incorporating patient-derived, rapidly evolving antigens. Atomically resolved exciton emission from single defects in MoS2

Original Paper | Two-dimensional materials | 2026-11-04 19:00 EST

Lysander Huberich, Eve Ammerman, Yu Gu, Yining Ren, Sotirios Papadopoulos, Chengye Dong, Joshua A. Robinson, Kenji Watanabe, Takashi Taniguchi, Oliver Gröning, Lukas Novotny, Tingxin Li, Shiyong Wang, Bruno Schuler

Excitons, bound electron-hole pairs, govern the optical response of semiconductors and underpin technologies ranging from photovoltaics to quantum light sources. In nanoscale systems, the spatial extent of excitons approaches atomic dimensions, yet direct imaging of excitonic processes in real space has remained elusive. Two-dimensional semiconductors such as transition metal dichalcogenides provide a platform where reduced screening, high light extraction and atomically defined defect sites enable the exploration of exciton physics at atomic length scales. However, the microscopic origin of defect-related emission and its variability across samples have remained debated. Here we show that scanning tunnelling microscopy-induced luminescence reveals the atomic and optical fingerprints of individual defects in monolayer MoS2. Decoupling the material from its substrate using bilayer hexagonal boron nitride enables the detection of sharp excitonic emission and spatially localized defect emission, which we attribute to free-to-bound recombination (e,A0), and map its distribution with subnanometre-resolved fluorescence imaging. We identify negatively charged carbon-hydrogen complexes as the defect species responsible for a ubiquitous defect emission band in MoS2. These results provide atomically resolved images of exciton emission in a solid-state material, linking atomic defect structure to its optical fingerprint and establishing a microscopic basis for deterministic quantum emitter engineering in two-dimensional materials.

Jiasen Niu, Maialen Ortego Larrazabal, Thomas Gozlinski, Yudai Sato, Koen M. Bastiaans, Tjerk Benschop, Jian-Feng Ge, Yaroslav M. Blanter, Genda Gu, Ingmar Swart, Milan P. Allan

The pseudogap stands out in the phase diagram of the cuprate high-temperature superconductors because its origin and relationship to superconductivity remain elusive. The origin of the pseudogap has been debated, with competing hypotheses attributing it to preformed electron pairs or local order, such as charge density waves. We present unambiguous evidence supporting the pairing scenario, using local shot-noise spectroscopy measurements in Bi2Sr2CaCu2O8+δ. Our data demonstrate that the pseudogap energy coincides with the onset of electron pairing and is spatially heterogeneous with values reaching up to 70 milli-electron volts. Our results exclude a pure local order origin of the pseudogap, link the pseudogap to Cooper pair formation, and suggest that the limiting factor for higher critical temperature in cuprates is phase coherence.

Research Article | Noise pollution | 2026-11-05 03:00 EST

Hansjoerg P. Kunc, Rouven Schmidt

Anthropogenic noise is a rapidly expanding global pollutant, but its effects across biological levels from individuals to ecosystems remain unquantified. We conducted phylogenetically controlled meta-analyses of 214 experimental studies on 177 aquatic and terrestrial species with ranges that span six continents. Noise altered at least one physiological, developmental, or behavioral trait in 80% of species studied, with declines in fitness and abundance and disruptions of predator-prey interactions. Effects occurred in every habitat, although response mechanisms varied for some traits and species, creating winners and losers. Only multispecies analyses can reveal such species- and ecosystem-level impacts of anthropogenic stressors. Our results establish noise as a novel selection pressure reshaping ecosystem dynamics and as a pervasive stressor requiring soundscape protection alongside habitat conservation.

Shapiro spikes in driven quantum fluids

New Submission | Quantum Gases (cond-mat.quant-gas) | 2026-11-05 20:00 EST

Vijay Pal Singh, Ludwig Mathey, Luigi Amico

Periodic driving of a Josephson junction produces resonant transport signatures known as Shapiro spikes, conventionally described by effective circuit models. Here, we demonstrate Shapiro spikes in a neutral quantum fluid using simulations of a potential-biased atomic Josephson junction. The dc-current resonances follow the expected Bessel-function scaling, including the suppression and revival of individual resonances with increasing modulation strength. We uncover their microscopic mechanism by showing that the phase-winding frequency develops finite plateaus at integer multiples of the drive frequency. These phase-locked states are sustained by quantized-vortex nucleation and the associated $ 2\pi$ phase slips. Remarkably, within a locking plateau, the dc current and density transfer can reverse direction while the phase-winding frequency remains locked to the drive, giving rise to negative-mobility transport. Our results establish a microscopic link between vortex-mediated phase slips, Shapiro resonances, and anomalous transport in a neutral quantum fluid, revealing how quantized vortices convert resonant transport into phase-locked dynamics with negative mobility.

Quasicrystals are Functionally Graded Materials

New Submission | Materials Science (cond-mat.mtrl-sci) | 2026-11-05 20:00 EST

Michael J. Landry, Dominic Bollettieri, Akhil Sadam, Denisse Córdova Carrizales, Mingda Li

Quasicrystals exhibit long-range order without translational periodicity, posing challenges to compute their electronic properties. Here we show that a quasicrystal can be represented exactly as a periodic approximant with a spatially graded phason, establishing a connection to functionally graded materials. Building on this connection, we develop a controlled quantum theory of quasicrystals. We derive a state-dependent control parameter governed by the quantum metric of local Bloch states, determining when finite approximations are reliable. We further construct a Hamiltonian that correctly predicts Bragg gap positions, quasimomentum equivalence, and quasiperiodic eigenstates to all orders in phason strain within a finite Fourier window. We verify the theory numerically and propose a diffraction-based experimental test of the gap shift induced by phason strain. Our framework opens a route to predicting the electronic structure and functional properties of quasicrystals using methods developed for functionally graded materials.
