Random matrix discrepancy – CLuP algorithm and parametric RDT approach

New Submission | Disordered Systems and Neural Networks (cond-mat.dis-nn) | 2026-11-17 20:00 EST

Mihailo Stojnic

We study information-theoretic and algorithmic aspects of random matrix discrepancy in proportional regime. The problem amounts to finding $ \kappa=\min_{\x\in\mB^n}|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{A}_i\x_i|_2$ , where $ \bar{A}i\in\mR^{d\times d}$ is a collection of $ n$ iid GOE matrices, $ \mB^n$ are vertices of the unit cube in $ \mR^n$ , and $ \alpha=\lim{n\rightarrow \infty}\frac{n}{d^2}$ is a proportionality constant.
Strong progress is made in several directions: (i) We develop a Random duality theory (RDT) framework and utilize its a lifted variant to establish lower bounds on $ \kappa$ , which precisely match the bounds obtained via the first moment in [81]. (ii) To address analytical challenges, we propose a negative Hopfield model as a proxy for implementation of a fully lifted RDT. On the 2nd lifting level, this model fits remarkably well between the information-theoretic lower bounds from [81] and the algorithmic upper bounds from [38]. (iii) On the 3rd and 4th lifting levels, parametric RDT concepts predict a sizeable computational gap for lower $ \kappa$ values in the proxy model. Extending proxy information-theoretic fitting to algorithmic suggests a similar computational gap for matrix discrepancy in low $ \kappa$ regime, aligning with [73]. (iv) We design a Controlled Loosening Up (CLuP) algorithm for matrix discrepancy. Its performance strongly matches our analytical results – closely approaching information-theoretic predictions in lower $ \alpha$ ranges (indicating a small or nonexistent computational gap) and following the proxy model’s algorithmic predictions in higher $ \alpha$ ranges (indicating a sizeable computational gap).
Both the information-theoretic and algorithmic predictions match the corresponding phenomenologies of the symmetric binary perceptron (SBP) vector analogue in the entire $ \alpha\in(0,\infty)$ range [1,2,14,31,51,92,111].

MagBerry: A Julia package for modeling magnon and phonon topology and transport

New Submission | Other Condensed Matter (cond-mat.other) | 2026-11-17 20:00 EST

Xiaonong Shen, Wei Ren

We present MagBerry, a Julia package for studying the topology and transport of magnons, phonons, and their hybrid excitations. It combines magnetic models and linear spin-wave Hamiltonians from this http URL with native modules for harmonic phonons and magnon–phonon coupling. These excitations are treated within a common bosonic Bogoliubov–de Gennes formulation. The package calculates bulk and boundary spectra, Berry curvature, and Chern numbers, and resolves the spin and phonon angular momentum carried by each mode. Transport calculations include thermal Hall, spin and orbital Nernst, and phonon angular momentum Hall responses. Liquid-liquid criticality in a fluid whose two structures favor mixing

New Submission | Soft Condensed Matter (cond-mat.soft) | 2026-11-17 20:00 EST

Marcio Pazetti

The generic two-state description of liquid polyamorphism requires a non-ideality opposing mixing for liquid-liquid separation. This condition assumes that the free energy of the underlying fluid is independent of the structural order, an assumption that fails when the local structures differ in size or attraction. We derive a closed-form stability condition after density relaxation for a van der Waals closure based on the second virial coefficient of a pair potential whose internal state determines both the contact distance and the well depth. The condition recovers the familiar result for structures identical in size and attraction but can change sign within the liquid when they differ. In ten of the thirty-four parameter sets examined, a critical state preferred by the liquid requires a non-ideality favoring mixing. For one such fluid, we construct the coexistence curve, which terminates at the predicted critical point at positive pressure. Attraction and excluded volume provide distinct contributions to the density-order coupling; only the former survives on a fixed lattice with single occupancy. Removing the excluded-volume contribution restores the familiar condition in 58 of the 82 such critical states found. Thus, within this closure, liquid-liquid separation can occur with a non-ideality favoring mixing.

Magnon binding in a quantum Hall ferromagnet with a periodic electric field

New Submission | Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics (cond-mat.mes-hall) | 2026-11-17 20:00 EST

Manas Kaushik Das

A spin wave in a filling-one quantum Hall ferromagnet is neutral but carries an electric dipole perpendicular to its momentum. A spatially periodic electrostatic potential therefore creates parallel channels for spin waves. We use this established mechanism to calculate how confinement changes the exchange binding of magnons travelling in the same direction. Narrower channels increase the overlap of their transverse wavefunctions and strengthen the effective attraction. In an isolated harmonic channel, the energy required to remove one magnon from a cluster of $ N$ is $ \pi(k_\ast\ell)^4N(N-1)/4$ times the transverse level spacing to leading order, where $ k_\ast$ is the mean wave number along the channel and $ \ell$ is the magnetic length. We predict the frequency and momentum dependence of the spectral line for adding a second magnon to one already present, both in an isolated channel and in a coupled array. Tunnelling in a wide array can make the binding energy exponentially small, while a strip narrower than the transverse pair size recovers one-dimensional binding at sufficiently weak attraction. We also find that a moving potential can carry a single wave packet with less spreading than free propagation when its initial group velocity matches the potential’s speed.

The intrinsic thermal conductivity ($ \kappa$ ) of graphene remains highly controversial because of physical and metrological complexities. Although the thermal bridge method enables reliable measurements, sample fabrication remains challenging. Quantum Cooling by Classical Shadows

New Submission | Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el) | 2026-11-17 20:00 EST

Xuepeng Wang, Debanjan Chowdhury

Cooling to ground states of many-body Hamiltonians by imaginary-time evolution is central to quantum simulation, but its non-unitary aspect prevents direct implementation on a quantum computer. Existing routes restore it by importing structure from outside the intrinsic quantum dynamics, e.g. by parameter estimation on many copies or recursive compilation. Here, we show that a single randomized snapshot of the quantum many-body system is sufficient to steer the state along the cooling direction on average. Our algorithm, shadow chain, uses randomized measurements to collapse the state to its classical record, which fixes the next unitary update. Snapshots across steps form a trajectory that is a quantum-executable sample of an auxiliary-field path integral; fluctuations among trajectories are exponentially suppressed in system size. With the randomness supplied intrinsically by the Born measurement rule, the trajectories sample themselves rather than going through external accept-reject steps. The resulting algorithm is ansatz-free and exact upon extrapolation to the continuous limit, with the exponential cost confined only to system size rather than temperature.
